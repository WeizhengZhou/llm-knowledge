# USAMTS (USA Mathematical Talent Search)

## Quick Facts

| Field | Value |
|-------|-------|
| Type | Free, proof-based math competition |
| Eligibility | US middle and high school students |
| Rounds per year | 3 |

| Scoring | 0-5 per problem, with personalized written feedback |

## Overview

According to its official website, USAMTS (USA Mathematical Talent Search) is a free, proof-based math competition for US middle and high school students, administered by the Art of Problem Solving Foundation and supported by the National Security Agency.

USAMTS has 3 rounds per year, each with 5 problems (1 puzzle + 4 proof-based problems) and over 1 month to solve. Any resources are allowed except help from another person. Each problem is graded 0-5 with personalized written feedback.

The extended timeline and feedback mechanism make USAMTS fundamentally different from timed competitions like [[amc-competitions|AMC 10/12]]. It functions as both a competition and a training tool for mathematical writing.

## Format Comparison with Timed Competitions

| Feature | USAMTS | AMC/AIME |
|---------|--------|----------|
| Time limit | 1 month per round | 75 min / 3 hours |
| Resources allowed | Yes (books, internet) | No |
| Proof required | Yes (mandatory) | No (AIME: fill-in number) |
| Feedback provided | Yes (personalized) | Score only |
| Cost | Free | $0-$30 depending on school |

## AIME Qualification

Strong USAMTS performance is one pathway to qualify for the AIME as an alternative to AMC 10/12. According to AwesomeMath, a score of approximately 68/75 or higher is needed.

This makes USAMTS a viable alternative route into the [[competition-math-pipeline|AMC-to-IMO pipeline]] for students who underperform on timed tests but have strong mathematical reasoning.

## Who Should Use USAMTS

USAMTS is particularly valuable for:

1. **Students who struggle with timed tests.** Test anxiety, slow writing speed, or deliberate thinking styles are not penalized. The month-long window rewards depth over speed.
2. **Students transitioning to proof-based math.** The requirement for fully written proofs with grader feedback is the best systematic proof-writing training available at the high school level outside of dedicated courses.
3. **Self-directed learners.** Students can register individually at any time during the year, solve 1-2 problems per week, and use grader feedback to improve iteratively.
4. **Students targeting olympiad math.** Problem difficulty spans AIME-level to entry-level USAMO, making USAMTS a bridge from computation to proof.

## Problem Types

USAMTS problems typically involve number theory, combinatorics, geometry, algebra, and logic/pattern puzzles. Problems are original and creative, often requiring genuine insight rather than formula application (source: usamts.org, April 2026).

## Prizes and Recognition

Top 45% of students receive prizes (math books and T-shirts). Top performers may receive Wolfram software subscriptions (source: usamts.org, April 2026).

## Common Mistakes

- **Treating USAMTS as a low-stakes afterthought.** The personalized written feedback on proofs is arguably more valuable for mathematical development than the competition score itself. Students who submit minimal work miss the primary training benefit.
- **Using USAMTS only for AIME qualification.** The 68/75 threshold is high. Most students will benefit more from the proof-writing practice than from the AIME pathway. Treat qualification as a bonus, not the goal.
- **Waiting until high school to start.** USAMTS is open to middle school students. Starting early builds proof-writing habits before USAMO-level problems demand them. Multiple wiki articles recommend starting USAMTS in middle school for this reason.
