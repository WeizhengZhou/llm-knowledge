## Overview

According to PROMYS, the 2026 PROMYS program costs up to $7,000 for 6 weeks at Boston University (June 28 - August 8, 2026). It is free for domestic students from families earning under $80,000/year, with need-based financial aid available at all income levels. The 2025 cost was $6,100.

PROMYS was founded by former Ross counselors and shares the same pedagogical DNA: number theory-focused, problem-set-driven, with emphasis on independent discovery. Students work through challenging problem sets with minimal lecturing.

## Financial Aid

PROMYS offers the strongest financial aid among the major summer programs alongside [[canada-usa-mathcamp|Mathcamp]]. It is free for domestic students from families earning under $80,000/year, with need-based financial aid available at all income levels.

## How PROMYS Differs from Ross

The educational experience at PROMYS is closely parallel to [[ross-program|Ross]]: both are number theory-focused, problem-set-driven programs emphasizing independent discovery. A parent reading both entity articles will find nearly identical pedagogical descriptions. The practical differences are:

- **Location:** PROMYS is at Boston University; Ross is at Otterbein University (OH) and Rose-Hulman Institute (IN). The BU campus means PROMYS students are embedded in a research university environment with potential for interaction with BU faculty and graduate students.
- **Cost and financial aid:** PROMYS 2026 costs up to $7,000 (vs. Ross $7,500). PROMYS is free for domestic families earning under $80,000/year, with need-based aid available at all income levels. Ross financial aid exists but the income threshold is less publicly defined.
- **Counselor return path:** PROMYS has a well-established counselor application for returning students — strong first-year students are often invited to return as junior counselors in subsequent years. This is a meaningful opportunity that families unfamiliar with residential math programs may not know to pursue.
- **Philosophy:** Both use the "think deeply" approach. Students interested in a broader topic selection should consider [[canada-usa-mathcamp|Mathcamp]] instead.

**Typical student profile:** A PROMYS applicant is typically a high school student (ages 15-18) with AIME qualification or equivalent mathematical maturity, who enjoys spending extended time on a single mathematical thread. Students who want topic variety or who have already attended Ross often choose Mathcamp.

## Common Mistakes

- **Missing the application window.** PROMYS applications open in early February and close in mid-April. Families who discover PROMYS in May have missed the cycle.
- **Not requesting need-based aid because family income is over $80,000.** PROMYS offers financial aid at all income levels, not just below the free-attendance threshold. Families earning $100,000-$150,000 can receive partial aid but commonly do not apply.
- **Not knowing about the counselor return path.** Strong first-year students are often invited to return as junior counselors in subsequent summers. This is a significant mathematical and leadership opportunity that families should plan for when budgeting and scheduling.
