## Overview

MathPath is a 4-week residential summer mathematics program for students ages 11-14. It serves as an earlier entry point into selective math programs than [[ross-program|Ross]], [[promys|PROMYS]], or [[canada-usa-mathcamp|Mathcamp]], which primarily serve ages 13-18 (Ross: typical ages 15-18).

AdmissionsAngle lists MathPath (middle school program) 2025 cost at $6,300 (financial aid available), with dates June 29 - July 27, 2025, for ages 11-14, and an approximate acceptance rate of 30%.

## Who Should Apply

MathPath fills a specific gap in the summer program landscape:

1. **Students ages 11-13 with demonstrated mathematical maturity.** Ross and PROMYS typically accept students ages 15-18. Mathcamp starts at 13 but is most competitive for older students. MathPath is the primary selective residential option for the 11-14 age band.
2. **Students preparing for more selective programs later.** MathPath experience can build the mathematical maturity and residential independence needed to succeed at Ross, PROMYS, or Mathcamp in subsequent years.
3. **Students who have outgrown local enrichment.** For a child performing well in [[mathcounts|MATHCOUNTS]] or [[amc-competitions|AMC 8]] but not yet ready for olympiad-level programs, MathPath provides a peer community of similarly motivated students.

## Admission

MathPath requires a qualifying test for admission. The ~30% acceptance rate (per AdmissionsAngle, 2025) makes it selective but significantly less competitive than Ross (~15%) or RSI (~2.5%).

## Common Mistakes

- **Applying to Ross/PROMYS when the student is 11-12.** These programs rarely accept students under 14. MathPath exists specifically for this age group. Applying too early to more selective programs wastes effort and can be discouraging.
- **Assuming MathPath is a competition-prep camp.** Like Ross and PROMYS, MathPath emphasizes mathematical exploration and enrichment, not AMC/AIME drilling. Parents expecting score improvements may be disappointed; the value is in mathematical maturity and peer exposure.
- **Skipping MathPath because the acceptance rate seems "easy."** A ~30% acceptance rate still means most applicants are rejected. The qualifying test requires genuine mathematical ability. Do not treat the application as a formality.
