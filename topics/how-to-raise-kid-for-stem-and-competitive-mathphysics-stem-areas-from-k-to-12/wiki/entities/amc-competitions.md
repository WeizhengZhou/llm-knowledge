## Overview

The AMC series comprises three competitions: AMC 8 (grade 8 and below, maximum age under 15.5), AMC 10 (grade 10 and below, maximum age under 17.5), and AMC 12 (grade 12 and below, maximum age under 19.5).

The AMC is the entry point to the [[competition-math-pipeline|full US math competition pipeline]]. According to the MAA, the AMC program began in 1950 as the American High School Mathematics Examination (AHSME).

## Format Details

### AMC 8

AMC 8 is a 25-question, 40-minute multiple-choice competition. Covers pre-algebra, introductory geometry, introductory number theory, and basic counting.

For the 2025-2026 cycle, the AMC 8 competition window was January 22-30, 2026.

### AMC 10 and AMC 12

AMC 10 and AMC 12 are each 25-question, 75-minute multiple-choice tests.

AMC 12 covers the full high school mathematics curriculum including trigonometry, advanced algebra, and advanced geometry; calculus is excluded.

**Scoring:** 6 points per correct, 1.5 points for blank, 0 for incorrect. Maximum: 150.

For the 2025-2026 cycle, AMC 10 A and AMC 12 A were held November 5, 2025; AMC 10 B and AMC 12 B were held November 13, 2025.

## AIME Qualification

AIME qualification requires finishing in the top 2.5% of AMC 10 participants or the top 5% of AMC 12 participants; approximately 6,000-7,000 students qualify annually.

For 2025-2026 onward, the former AIME qualification score caps of 120 (AMC 10) and 100 (AMC 12) were removed; cutoffs can theoretically reach 150.

As of August 2025, international students no longer affect AIME cutoffs.

## USAMO/USAJMO Qualification

For 2025-2026 onward, the USAMO/USAJMO qualifying index formula is (AIME score x 20) + AMC score. The previous formula (through 2024-2025) was (AIME score x 10) + AMC score.

For historical cutoffs, see [[competition-math-pipeline]].

## Related Competitions

### ARML (American Regions Mathematics League)

According to Wikipedia, ARML (American Regions Mathematics League) is an annual national high school math team competition held simultaneously at 5 US sites on the first Saturday after Memorial Day.

According to Wikipedia, ARML teams consist of 15 members representing a geographic region (typically a state or major city). Students must join a regional or state math team via their qualifying process.

According to Wikipedia, ARML competition rounds include: Team Round (20 min, 10 problems), Power Question (1-hour proof exploration), Individual Round (5 sets of 2 problems, 10 min per set), Relay Round (groups of 3, 6 min per relay), and Super Relay (not scored toward official total).

According to Wikipedia, ARML 2025-2026 competition sites are University of Iowa, Penn State University, University of Nevada Reno, Western Carolina University, and Saint Anselm College.

### USAMTS (USA Mathematical Talent Search)

According to its official website, USAMTS (USA Mathematical Talent Search) is a free, proof-based math competition for US middle and high school students, administered by the Art of Problem Solving Foundation and supported by the National Security Agency.

USAMTS has 3 rounds per year, each with 5 problems (1 puzzle + 4 proof-based problems) and over 1 month to solve. Any resources are allowed except help from another person. Each problem is graded 0-5 with personalized written feedback.

Strong USAMTS performance is one pathway to qualify for the AIME as an alternative to AMC 10/12. According to AwesomeMath, a score of approximately 68/75 or higher is needed.

USAMTS is particularly valuable for students who dislike timed competition pressure but excel at careful mathematical reasoning.

### Math Kangaroo

AwesomeMath and RSM both list Math Kangaroo as an annual competition for grades 1-12, held on March 19, 2026, with 24 problems for grades 1-5 and 30 questions for grades 5+, all in 75 minutes.

Math Kangaroo serves as a good first competition experience for elementary students.

## What Participants Observe

> **Epistemic note:** The following reflects patterns across multiple parent accounts
> (Davidson Gifted Forum, AoPS Forums, 2024-2026). Individual experiences vary. Official sources do not
> confirm or deny. Treat as directional signal, not verified fact.

Some parents on gifted education forums describe the AIME-to-USAMO step as the hardest in the AMC pipeline, primarily because it requires proof writing that most students have not practiced. Forum participants frequently cite peer community (math circles, PROMYS, Mathcamp) as important for growth at this level.

Some parents on gifted education forums report that the transition from AMC 8 to AMC 10 is frequently described as a significant difficulty jump. Students who score 20+ on AMC 8 may initially struggle to score above 80 on AMC 10, which is normal and reflects the broader topic coverage and higher difficulty, not a lack of talent.

## Common Mistakes

- **Taking AMC 12 as a 10th grader aiming for USAJMO.** USAJMO qualification uses AMC 10 scores, not AMC 12. Students in grades 9-10 targeting USAJMO should take AMC 10. Students in grades 11-12 targeting USAMO should take AMC 12. A student who qualifies for both USAMO and USAJMO is invited to USAMO.
- **Not knowing that AMC competitions are school-administered.** AMC tests must be proctored through a registered school or testing center. Students whose school does not offer AMC must find an alternative site. Many parents discover this problem in November when AMC 10/12 registration is closing. Check school participation in September.
- **Assuming AIME registration is automatic after qualifying.** After earning an AIME-qualifying score on AMC 10 or 12, the student must separately confirm AIME registration. Students (and parents) who do not realize this may miss the AIME.
