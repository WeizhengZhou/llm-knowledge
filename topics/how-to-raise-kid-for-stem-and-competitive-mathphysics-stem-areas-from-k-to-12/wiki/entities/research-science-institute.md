## Overview

According to the Center for Excellence in Education, RSI (Research Science Institute) is hosted at MIT and is a free 6-week program (tuition, room, and board all covered). RSI receives over 3,100 applications per year and accepts approximately 100 students (roughly 60-70 domestic), for an acceptance rate of approximately 2.5-3%. The application deadline is in December.

RSI is unique among top summer programs: it is completely free, extremely selective, and focused on mentored research rather than coursework. Students are paired with research mentors at MIT and Harvard for a 5-week research project, producing an original paper.

## Application

According to program materials, RSI eligibility is for rising high school seniors. Application requirements include an online application with essays, high school transcript, PSAT/SAT/ACT/AP scores, and two teacher recommendation letters.

## Why RSI Matters for College Admissions

RSI is widely considered the most prestigious pre-college summer program in the US. According to Rise Global Education, STS alumni have a notably high college admissions success rate at elite schools, and top colleges regularly announce STS Scholars and Finalists in admissions materials. RSI alumni frequently submit their RSI research papers to [[science-competitions|Regeneron STS]] as well.

## Common Mistakes

- **Applying without a research narrative.** RSI pairs accepted students with mentors, but the application essays ask about research interests. Students who apply with only competition math achievements and no articulated research direction are at a disadvantage. Strong RSI applications typically describe a specific research question the student wants to investigate.
- **Missing the December application deadline.** RSI's deadline is in December, earlier than most summer programs (January-April). Families who discover RSI in January have missed the cycle.
- **Not planning for RSI until 12th grade.** RSI is for rising seniors only, meaning students have exactly one chance to apply. A student who discovers RSI in 11th grade has one shot; the earlier the student develops research interests and credentials, the stronger the application will be.
