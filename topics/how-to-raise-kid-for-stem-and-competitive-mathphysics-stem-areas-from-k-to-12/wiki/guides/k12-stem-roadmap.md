# K-12 Competitive STEM Roadmap

## Overview

This guide provides a grade-by-grade framework for developing competitive STEM skills from kindergarten through 12th grade. It covers both math and physics pathways, with recommended curricula, competitions, and programs at each stage.

**Important:** This is a reference framework, not a prescription. Every child develops differently. The right pace is the one that maintains enthusiasm. See [[is-this-right-for-my-child]] before committing to this pathway.

## Prerequisites

- A child who shows genuine interest in mathematical or scientific thinking (not just good grades).
- A parent willing to facilitate access to resources without over-directing.
- Familiarity with the distinction between [[competition-math-vs-school-math|school math and competition math]].

## Grade Band: K-2 (Ages 5-8) — Exploration

**Goal:** Build number sense, arithmetic fluency, and love of puzzles. No formal competition prep.

One K-12 roadmap suggests that for grades K-2, focus should be on number sense, arithmetic fluency, and logic puzzles, with Beast Academy Online (Level 2) accessible for gifted K-2 students and Math Kangaroo as a first competition exposure.

### Recommended Activities

| Resource | Type | Notes |
|----------|------|-------|
| [[aops-art-of-problem-solving|Beast Academy]] Level 2 | Curriculum | Comic-style; gifted K-2 students can start here |
| Math Kangaroo (Grades 1-2) | Competition | Low-stakes; 24 problems in 75 minutes |
| Logic puzzles, tangrams, pattern blocks | Play | Develops spatial reasoning |
| Mental math games | Play | Build arithmetic fluency naturally |

### Parent Role

- Provide access to materials; let the child lead.
- Do NOT assign "extra math homework." Advanced work *instead of* — not *after* — is the key principle.
- Celebrate curiosity and persistence, not correct answers.

### Fostering Mathematical Thinking Without Drilling

The RO2 question "How do I foster mathematical thinking without drilling or pressure?" is best answered through specific activities that develop problem-solving instincts naturally:

- **Count and estimate during daily life.** "How many steps to the car?" "About how many blueberries are in this bowl?" These build number sense without worksheets.
- **Ask open-ended math questions.** "How many different ways can you arrange these 4 blocks?" "If we cut this sandwich into triangles instead of rectangles, how many pieces could we make?" These develop combinatorial and geometric thinking.
- **Play mathematical games.** Set, Blokus, Rush Hour, KenKen, and tangrams all develop spatial reasoning, logic, and pattern recognition while feeling like play, not homework.
- **Read math-adjacent books.** *Bedtime Math*, *The Number Devil*, *Mathematicians Are People Too*, and *Sir Cumference* series normalize mathematical curiosity as entertainment.
- **Let the child be stuck.** When a child encounters a hard puzzle, resist solving it for them. Productive struggle — sitting with a problem for 15-30 minutes without help — is the foundational skill that separates enrichment from drilling.

## Grade Band: 3-5 (Ages 8-11) — Foundation

**Goal:** Develop problem-solving instincts before formal algebra. First structured competitions.

One K-12 roadmap suggests that for grades 3-5, Beast Academy grades 3-5 and MOEMS Division E (starting grade 4) are appropriate, with a core goal of developing problem-solving instincts before formal algebra.

### Recommended Activities

| Resource | Type | Grade | Notes |
|----------|------|-------|-------|
| [[aops-art-of-problem-solving|Beast Academy]] Levels 3-5 | Curriculum | 3-5 | Core enrichment |
| Singapore Math | Curriculum | 3-5 | Strong foundations; bar model approach |
| [[mathcounts|MOEMS]] Division E | Competition | 4-6 | Team-based; low pressure |
| Math Kangaroo (Grades 3-5) | Competition | 3-5 | Annual; good benchmark |
| AoPS Prealgebra | Curriculum | 5+ | Bridge to competition prep |

### Key Decisions

- **When to start AoPS Prealgebra:** When the child has mastered Beast Academy Level 5 or equivalent. Grade level matters less than readiness.
- **Drill programs (Kumon, Mathnasium):** These serve a different function from competition prep — building automaticity for arithmetic rather than problem-solving skills.

Some parents on gifted education forums report that drill programs (Kumon, Mathnasium) serve a different function from competition math preparation, building automaticity for arithmetic rather than problem-solving skills. Some families report using Kumon in early elementary for fluency, then switching to problem-solving-focused programs by grades 3-4.

## Grade Band: 6-8 (Ages 11-14) — Competition Entry

**Goal:** Enter structured competitions. Build algebra, geometry, and combinatorics foundations.

One K-12 roadmap suggests that for grades 6-8, MATHCOUNTS, AMC 8, AoPS Prealgebra and Introduction to Algebra, and MOEMS Division M are appropriate. Advanced students may attempt AMC 10 in grade 8.

| [[mathcounts|MATHCOUNTS]] | Competition | Grades 6-8; school → chapter → state → national |
| AoPS Introduction Series | Curriculum | Algebra, Geometry, Counting & Probability, Number Theory |
| [[mathcounts|MOEMS]] Division M | Competition | Grades 6-8 |
| [[entities/amc-competitions|Math Kangaroo]] | Competition | Grades 5+ version: 30 questions in 75 minutes |
| [[entities/mathpath|MathPath]] | Summer | Ages 11-14; ~30% acceptance rate |

### Key Milestones

- **AMC 8 score of 20+:** Indicates readiness for AMC 10 preparation.
- **MATHCOUNTS State qualifier:** Strong signal of competition talent.
- **Completing AoPS Introduction to Algebra:** Ready for AMC 10/12 preparation.

Some parents on gifted education forums report that MATHCOUNTS preparation typically begins in grade 6 with AoPS Prealgebra and MATHCOUNTS handbooks. The transition from AMC 8 to AMC 10 is frequently described on forums as a significant difficulty jump.

## Grade Band: 9-12 (Ages 14-18) — Specialization

**Goal:** Advance through the competition pipeline. Add physics if interested. Consider summer programs and research.

One K-12 roadmap suggests that for grades 9-12, the path includes AMC 10/12 -> AIME -> USAMO, AoPS Intermediate/Advanced courses, F=ma (physics, typically starting grade 10+), science research for ISEF/STS (grades 10-12), and USAMTS for proof-writing development.

### Summer Programs

See [[summer-programs-guide]] for the full comparison. Key programs by selectivity:

### Key Decisions

- **When to add physics:** After completing algebra-based mechanics (typically grade 10). See [[physics-competitions-pathway]].
- **Competition vs. research:** Students interested in [[science-competitions|ISEF or STS]] should begin research by grade 10-11.
- **When to seek coaching:** Private coaching becomes most relevant at AIME+ level for proof-writing feedback. See [[enrichment-programs-guide]].

## Common Mistakes

- **Enrolling a grade-3 student in MATHCOUNTS-style training when they have not completed Beast Academy.** This leads to frustration with multi-step problems the student lacks foundations for. Competition-style training requires algebraic reasoning skills that develop in grades 4-6, not grades 2-3.
- **Forcing AMC 10 in 8th grade because "the roadmap says so."** If the student has not completed the AoPS Introduction series, they are not ready regardless of grade. Readiness is skill-based, not age-based.
- **Ignoring physics until too late.** The F=ma → USAPhO pipeline is shorter than the math pipeline — starting in grade 10 still allows two attempts. See [[physics-competitions-pathway]].
- **Neglecting proof writing.** The AIME-to-USAMO transition requires proof skills that most students lack. Start [[entities/usamts|USAMTS]] early for practice.
- **Over-scheduling beyond one enrichment program plus one competition series.** More programs do not accelerate progress; they accelerate [[burnout-prevention|burnout]].
