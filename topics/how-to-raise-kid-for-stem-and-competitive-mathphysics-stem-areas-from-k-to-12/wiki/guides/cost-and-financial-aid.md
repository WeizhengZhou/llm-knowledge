# Cost and Financial Aid for Competitive STEM Preparation

## Overview

Competitive STEM preparation can cost anywhere from $0 to $20,000+ per year depending on the level of engagement and program choices. This guide breaks down costs by tier, identifies financial aid sources, and highlights the extensive free resources available.

## Annual Cost Tiers

One aggregator source estimates annual cost tiers for competitive math preparation as: Tier 1 ($0-$500/year) for free resources and competition registrations; Tier 2 ($500-$3,000/year) for AoPS books and 1-2 courses; Tier 3 ($3,000-$8,000/year) for multiple courses and summer programs; Tier 4 ($8,000-$20,000+/year) for private tutoring and selective summer programs. These are approximations that vary by geography and program choice.

| Tier | Annual Cost | Typical Profile |
|------|-----------|----------------|
| 1 — Entry level | $0-$500 | Free resources + competition registrations + 1-2 books |
| 2 — Serious | $500-$3,000 | AoPS books + 1-2 courses or RSM |
| 3 — Advanced | $3,000-$8,000 | Multiple courses + one summer program |
| 4 — Elite | $8,000-$20,000+ | Private tutoring + selective summer programs + travel |

### Competition Registration

Most competitions have minimal registration fees ($10-$30) or are free. Travel costs for state and national competitions can add $500-$2,000/year for serious competitors.

## Financial Aid Strategy

### Step 1: Start with Free Resources
Alcumus + past tests + library copies of AoPS books cover most needs through AIME level.

### Step 2: Invest Selectively
The highest-value paid investments are: (1) AoPS Introduction books (~$240 for the set), and (2) one summer program. The summer program community experience is difficult to replicate for free.

### Step 3: Apply for Summer Program Aid
PROMYS is free for families under $80K. Mathcamp is free for families under $100K. RSI is free for everyone. All other major programs offer need-based aid — apply regardless of income.

### Step 4: Avoid Low-Value Spending
Private tutoring before AIME level rarely outperforms self-study with AoPS books. Multiple simultaneous programs produce diminishing returns and increase [[burnout-prevention|burnout]] risk.

## Opportunity Cost: What Heavy Competition Focus Displaces

Financial cost is only one dimension. The more significant cost for many families is what the child gives up in time, social activities, and breadth of experience.

One review source estimates practice hours by competition level as: AMC 8 preparation at 2-4 hours/week, AMC 10/12 at 5-10 hours/week, AIME at 8-15 hours/week, and USAMO-level at 15-25+ hours/week. These are unverified estimates from an aggregator site, not empirical data.

At AIME level (8-15 hours/week), a student typically cannot maintain more than one additional serious extracurricular (e.g., a varsity sport OR a musical instrument, but not both). At USAMO level (15-25+ hours/week), competition math becomes the student's primary activity outside of school, and college applications will show a correspondingly narrow extracurricular profile.

**What this means practically:**

- **At AMC 8/10 level (2-10 hrs/week):** Minimal displacement. Compatible with sports, music, social activities, and normal childhood. This is where most students stop, and stopping here is a perfectly legitimate outcome.
- **At AIME level (8-15 hrs/week):** Meaningful trade-offs. The student begins to specialize. This is the level where the family should have an explicit conversation about whether the depth is worth the narrowing.
- **At USAMO level (15-25+ hrs/week):** Major lifestyle commitment. The student's college application will be math-heavy. This is appropriate for students with genuine passion; it is problematic for students who are continuing out of sunk-cost reasoning or parental expectation.

For a broader framework on whether this path is right for your child, see [[is-this-right-for-my-child]]. For signs that the intensity is becoming harmful, see [[burnout-prevention]].

## Common Mistakes

- **Spending heavily in elementary.** Free and low-cost resources (Beast Academy, library books, Math Kangaroo) are sufficient for K-5.
- **Paying for tutoring too early.** Self-study with AoPS books is more effective than tutoring for most students through AMC 10/12 level.
- **Not applying for financial aid.** Many families assume they will not qualify but should apply regardless — aid formulas vary by program.
- **Equating spending with outcomes.** The most important factor is the student's intrinsic motivation and practice volume, not program cost.
