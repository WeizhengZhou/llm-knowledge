# Chapter 8: Training Providers, Coaching, and Books — Getting the Right Help

The most successful competition math students rarely owe their results to expensive tutoring or elite prep programs. They owe them to the right books, at the right time, with enough patience to work through the hard problems themselves.

This is counterintuitive in an industry that sells coaching packages and curriculum subscriptions and test prep courses. But the evidence from competition math — the actual trajectory of students who reach AIME and beyond — points consistently in the same direction. The bottleneck at almost every level is not access to an expert. It's access to the right material, the willingness to sit with hard problems, and the time to do it consistently.

That doesn't mean external help is never valuable. It means you should know what kind of help actually moves the needle at each stage, and spend accordingly.

---

## The Hierarchy of Help

Before picking a program or provider, it helps to understand the order in which different types of help become useful:

**Books first.** Mathematical content knowledge is the primary bottleneck through AMC 10/12 level. The right book, worked through seriously, builds more capability per dollar than any tutoring or online course at this stage. A student who has genuinely understood every problem in AoPS Introduction to Algebra has built something that a year of once-weekly tutoring typically can't replicate.

**Structured curriculum second.** AoPS online courses, math circles, and school math teams add value when a student needs external accountability, peer discussion, or instructor feedback on problem-solving approaches. These are real benefits — not everyone is self-directed enough to work through a textbook without a class structure. But they come second to the books themselves, which are the underlying content.

**Coaching third.** Private coaching becomes genuinely valuable at the AIME level and above, specifically for proof-writing feedback. Below that level, competition math instructors broadly agree that well-motivated students working through the right books do at least as well as — and often better than — students receiving private tutoring.

The rest of this chapter explains what to use at each level, and why.

## AoPS: The Ecosystem That Runs the Competition Math World

Art of Problem Solving is not a single product. It's an ecosystem — books, online courses, a free adaptive practice tool, an elementary curriculum, community forums, and an olympiad training course — and understanding what each piece does helps you choose what you actually need.

### Beast Academy (Grades 2–5)

Beast Academy covers elementary mathematics in a comic-book format, organized into Levels 2 through 5 (with each level containing four parts, e.g., 2A, 2B, 2C, 2D). According to AoPS, each level costs approximately $118, available as an online subscription or physical books.

The content — arithmetic, fractions, basic geometry, introductory logic, early combinatorics — overlaps with what school math covers, but the presentation and difficulty are fundamentally different. Beast Academy problems require thinking, not just procedure application. Many families describe what educators call the "engagement effect": kids who are bored by school math voluntarily work through Beast Academy problems because the problems feel like puzzles.

Beast Academy is the right starting point for gifted elementary students who need something beyond school math. It's not competition prep in the formal sense — it doesn't directly prepare students for AMC 8 or MATHCOUNTS — but it builds the problem-solving instincts that competition prep will later develop. Skipping it and jumping straight to AoPS Prealgebra typically produces frustration, because the Introduction series assumes a level of comfort with mathematical reasoning that Beast Academy develops and school math often doesn't.

### The AoPS Textbooks: The Full Progression

The book progression is the most important practical guidance in this chapter. Here it is, in order:

1. **AoPS Prealgebra** ($59) — Bridge from Beast Academy to competition math. Covers proportional reasoning, number theory basics, combinatorics basics. The bridge from arithmetic to algebraic thinking.

2. **Introduction to Algebra** ($59) — Core AMC 8/MATHCOUNTS text. Covers equations, polynomials, quadratics, functions. Start here for AMC competition preparation.

3. **Introduction to Counting and Probability** ($59) — Counting is a disproportionate weakness for most students relative to how often it appears on AMC tests. Do this book early.

4. **Introduction to Geometry** ($63) — Triangles, circles, similarity, area, volume. Essential for both AMC 8/10 and MATHCOUNTS.

5. **Introduction to Number Theory** ($59) — Divisibility, primes, modular arithmetic. Less immediately useful for MATHCOUNTS but foundational for AIME.

6. **AoPS Precalculus** ($59) — Trigonometry, complex numbers, vectors. Required before AMC 12 is accessible.

7. **Intermediate Algebra** ($63) — The key AMC 12/AIME text. Polynomials, inequalities, complex numbers at deeper level. Start this after finishing Introduction to Algebra.

8. **Intermediate Counting and Probability** ($63) — Generating functions, recurrences, advanced combinatorics. AIME level.

9. **The Art of Problem Solving Vol 1** ($59) — A different style of book: broad, problem-collection format rather than textbook format. Good supplement to the Introduction series.

10. **The Art of Problem Solving Vol 2** ($63) — Advanced problem techniques across all subjects. Essential for serious AIME preparation.

The principle underlying this progression: complete each book before moving to the next. Many families make the mistake of buying several books simultaneously and working through them shallowly. A student who has truly finished Introduction to Algebra — understood every problem, worked the hard exercises, not just read the chapters — is better prepared than a student who has skimmed Intermediate Algebra.

According to the AoPS store, the complete Introduction series (Algebra, Counting, Geometry, Number Theory) is approximately $236 in physical books. That's less than the cost of a single month of private tutoring for most families. It's the most cost-efficient significant investment available in competition math preparation.

### AoPS Online Courses: When They're Worth It

AoPS online courses cost approximately $415–$895 per course, depending on level and length. WOOT (Worldwide Online Olympiad Training) is $845–$895.

The honest case for taking a course rather than using the textbook: live instructor feedback on your problem-solving approaches, a peer cohort to work through problems with, and structured pacing that prevents the self-study phenomenon of getting stuck on one chapter and quietly abandoning the book. For students who have external accountability as a genuine need — not a preference, but something that actually affects whether they complete the work — an AoPS online course is worth the premium.

The honest case against: the mathematical content is identical to the textbook. A self-motivated student with a parent who can check work gets the same content from the $59 book that another student gets from a $415 course. If motivation is not the bottleneck, the book is sufficient.

WOOT is a special case. It's designed specifically for students who have already reached AIME qualification and are developing olympiad-level proof skills. Purchasing WOOT before AIME level — a common mistake according to AoPS instructors — wastes both money and the student's confidence. The material will be genuinely inaccessible. WOOT is for students at AIME 8+ level who are working toward USAMO, not for students who aspire to reach AIME someday.

### Alcumus: The Free Resource Most Families Underuse

Alcumus is a free, adaptive online math practice tool from AoPS. It adjusts problem difficulty based on performance, covers topics from prealgebra through AMC 12 level, and provides unlimited practice problems in every content area.

According to AoPS, Alcumus is free with an account on artofproblemsolving.com. There is no subscription required.

Alcumus works best as a daily practice tool alongside a textbook or course — 20–30 problems per day in the relevant topic area. Students who use it as a standalone curriculum typically find it lacks the explanatory depth of the books. Students who use it alongside Introduction to Algebra, for example, are doing real content learning in the book and daily fluency maintenance in Alcumus. That combination is highly effective.

### The AoPS Forums: The Free Resource Almost Everyone Underuses

The AoPS community forums are the largest online community for competition math students. They include full solutions to almost every major competition problem ever administered — AMC, AIME, MATHCOUNTS, USAMO, IMO — along with problem discussions, competition result threads, and mentorship from advanced students.

For a student stuck on an AIME problem, the forum solution thread is better than a tutor for most cases. The solutions are often multiple approaches written by students and mathematicians, with discussion of why each approach works. The level of detail and the variety of solution methods available for free in AoPS forums is genuinely remarkable, and most families using AoPS textbooks don't know it exists.

## Alternative Training Providers: An Honest Assessment

### AlphaStar Academy

AlphaStar offers online courses at AMC, AIME, and USAMO levels. The courses are competition-focused and taught by instructors with strong competition backgrounds. Experienced coaches consider AlphaStar a solid option for students who want structured online instruction at the AMC/AIME level and find AoPS courses too generalist in style. It's most useful as an alternative to AoPS online courses, not a replacement for the AoPS textbooks.

### Russian School of Math (RSM)

RSM is a chain of after-school math programs with a structured, mastery-based approach using in-person weekly classes and ability-based placement. Some parents on forums describe RSM as serving a different function from competition math preparation — building arithmetic fluency and school math readiness rather than the problem-solving skills competitions reward.

RSM is better positioned as an enrichment program for students who need structured in-person instruction and want to perform well in school math, AP courses, and standardized testing. For students specifically targeting competition math (AMC, MATHCOUNTS, AIME), AoPS is the more relevant ecosystem. Some families combine them in elementary school — RSM for arithmetic fluency, Beast Academy for problem-solving — and transition to purely AoPS-track preparation in middle school.

### AwesomeMath

AwesomeMath offers summer online programs and year-round courses with explicit competition prep focus — AMC, AIME, and USAMO level. According to AdmissionsAngle, summer courses run $1,275–$1,575 per course. International students are eligible.

AwesomeMath's summer program is the best structured alternative for students who don't get into selective residential programs (Ross, PROMYS, Mathcamp). It's less immersive but more content-focused than a month of undirected self-study, and the USAMO-level courses are genuinely challenging.

### OTIS (Evan Chen's Olympiad Training for Individual Study)

OTIS is an invitation-only, proof-based olympiad training program run by Evan Chen — a USAMO winner and IMO gold medalist. It targets AIME and USAMO level students who need structured proof-writing training.

OTIS is not a commercial program you can purchase. Students are admitted by invitation, typically based on strong competition results. It represents the far end of the structured training spectrum: for students who are genuinely at the olympiad level, it provides the kind of focused, high-quality feedback that no other program currently offers at that level of difficulty.

Evan Chen also makes substantial free material available — OTIS Excerpts, Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads (EGMO), and handouts from his courses — which are valuable for serious students at AIME level even if they're not OTIS participants.

## When to Hire a Private Coach: The Honest Answer

The short answer is: not until AIME level, and often not even then.

Before AIME level, the limiting factor for most students is mathematical content knowledge and volume of deliberate practice, not personalized feedback. The AoPS textbooks deliver content efficiently, Alcumus delivers practice, and AoPS forums deliver problem solutions. A private tutor working with a student at AMC 8 level is, in most cases, doing what the books would do, at ten times the cost.

Competition math instructors who work with advanced students consistently advise parents of younger students against hiring private coaches early. Many families who've raised USAMO qualifiers report that they never hired a private coach through the AMC level — the books and AoPS online courses were sufficient. The coaches came later, if at all.

At AIME level, the value proposition changes. Proof-writing is the primary skill gap separating AIME-level students from USAMO, and proof-writing requires personalized feedback that books cannot provide. An instructor who reads your proof attempts and explains specifically what's wrong — not just "the approach is incorrect" but "your case analysis is incomplete here and your notation is ambiguous there" — is genuinely valuable. The AoPS online courses provide this for students in Intermediate-level courses; WOOT provides it for AIME+ students; OTIS provides it for olympiad-level students.

If you are considering a private coach, look for someone who has personal competition math experience — ideally AIME or USAMO level — not just someone with a math degree or tutoring experience. Competition math requires a specific kind of mathematical thinking that academic credentials don't reliably signal.

## The Book Progression: From Start to Olympiad

Here is the complete resource path, organized by competition level:

### Elementary Foundation (Grades 2–6)
- **Beast Academy** Levels 2–5 (~$118/level, online or physical)
- **Singapore Math** — Strong bar-model approach to word problems; complement to Beast Academy
- **Competition Math for Middle School** (Batterson, $45) — Bridge from Beast Academy to AoPS Introduction; MATHCOUNTS-focused

### AMC 8 / MATHCOUNTS Level (Grades 5–8)
- **AoPS Prealgebra** ($59) — Start here after Beast Academy Level 5
- **Introduction to Algebra** ($59) — Core AMC 8 / MATHCOUNTS prep
- **Introduction to Counting and Probability** ($59)
- **Introduction to Geometry** ($63)
- **Introduction to Number Theory** ($59)
- *Free:* MATHCOUNTS handbooks (mathcounts.org), AMC 8 past tests (MAA), Alcumus

### AMC 10/12 / AIME Level (Grades 8–11)
- **Intermediate Algebra** ($63) — Key AIME text
- **Intermediate Counting and Probability** ($63)
- **AoPS Precalculus** ($59) — Required for AMC 12 content
- **The Art of Problem Solving Vol 2** ($63)
- **The Art and Craft of Problem Solving** (Zeitz, ~$50) — Best general problem-solving strategies text; not problem-type-specific
- **Problem Solving Strategies** (Engel, ~$60) — Comprehensive olympiad technique reference
- *Free:* AMC 10/12 past tests (MAA), AIME past problems (MAA), Alcumus, AoPS forums

### Olympiad Level (USAMO / IMO Aspirants)
**Geometry:**
- *Euclidean Geometry in Mathematical Olympiads* (Evan Chen, "EGMO") — Start here; modern, clear, comprehensive
- *Geometry Revisited* (Coxeter and Greitzer) — Classic; use after EGMO

**Algebra and Inequalities:**
- *Inequalities* (AoPS/Andreescu)
- OTIS Excerpts by Evan Chen (free online)

**Number Theory:**
- *104 Number Theory Problems* (Andreescu)
- *Modern Olympiad Number Theory* (Evan Chen OTIS, free)

**Combinatorics:**
- *A Path to Combinatorics for Undergraduates* (Andreescu)

**General Olympiad:**
- *Putnam and Beyond* (Gelca/Andreescu)
- Past USAMO and IMO problems (free, MAA and imo-official.org)

### Physics: F=ma Through IPhO
**F=ma level:**
- *Problems and Solutions in Introductory Mechanics* (David Morin) — Most effective F=ma prep; problems are well-calibrated to competition level
- *Halliday/Resnick/Krane Physics* — Comprehensive reference
- *Past F=ma exams* (free from AAPT)

**USAPhO level:**
- *Introduction to Mechanics* (Kleppner and Kolenkow) — MIT standard; calculus-based
- *Electricity and Magnetism* (Purcell and Morin) — Strong electromagnetic theory coverage
- *Irodov: Problems in General Physics* — Classic comprehensive problem collection; challenging
- *Classical Mechanics* (Taylor) — Accessible mechanics with good problem sets

**IPhO level:**
- *Classical Mechanics* (Morin) — Includes Lagrangian and Hamiltonian mechanics
- *Introduction to Electrodynamics* (Griffiths) — Standard undergraduate electrodynamics
- Kevin Zhou's Physics Olympiad Handouts (free at knzhou.github.io) — The best free IPhO preparation resource currently available

## Free Resources Worth Knowing About

There is a substantial amount of genuinely excellent, free competition math material that many families don't know exists. Before purchasing anything, these are worth using:

**Alcumus** (artofproblemsolving.com) — Adaptive practice, AMC 8 through AMC 12 level. Free with account.

**Expii** (expii.com) — Free personalized learning platform founded by Po-Shen Loh, targeting middle school level (grades 5–9), aligned with MATHCOUNTS and AMC 8. According to Expii, Po-Shen Loh is a Carnegie Mellon professor who coached the US IMO team to first place in 2015, 2016, and 2018.

**AMC and AIME past tests** — All AMC 8, AMC 10, AMC 12, and AIME exams from every prior year are available free from the MAA website. Working through past tests is the single most competition-relevant practice activity available, and it costs nothing.

**MATHCOUNTS handbooks** — Free from mathcounts.org. These are the official preparation materials and include worked examples, problem sets, and past competition problems.

**AoPS forums** — Free. Contains solutions to virtually every major competition problem ever administered, with discussion.

**USAMTS problems and feedback** — USAMTS is free to enter and provides written feedback on submitted proofs. This is essentially free proof-writing coaching, and almost nobody outside the competition math community knows about it.

**Evan Chen OTIS Excerpts** — Free PDF materials covering olympiad techniques in algebra, geometry, number theory, and combinatorics. Available at web.evanchen.cc.

**Kevin Zhou Physics Handouts** — Free PDF covering IPhO-level physics. Available at knzhou.github.io. The most comprehensive free physics olympiad resource currently available.

**F=ma and USAPhO past exams** — Free from the AAPT website.

## What to Actually Spend Money On

Given the breadth of high-quality free resources, here's how to think about where money adds genuine value:

**Buy the books.** The AoPS textbooks are irreplaceable. A student who owns and works through Introduction to Algebra, Introduction to Counting, and Introduction to Geometry has spent roughly $180 and covered the core content for AMC 8 through AMC 10 preparation. This is almost always the best first investment.

**Consider AoPS online courses when motivation is the bottleneck.** If your student needs external accountability to make consistent progress, the course structure is worth the premium over the textbook. If they're self-directed, the book is sufficient.

**Wait on WOOT until AIME qualification.** Seriously.

**Consider an AwesomeMath summer course if residential programs are closed.** $1,275–$1,575 for an intensive summer course is worth it compared to an unstructured summer.

**Hold off on private coaching until the student hits a plateau at AIME level.** When a student's AIME score has been in the same range for two cycles despite consistent practice, that's when proof-writing feedback from a skilled coach starts to add value that books and forums can't provide.
