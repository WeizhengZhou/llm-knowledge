# American Regions Mathematics League (ARML)

Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Regions_Mathematics_League
Source: https://www.arml.com/ARML/arml_2019/page/index.php?page_type=public&page=14
Source: https://admissionsight.com/arml/

## Overview

ARML is an annual, national high school mathematics team competition. It is held simultaneously at five locations in the United States on the same day (first Saturday after Memorial Day, typically late May or early June).

**2025-2026 competition date:** First Saturday after Memorial Day.

**Competition sites (2025-2026):**
- University of Iowa (Iowa City, IA)
- Penn State University (University Park, PA)
- University of Nevada, Reno (Reno, NV)
- Western Carolina University (Cullowhee, NC)
- Saint Anselm College (Manchester, NH)

## Team Structure

- Teams consist of **15 members**
- Teams typically represent a large geographic region (a state) or large population center (major city)
- Each region has its own qualifying process (local math leagues, state contests, or selection tests)
- High scorers on regional/state math competitions are often invited to ARML teams

## Competition Rounds (6 total)

### 1. Team Round
- Entire team has **20 minutes** to solve **10 problems**
- Team collaborates; one answer sheet submitted

### 2. Power Question
- Entire team has **1 hour** to solve a multi-part proof/exploration question
- Typically 10 sub-parts requiring written explanations and proofs
- Graded on completeness, rigor, and mathematical insight

### 3. Individual Round
- Each student individually solves **5 sets of 2 problems**
- **10 minutes** per set (2 problems)
- No team collaboration
- Total: 10 individual problems per student

### 4. Relay Round
- Team is divided into **5 groups of 3**
- Within each group: first member solves a problem, passes answer to next member who plugs it into their problem, then passes to third member
- Time allotted: **6 minutes** per relay
- Bonus points for solving in **3 minutes** (5 points vs. 3 points for 6-minute solve)

### 5. Super Relay
- All 15 team members work in two chains of 7, with answers feeding to the 15th member
- Does NOT count toward official team score (used as a fun activity while scores are tabulated)
- Exists for community/team experience

### 6. Tiebreaker
- Used to determine top individual scorers when tied
- Head-to-head format for top individuals

## Scoring

- Team, Individual, Relay rounds all count toward team and individual standings
- Power Question is a significant portion of team score
- Individual scores contribute to team totals

## How to Participate

ARML is not an open-signup contest. Students must:
1. Join a regional or state math team that participates
2. Complete their region's qualifying process (varies by state)
3. Be selected based on local math competition performance
4. Complete team registration including consent forms and fees
5. Travel to one of the five competition sites (some regions provide transportation)

## Preparation Notes

- ARML tests all areas of competition math: algebra, geometry, number theory, combinatorics
- The Power Question is unusual — requires written proofs and mathematical exposition
- Relay round requires both accuracy and communication within small groups
- Most participants already compete in AMC 10/12 or have AIME experience
- Best preparation: AoPS Intermediate series, AIME practice, proof-writing experience

## ARML Feeder vs. ARML Itself

- Some states run "ARML Local" — a practice competition at the school or regional level using ARML-style problems
- ARML Local is more accessible and can introduce the format before trying for a full team spot
