# Summer Math Programs: Ross, PROMYS, Mathcamp Comparison

## Ross Mathematics Program

**Location (2026):**
- Otterbein University, Columbus, OH
- Rose-Hulman Institute of Technology, Terre Haute, IN (two simultaneous sites)

**Duration:** 6 weeks (Summer 2026: June 14 – July 24)

**Philosophy:**
"Think deeply about simple things." Singular focus on number theory and proof-based mathematics. Unlike programs that survey multiple topics or prepare students for competitions, Ross dedicates six weeks to exploring fundamental concepts in unprecedented depth.

**Selectivity:**
- ~15% acceptance rate (2023 data)
- ~60 first-year students per site accepted
- ~500+ applicants per cycle (both sites combined)
- Most students ages 15-18, average 16-17

**Curriculum:**
Intensive focus on a single mathematical subject (number theory). Daily problem sets, counselor interaction, mathematical discovery model. Returning students (counselors-in-training) add continuity.

**Financial aid:** Available (contact program)

**Sponsors:** American Mathematical Society, Jane Street, Citadel

## PROMYS (Program in Mathematics for Young Scientists)

**Location:** Boston University, Boston, MA

**Duration:** 6 weeks (similar timeline to Ross)

**Philosophy:**
Most similar to Ross; also emphasizes number theory and deep exploration over breadth. Founded by Ross alumni.

**Cost (2025):**
- Full program: $6,100
- Free for domestic students from families earning under $80,000/year
- Need-based financial aid available at all income levels

**Selectivity:** Highly selective, similar to Ross

## Canada/USA Mathcamp

**Location:** Rotates (residential, different campus each year)

**Duration:** 5 weeks

**Philosophy:**
Expose students to the beauty of advanced mathematical ideas and new ways of thinking. More survey-oriented than Ross/PROMYS — students attend different "classes" on varied topics each day.

**Cost (2026):**
- Base: $7,500 USD (tuition, room, board, local transport)
- After financial aid: $0-$7,500
- Free for US/Canadian households earning under $100,000 annually
- Need-based aid available internationally
- Over $100,000 distributed per year in scholarships
- No application fee

**Age range:** 13-18

**Selectivity:** Highly selective (different application — math puzzle-based "Qualifying Quiz")

## RSI (Research Science Institute)

**Host:** MIT, organized by Center for Excellence in Education (CEE)

**Duration:** 6 weeks

**Who eligible:** Rising high school seniors (juniors applying for senior year)

**Structure:**
- Week 1: Lectures and seminars
- Weeks 2-5.5: Hands-on research with MIT/university mentors
- Final week: Written paper + oral presentation

**Cost:** FREE — tuition, room, board all covered

**Selectivity:**
- ~3,000 applicants/year
- ~80-100 students accepted (about 60-70 domestic)
- Acceptance rate: ~2-3%

**Application deadline:** December 10 (for the following summer)

**Application requirements:**
- Online application with essays
- High school transcript
- PSAT/SAT/ACT/AP scores
- Two teacher recommendation letters

**College admissions impact:** Extremely significant — one of the most prestigious high school programs nationally. Viewed as a genuine research credential.

## Other Notable Programs

**HCSSiM** (Hampshire College Summer Studies in Mathematics)
- 6 weeks, Hampshire College MA
- Very small, student-centered
- Very selective

**SUMaC** (Stanford University Mathematics Camp)
- Stanford hosted, strong alumni outcomes
- 3-4 weeks

**MathILy / MathILy-Er**
- MathILy: 5 weeks, primarily for AIME-level students
- MathILy-Er: Feeder program for less experienced students
- Both emphasize inquiry-based learning

**SPARC** (Summer Program on Applied Rationality and Cognition)
- Application-based; focuses on math + interdisciplinary thinking
- Very selective, free

**AwesomeMath Summer Program**
- 3 weeks
- Competition-prep oriented (AMC/AIME/USAMO focus)
- Multiple sites

**MathPath**
- Middle school (ages 11-14)
- Residential, 4 weeks
- Earlier entry point than Ross/Mathcamp
