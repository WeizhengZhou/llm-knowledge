# Gender Gap in Math Competitions: Statistics and Pipeline Data

## AMC Participation Rates

- Approximately 43% of AMC 12 takers are female (source: Ellison-Swanson research, MIT Economics).
- For the 2021 AMC 12A, 37.3% of participants were girls; for AMC 12B, 34.8% were girls.
- Female students are underrepresented in the AMC program, and "this underrepresentation becomes increasingly more pronounced at the later rounds."

## AIME Participation (2021 data)

- Of the 6,350 students who took the 2021 AIME exam, 1,340 were girls.
- That is approximately 21.1% female, 78.9% male.
- This represents a substantial drop from ~37-43% female at the AMC 12 level.

## USAMO / USA(J)MO Participation

- Approximately 500 students qualify for USA(J)MO annually.
- Female qualification rate: approximately 10% (remained relatively constant over five years as of 2017 data from Awesome Math Girls).
- In 2020, approximately 14% of USAMO participants were women — described as an improvement compared to 6–8% in prior years (source: arxiv.org/html/2404.15511v1).
- The average number of girls who qualify has stayed constant at approximately 10% despite outreach efforts.
- The article notes a concern: "the qualification of girls have stayed mostly constant... given the drastic drop in number of female students who qualify for the AIME."

## IMO Participation (Historical)

- From 1959 to 2021, there were 43 female (3.3%) and 1,295 male gold medal winners at the IMO.
- Female participation at the IMO is typically in the single-digit percentage range.

## Pipeline Dropout Pattern

The data shows a consistent funnel effect:
- AMC 8: Not disaggregated by available data, but girls may participate at higher rates among younger students.
- AMC 10/12: ~37–43% female.
- AIME: ~21% female.
- USAMO: ~10–14% female.
- IMO team: Very rare — US has had extremely few female IMO team members historically.

## Research Findings on Dropout Causes (Ellison-Swanson, MIT)

- "High-achieving girls improve by less from year to year on average than do boys with similar initial performance."
- "Girls at each performance level are more likely to drop out of participating."
- "Girls are underrepresented among the high-scoring entrants."
- These are structural and behavioral patterns, not explained by initial performance differences.

## MAA Response

- The MAA invites "the top-scoring AMC girl-identifying students to USAMO and USAJMO each year" to create pathways to MOP, EGMO, and potentially IMO.
- AMC Young Women in Mathematics Award: top 5 scorers on each of AMC 8, AMC 10, and AMC 12 split a $5,000 scholarship; top 5 scorers per MAA Section receive certificates.
- The MAA distributes 20 scholarships and 580 certificates to top-scoring women annually.
