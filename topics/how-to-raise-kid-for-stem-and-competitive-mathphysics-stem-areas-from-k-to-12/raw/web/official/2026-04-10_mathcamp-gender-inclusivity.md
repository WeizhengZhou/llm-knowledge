# Canada/USA Mathcamp: Gender Inclusivity

## Student Demographics

- Student body is typically 45–50% girls and non-binary students.
- One source gives more specific breakdown: approximately 47% girls, 1% non-binary, 52% boys.
- This is dramatically higher female representation than at comparable programs (e.g., Ross, PROMYS do not publish similar statistics).

## Core Philosophy

The camp's foundational principle is "Be excellent to each other."

The camp is "intentionally welcoming to students and staff of all gender experiences" and pays "particular attention to building support for girls and women (cis and trans), non-binary folks, and trans boys and men."

The camp also creates space for cisgender boys and men to develop allyship skills.

## Staff Composition

- Mathcamp deliberately recruits diverse staff members and visiting speakers.
- Women serve as both instructors and residential advisors.
- Students report having access to female mathematics teachers and mentors — described as a notable contrast to many traditional academic settings and other elite math programs.

## Programmatic Interventions for Gender Inclusion

**Workshops and discussion groups specifically addressing:**
- "Women and Gender Minorities in Math": covers bias in academia, affirmative action, building mentor networks, and allyship strategies.
- Impostor Syndrome: explores the psychological phenomenon and support strategies.
- "Future of You": discusses post-high school opportunities and career pathways.
- Experiences of girls and non-binary students affected by (trans)misogyny in academia.
- Implicit and institutionalized bias.
- Succeeding as a woman in a STEM field.
- How to be a good ally.

## Community Features

- Strong community of girls and non-binary students comfortable being grouped with girls.
- Described by participants as "a safe space to explore their experience of gender identity."
- Thriving queer community supported by staff.

## Post-Camp Connections

- Mathcamp alumnae frequently reconnect at Math Prize for Girls competitions, sustaining community bonds beyond summer.
- This post-camp network is identified as a significant additional benefit.

## Why It Matters (Research Context)

Research on stereotype threat and sense of belonging shows that:
- Female role models in math (instructors and staff who are women) directly reduce girls' stereotype threat.
- Peer communities of female mathematicians reduce the "male domain" signal.
- Named workshops addressing gender dynamics help students develop strategies to navigate stereotype threat throughout their careers.

Mathcamp's approach — intentional staff recruitment, dedicated programming, demographic goals, and post-camp networks — addresses multiple evidence-based leverage points simultaneously.

## Comparison to Other Programs

No comparable published data was found for HCSSiM, PROMYS, or Ross on gender demographics. However:

- HCSSiM: Prior research session found a note that "gender balance explicitly maintained" at HCSSiM (from s-2026-04-10-034 findings; source: HCSSiM official site).
- PROMYS and Ross: No stated gender balance goals found in official documentation.
- MathILy: Held at Bryn Mawr College (historically women's college) — likely has different demographics but no published statistics found.
