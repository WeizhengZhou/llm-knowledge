# USA Mathematical Talent Search (USAMTS)

Source: https://www.usamts.org/
Source: https://www.usamts.org/contest/getting-started/
Source: https://admissionsight.com/usamts/

## Overview

USAMTS (USA Mathematical Talent Search) is a free, proof-based math competition for US middle and high school students. It is administered by the Art of Problem Solving Foundation and supported by the National Security Agency.

**Key distinguishing feature:** Students have over a month to solve each round — completely unlike timed competitions. This makes USAMTS a training tool for mathematical writing and deep problem solving rather than a test of speed.

## Round Structure

**Three rounds per school year:**

| Feature | Detail |
|---------|--------|
| Rounds per year | 3 |
| Problems per round | 5 (1 puzzle + 4 proof-based problems) |
| Time to solve | Over 1 month per round |
| Problem types | 1 puzzle/pattern problem + 4 proof-based problems |
| Resources allowed | Any books, calculators, or online resources — but NO help from another person |
| Solution format | Full written proof/justification required for each problem |

## Scoring System

- Each problem is graded on a **scale of 0 to 5**
  - 0: highly flawed or incomplete
  - 5: rigorous, well-written proof
- Graders provide **personalized written feedback** on each submission, not just a score
- This feedback is a primary training value of USAMTS

**Maximum score per round:** 25 points (5 problems × 5 points)
**Maximum score per year:** 75 points

## Registration and Cost

- **Completely free**
- Open to all US middle and high school students
- Students can register at any time during the year, even after Round 1 has started
- No school registration required — individual students sign up directly at usamts.org

## Prizes and Recognition

- Top 45% of students receive prizes: math books and T-shirts
- Potential Wolfram software subscriptions for top performers
- **AIME qualification:** Strong USAMTS performance is one pathway to qualify for the AIME (as an alternative to AMC 10/12)

## How USAMTS Is Used as Training

**Primary use cases:**

1. **Proof writing development:** The requirement for fully written, graded proofs with feedback is the best systematic proof-writing training available at the high school level outside of dedicated courses

2. **Deep problem exploration:** The extended timeline (1 month per round) encourages genuine mathematical investigation rather than test tricks

3. **Self-study tool:** Students can solve 1-2 problems per week and use the grader feedback to improve

4. **Bridge to olympiad-level mathematics:** Problem difficulty overlaps with AIME and entry-level USAMO

5. **Low-pressure alternative:** For students who struggle with timed competitions (test anxiety, slow writers) but have strong mathematical thinking

## Difficulty Level

- Problems vary in difficulty within each round
- Round structure: "There is something challenging yet approachable for everyone"
- Difficulty spans AIME-level to USAMO-level
- The puzzle problem is often more accessible; proofs increase in difficulty through the round

## How USAMTS Differs from Timed Competitions

| Feature | USAMTS | AMC/AIME |
|---------|--------|----------|
| Time limit | 1 month per round | 75 min / 3 hours |
| Resources allowed | Yes (books, internet) | No |
| Team/collaboration | No (strictly individual) | No |
| Proof required | Yes (mandatory) | No (AIME: fill-in number) |
| Feedback provided | Yes (personalized) | Score only |
| Cost | Free | $0-$30 depending on school |
| AIME qualification | Yes (strong performance) | Primary pathway |

## Problem Types

USAMTS problems typically involve:
- Number theory
- Combinatorics
- Geometry
- Algebra
- Logic/pattern puzzles (the "puzzle" problem each round)

Problems are original and creative — not recycled from existing sources. They often have elegant solutions that require genuine insight rather than formula application.

## Relationship to Evan Chen's Advice

Evan Chen and other USAMO/IMO competitors specifically recommend USAMTS for students wanting to transition from AMC-style computation to proof-based olympiad math. The feedback mechanism accelerates the learning curve for proof writing.
