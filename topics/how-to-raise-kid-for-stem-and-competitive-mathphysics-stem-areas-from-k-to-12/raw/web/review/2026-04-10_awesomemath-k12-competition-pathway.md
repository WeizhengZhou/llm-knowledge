# K-12 Math Competition Pathway Guide — AwesomeMath / AoPS

## Elementary Level

**Math Kangaroo** — grades 1-12, no invitation required
- 75-minute format; varying question counts by grade level
- Registration through local centers like schools and math circles

**MOEMS (Mathematical Olympiads for Elementary and Middle Schools)** — grades 4-8
- Open school registration
- Monthly 30-minute rounds, November through March
- Emphasizes team-based collaboration

## Middle School (Grades 6-8)

**MATHCOUNTS** — exclusively grades 6-8, requires school enrollment
- Four-round series: Sprint, Target, Team, and Countdown
- Progression: school → chapter → state → national
- Pinnacle of middle school math competition

**AMC 8** — students under 14.5 years old in grade 8 or below
- 25 multiple-choice questions, 40 minutes
- "The earliest entry point to the Olympiad pathway"

## High School Progression

**AMC 10/12** — two levels based on age and grade
- 25 questions, 75 minutes
- Top performers earn AIME invitations

**AIME** — invitation-only after strong AMC performance
- 15 free-response questions over three hours
- Requires "sophisticated problem-solving strategies"
- Integer answers 0–999

**USA(J)MO** — invitation-based using combined AMC and AIME scores
- Proof-based format
- Six questions spread over two days

**IMO** — ultimate pinnacle
- Six students selected annually to represent USA internationally

## Alternative Entry Competitions

**USAMTS (USA Mathematical Talent Search)**
- No invitation required
- Proof-based, monthly format
- ~68/75 qualifies for AIME credit

**Purple Comet! Math Meet**
- Free, online, team-based
- Open to middle and high school

## When to Start (Implied Timeline)

- Grades 1-5: Math Kangaroo (lowest pressure entry)
- Grades 4-8: MOEMS (team, school-based)
- Grades 6-8: MATHCOUNTS (primary middle school competition)
- Grade 6-8: AMC 8 (begin Olympiad pathway)
- Grades 9-10: AMC 10 (move to high school competition)
- Grades 10-12: AMC 12, AIME (for top performers)

## Preparation Philosophy

Success requires "deliberate practice, expert instruction, and sustained engagement with challenging problems."

"Competition excellence isn't about achieving perfect scores" but developing sophisticated reasoning and conceptual understanding across multiple formats.
