# Regeneron STS vs. ISEF vs. JSHS: Comparison and What Wins

## The Three Major US Science Research Competitions

### 1. Regeneron Science Talent Search (STS)

**Administered by:** Society for Science (in partnership with Regeneron)
**Eligible students:** HIGH SCHOOL SENIORS ONLY; must attend school in the United States or its territories; US citizenship or residency required
**Scale:** ~2,500 applicants per year → 300 Scholars → 40 Finalists

**Format:**
- Entirely application-based (not a traditional science fair)
- Submitted materials include:
  - Research report up to 20 pages (includes all photos, tables, graphs, appendices)
  - Short essays about scientific thinking and motivations
  - Educator recommendations
  - Academic transcript
  - Optional: standardized test scores
- NO in-person booth presentation for Scholars; Finalists travel to Washington, DC for interviews and presentations

**Awards:**
- Total prizes: $3.1 million annually
- Top prize: $250,000
- 300 Scholars each receive $2,000
- 40 Finalists each receive at least $25,000 (top prize $250,000)

**What wins:**
- Research demonstrating deep intellectual ownership of original investigation
- Projects showing scientific originality and creative thinking
- Completed, original research — not proposals, reviews, or incomplete work
- Research report is 20 pages or fewer
- Holistic review: the application resembles a highly selective college application; essays, recommendations, and GPA matter alongside the research itself
- Judges seek "future leaders in STEM" not just impressive results
- Big data analysis, computational biology, mathematics, and bioinformatics projects can be done without a university lab

**Note on mathematics:** A math project won a top prize in 2025 (solving a long-standing math problem). This is unusual but signals that theoretical work is welcome.

---

### 2. International Science and Engineering Fair (ISEF)

**Administered by:** Society for Science (now "Regeneron ISEF")
**Eligible students:** Grades 9-12; welcomes international students from 60+ countries
**Scale:** ~1,700 finalists from 60+ countries at the international fair; hundreds of thousands compete at local/regional level first

**Format:**
- Traditional science fair model: booth-style presentations
- PATHWAY to ISEF: Must first qualify through a Society for Science-affiliated regional fair (there are 350+ worldwide)
- Teams of up to three students permitted (solo is most common at top level)
- Students travel to ISEF and present their project at a booth to judges over multiple days

**Awards:**
- Total awards: nearly $9 million annually
- Top prize (Gordon E. Moore Award): $100,000
- Hundreds of category and special awards from external organizations

**What wins:**
- Projects valued for clarity, clean experimental design, and strong scientific communication
- Judges are professional scientists and engineers who evaluate how well students understand their own research
- Projects with clear experimental controls, reproducible methods, and honest limitations tend to score well
- Original data collection and genuine discovery are rewarded over literature review

---

### 3. Junior Science and Humanities Symposia (JSHS)

**Administered by:** U.S. Army, Navy, and Air Force (tri-service)
**Status as of late 2025:** Temporarily suspended pending further updates
**Eligible students:** Grades 9-12; limited to US citizens and Lawful Permanent Residents

**Format (when active):**
- Formal oral presentations (12 minutes) plus Q&A at regional and national symposia
- Individual submissions only
- Poster presentations also required at national level

**Awards (when active):**
- Over $400,000 in total scholarships and cash prizes
- National first-place oral presenters earn $12,000 scholarships per category

## Head-to-Head Comparison

| Feature | STS | ISEF | JSHS |
|---------|-----|------|------|
| Grade level | Seniors only | 9-12 | 9-12 |
| Nationality | US only | International | US only |
| Format | Application | Booth presentation | Oral presentation |
| Team | Individual | Up to 3 | Individual |
| Pathway | Direct application | Regional fair → ISEF | Regional symposium |
| Scale | ~2,500 applicants | ~1,700 finalists | National finalists |
| Top prize | $250,000 | $100,000 | $12,000 |
| Prestige | Highest in US | Highest globally | Moderate |
| Currently active | Yes | Yes | Suspended (2025) |

## Preparation Timeline for Maximum Impact

**Grades 9-10:** Build research habits. JSHS regional entry (if available) for practice.

**Grades 10-11:** ISEF becomes viable. Need 6-12 months of genuine project development plus regional fair qualification.

**Grade 12:** STS requires research substantially completed before senior year begins. Applications open June, due November.

---

## Key Strategic Differences

**STS is for seniors who have already done the research.** Students cannot start a project in senior year and submit it to STS — the research should be substantially complete. The holistic application review means a student with weaker research but exceptional essays and academic record can be a Scholar.

**ISEF requires qualifying regionally first.** Not every region has an affiliated fair. Students should identify their regional fair early (often affiliated with their school district, county, or state) and enter regionally in grades 10 or 11. Winning at regional opens the door to ISEF.

**Both STS and ISEF reward original data collection** over literature review or mathematical modeling without experiments — though theoretical/computational projects are accepted and can win.

---

## Admissions Signal and College Context

**STS:** "Signals sustained independent inquiry and advanced academic maturity." STS alumni have earned thirteen Nobel Prizes. Top colleges regularly announce STS Scholars and Finalists in admissions announcements.

**ISEF:** "Signals applied research skill and scientific communication at a global level." Being an ISEF finalist demonstrates internationally competitive scientific rigor. Particularly valued at research universities.

**JSHS:** When active, "signals early research engagement and strong presentation ability." Most accessible entry point for building research credentials.

**Key insight:** "All three competitions carry real admissions weight, but context determines value." A student who is an ISEF Finalist but not an STS Scholar is not disadvantaged in admissions — the two competitions are complementary pathways, not alternatives where one is strictly better.
