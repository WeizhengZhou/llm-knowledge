# Expert Training Philosophies: Evan Chen and Po-Shen Loh

## Evan Chen (2-time IMO gold medalist, USAMO winner, MIT PhD)

### Core Philosophy
Chen emphasizes proof-writing excellence and problem-solving methodology. His approach centers on understanding the "thought process" behind solutions rather than memorizing techniques.

Key principle: "if you really have a completely correct solution to a problem, it is actually pretty hard to not get full credit." Proof-writing is not about formalism — it's about clearly communicating correct reasoning.

### What to Study (Chen's Recommended Path)

For beginners approaching olympiad math:
1. Read USAMO/USAJMO welcome letters with sample problems and complete solutions
2. Start attempting problems, then compare solutions on AoPS forum with experienced users
3. Progress through four olympiad areas: Algebra (functional equations, inequalities), Combinatorics (probabilistic methods), Geometry (EGMO textbook), Number Theory (modular arithmetic)

Chen's recommended books:
- Art and Craft of Problem-Solving (Paul Zeitz) — general foundation
- EGMO (Chen's own geometry textbook, free PDF) — modern standard
- Modern Olympiad Number Theory (OTIS, free) — comprehensive
- OTIS Excerpts (free online) — inequalities, functional equations, combinatorics

For practice: USAMTS (untimed, one month, feedback), then USEMO (hard IMO-level), then past USAMO/IMO problems.

### Training Philosophy

"[The value] lies solely in the student's effort... I am just a guide."

Key insight on effort: The genuine learning comes from struggling with problems, not from reading solutions. Students need to develop the habit of sitting with a hard problem for an extended time.

On training trajectory: "At some point, students need to start working through past problems from recent years of contests." Don't just read — do.

### Non-Zero-Sum View
"The contest community is good at thinking about it as just trying to maximize their own score. The score of the person next to you isn't supposed to matter."

Chen views mathematical development as fundamentally collaborative, not competitive. The community (AoPS forums, study groups, camps like MOP) is as valuable as the competitions themselves.

### Lessons from Olympiads (Blog Post, 2018)

**Two major benefits:**
1. Building social networks among gifted students with shared interests
2. Fostering intellectual growth through challenging problem-solving

**Underappreciated lessons:**
- Learning independence (largely self-directed preparation)
- Developing organizational abilities
- Understanding learning itself (reflecting on training methodologies)
- Recognizing depth in fundamentals rather than collecting advanced technique names
- Building intuition about vague mathematical concepts

**On failure:**
"If you don't do well, if you fail, it's better to you." (attributed to Zuming Feng)

The genuine value emerges from the year-long training journey itself, not the final USAMO result.

### Resources Chen Created
- OTIS (Olympiad Training for Individual Study) — program for USA(J)MO-level students
- USEMO (US Ersatz Math Olympiad) — free practice olympiad at USAMO/IMO level
- EGMO textbook (free PDF at web.evanchen.cc)

## Po-Shen Loh (CMU professor, US IMO Team coach 2014-2023)

### Core Philosophy

Goal as coach: "My goal as coach wasn't going to be to win, it was going to be to maximize the number of them that I read about in The New York Times in 20 years."

Not about competition tricks — about developing future leaders in science and technology.

### Training Innovations

**International collaboration:** Invited international Olympiad teams to train alongside US competitors. "We teach them all the secrets. We treat them as our own. And we just have fun for three weeks learning all kinds of crazy mathematics."

**Exposure beyond competition:** Evening seminars feature working mathematicians on real-world applications (e.g., quantum algorithms) that won't appear on the actual exam.

**Philosophy:** Don't "spend all day and all night helping the students know competition tricks inside and out." Priority: exposure to how professionals actually use mathematics.

### Problem-Solving Approach

Loh's principle: Tackling hard math problems requires persistence and unorthodox thinking.

"It is not about sitting down and trying to think of the 'right idea' that will work. It's about starting with any idea and see where it takes you. In improv the principle is if you start doing something, you're not supposed to suddenly go and say, 'Oh, no, sorry, that was wrong.' Solving a math problem, it's also the same."

Key: Dive in without knowing where the idea leads. Push every idea all the way through.

### Expii Platform

Loh founded Expii, a crowdsourced math lesson and problem-solving platform. Philosophy: "teach you how to invent, not to memorize and repeat." Covers concepts for MATHCOUNTS, AMC 8, AMC 10.

### Common Mistakes Loh Identifies

1. Over-focusing on competition tricks at the expense of mathematical understanding
2. Training to win rather than to grow as mathematicians and thinkers
3. Treating competition as zero-sum when the real goal is everyone's development

### What He Changed About US Team Prep

- 2015: Won IMO for first time in 21 years
- 2016: All 6 team members won gold medals
- Key change: Culture shift from "learn tricks to win" to "develop mathematical thinkers"

## Zuming Feng (IMO team leader 1997-2013, Phillips Exeter Academy)

Not as extensively documented in public sources, but known for:
- Long tenure as USAMO and IMO team leader
- Teaching at Phillips Exeter with rigorous proof-based approach
- Authored multiple olympiad books with Titu Andreescu
- Philosophy: depth over breadth, understanding over technique collection
