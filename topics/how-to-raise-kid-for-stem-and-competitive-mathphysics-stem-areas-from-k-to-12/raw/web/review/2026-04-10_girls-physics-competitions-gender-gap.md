# Girls in Physics Competitions: Gender Gap Data and Research

## Scale of the Physics Gender Gap

Physics competitions have a more extreme gender gap than math competitions:

- At the international level: female rates in physics olympiads range from 0% to 14% across participating countries.
- US Physics Olympiad training camp (USAPhO semifinalists/finalists): Documented instance with 19 boys and 1 girl among 20 qualifiers (5% female).
- German Physics Olympiad study (N=298 first-round participants): 28% female at the first round, declining significantly at later rounds.
- International systematic review (Steegh et al., Journal of Research in Science Teaching, 2019): Female underrepresentation in physics competitions is documented across multiple countries and cultures, more severe than in mathematics.

## Why Physics Has a Larger Gap Than Math

Research identifies several compounding factors specific to physics:

1. **Stronger "male domain" stereotype**: Physics carries a stronger cultural association with masculinity than even mathematics.
2. **Self-concept deficit**: Female physics competitors show significantly lower self-concept and success expectations than male peers, even when objectively equally capable.
3. **Sense of belonging**: The Physics Olympiad environment "is predominantly male and thereby promoting the association of physics being rather male, which presents cues to female participants that might reduce their belonging."
4. **Stereotype endorsement amplifier**: Female participants who endorse negative stereotypes about female talent for physics show dramatically reduced sense of belonging (r = −0.39). Males show no such effect (r = −0.01).

## USAPhO-Specific Context

- F=ma exam: No publicly available gender breakdown data found. The pipeline effect (fewer girls at each stage) mirrors math competitions but is more severe.
- USAPhO: No girls-specific analog to EGMO exists for physics. Girls qualify through the same F=ma → USAPhO pathway as boys.
- US Physics Olympiad team (5 members): Female representation has historically been very rare. No published annual gender statistics found from AAPT.
- IPhO: Female participation across all countries typically under 15%.

## No Girls-Only Physics Competition in the US

Unlike math (which has EGMO, Math Prize for Girls, AMC Young Women in Mathematics awards), there is no girls-specific physics competition program in the United States as of 2026.

Internationally: The Asian Physics Olympiad and some regional programs have made specific efforts to recruit female participants, but no US-specific girls physics olympiad exists.

## Women in Physics Programs (Non-Competition)

- **American Physical Society (APS) Committee on the Status of Women in Physics (CSWP):** Runs site visits, workshops, and career resources for women in physics. Not competition-focused.
- **Society of Physics Students (SPS):** Includes outreach to underrepresented groups but no girls-specific competition program.
- **APS Women in Physics groups:** University-level; not relevant to K-12 competition pipeline.

## Research Conclusions: What Could Help

German Physics Olympiad study (2022) conclusions:
- Interventions should address environmental cues that signal "physics is for men."
- Role modeling: Exposure to female physicists and female physics olympiad champions reduces stereotype endorsement.
- Belonging-focused interventions are more promising than self-concept interventions because belonging mediates the stereotype-performance relationship.
- Simply reducing gender salience before competitions could reduce stereotype threat activation.

The Springer Nature study (2022) on sense of belonging in physics olympiad found:
- Sense of belonging predicted success expectations beyond self-concept and interest.
- This suggests belonging is the proximate cause of dropout, with stereotype threat as the distal cause.
- Creating peer communities of female physics students (similar to what Mathcamp does for math) could be a high-leverage intervention.
