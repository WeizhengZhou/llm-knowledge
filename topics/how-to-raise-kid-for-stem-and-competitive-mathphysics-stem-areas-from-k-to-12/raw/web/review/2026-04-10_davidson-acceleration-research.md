# Research Findings on Acceleration Options for Gifted Students — Davidson Institute

## Types of Acceleration (12 Identified)

1. Early Entrance to School
2. Grade Skipping
3. Nongraded Classrooms
4. Curriculum Compaction
5. Grade Telescoping
6. Concurrent Enrollment
7. Subject Acceleration
8. Advanced Placement
9. Mentorship
10. Credit by Examination
11. Early Admission to College
12. Combined Accelerative Options

## Academic Outcomes — Effect Sizes

The research synthesized 314 studies (1912-1988), with 81 providing sufficient data for effect size calculations.

**Significant academic gains found for all but three options**: Concurrent Enrollment, Advanced Placement, and Combined Accelerative Options.

**Effect sizes by acceleration type:**
- Curriculum Compaction: 1.48
- Subject Acceleration: 1.02 (also strong psychological effect: ES = +0.58)
- Grade Skipping: 0.78 (also significant positive socialization: ES = 0.46)
- Credit by Examination: 0.75

**By grade level**, academic benefits decreased with age:
- Primary (ES = 0.64)
- Intermediate (ES = 0.59)
- Junior High (ES = 0.34)
- Senior High (ES = 0.31)

## Social and Emotional Outcomes

Contrary to common parental concerns: "no substantial positive or negative socialization and psychological effect differences for most forms of acceleration."

Two exceptions with positive effects:
- **Grade Skipping**: significant positive socialization effects (ES = 0.46)
- **Mentorship**: both positive socialization (ES = 0.50) and psychological adjustment (ES = 0.48)
- **Concurrent Enrollment**: also showed strong psychological adjustment benefits (ES = 0.74)

## Long-term Findings

- Accelerated students have few regrets; many wish they had more acceleration
- Overwhelming majority described acceleration as "an excellent experience"
- Accelerated students outperform nonaccelerated peers academically in the long term
- 35-year longitudinal study: "neither social nor emotional development is compromised" when acceleration matches student's pace of assimilation

## Expert Recommendation

Best practices combine both acceleration and enrichment:
- Enrichment builds depth and context
- Acceleration ensures the pace matches the child's ability
- Most successful gifted programs incorporate both approaches

## Key Finding

The study challenged the misconception that acceleration primarily means grade skipping. It identified 12 forms of acceleration and refuted beliefs about inevitable social-emotional harm, offering educators a research-supported menu of options for academically gifted learners.

## Enrichment Outcomes (from NAGC)

Gifted and talented students from enriched classes do better on grade equivalent scales (4-5 months better than conventional class peers).
