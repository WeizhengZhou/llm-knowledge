## Beast Academy vs. Singapore Math vs. AoPS (Elementary Level)

**Theme: Let the child lead in early years**

Parents consistently report that Beast Academy generates spontaneous enthusiasm in gifted elementary students. A common theme: children asking for "the next book" rather than being told to do math.

- "He begged me for each one after he was done with the other...He literally spent 5 minutes jumping up and down and screaming 'I love this one!'" (parent of a 7-year-old)
- Singapore Math is praised for foundations but noted as potentially boring when added on top of school math ("too textbook-based")
- Beast Academy is described as a better entry point specifically because it doesn't feel like extra work

**Theme: Supplemental work vs. replacement work**

Critical parent insight from multiple threads: gifted students who view extra math as punishment (added after finishing school assignments) will deliberately slow down schoolwork to avoid it. The principle articulated:

**"Advanced work *instead of* and not *after* was key."**

This suggests the replacement vs. add-on distinction matters enormously for gifted children's motivation.

**Theme: AoPS timing**

AoPS is consistently flagged as "QUITE challenging...probably much, much more so than most students are EVER likely to see." Multiple parents recommend:
- Beast Academy through grade 4-5, then transition to AoPS Prealgebra
- Don't start AoPS Introduction to Algebra before the student has fully mastered pre-algebra concepts
- Grade level is less important than readiness

## AoPS vs. Russian School of Math (RSM) — Parent Perspectives

**AoPS strengths (from parents):**
- Produces the "vast majority" of state and national MATHCOUNTS qualifiers
- Focus is competition math — problem solving over drill
- Homework emphasizes conceptual understanding, not repetitive practice
- Now accredited; can be used to substitute for school math courses
- Alcumus software is free and provides adaptive practice
- Online format gives flexibility (classes, self-paced, books)

**RSM strengths (from parents):**
- Mastery-based Russian teaching methods
- Structured in-person format with weekly classes
- Placement based on ability assessment (not grade level) — younger advanced students placed appropriately
- More practice volume than AoPS ("more practice than the AoPS homework challenge questions")
- Serves a broader population beyond just competition-focused students

**Key differentiator identified by parents:**
- AoPS targets gifted students pursuing competition math
- RSM caters to "high average to moderately gifted kids" preparing for standardized testing and AP Calculus readiness
- These are different audiences; the programs are not direct competitors for the same student

**Concerns raised about RSM:**
- Occasional teacher quality inconsistencies (one parent reported a teacher making errors on rate problems)
- May not develop problem-solving skills at competitive levels
- Some RSM staff reportedly unfamiliar with AoPS materials

**Parent recommendation pattern:**
- For serious competition math students: AoPS first, RSM as optional supplement for more drill practice
- For general math enrichment and strong school performance: RSM may be better fit
- For students who want both: some families use RSM for structure/foundation + AoPS books for competition prep

## Common Parent Wisdom on Competition Math Journey

**On starting age:**
- Don't rush formal competition prep; playful math exposure in K-3 matters more than starting AMC prep early
- Most successful national competitors started organized competition prep in grades 5-7, not earlier
- Parents who pushed hard in grades 1-3 often report burnout by grades 7-9

**On motivation vs. achievement:**
- "The students who do best are the ones who can't stop talking about math problems at dinner" — a common observation among competition math coaches cited in parent discussions
- Extrinsic motivation (parent pressure, grades) correlates with burnout; intrinsic motivation correlates with long-term progress

**On tutors and coaching:**
- Parents of USAMO qualifiers rarely describe hiring tutors early; most describe facilitating access to programs (AoPS classes, math circles) and providing encouragement
- Private coaching becomes more relevant at AIME+ level when students need feedback on proof-writing

**On balance:**
- Many threads contain parents expressing regret about over-structuring elementary math
- Common positive pattern: unstructured exploration in elementary → structured competition prep starting grade 5-6 → intensive work grade 8-10 if talent warrants

**On RSM/Kumon drill:**
- Clear community consensus: drill programs (Kumon, Mathnasium) do not produce competition math competitors; they serve a different function (building automaticity for arithmetic)
- Some families use Kumon in early elementary for fluency, then switch to problem-solving-focused programs by grade 3-4

## AoPS Community Forum Themes (competition.math category)

From AoPS forums (from search result summaries, not direct fetch):
- MATHCOUNTS preparation typically begins in grade 6 with AoPS Prealgebra + handbooks
- The transition from AMC 8 to AMC 10 is described as a significant difficulty jump
- AMC 10 → AIME qualification requires "leaving school math behind" and developing genuine problem-solving skills
- AIME → USAMO is considered the hardest step — requires proof writing which most students have never practiced
- Peer community is consistently cited as essential: students who join math circles or attend PROMYS/Mathcamp show dramatically faster growth
