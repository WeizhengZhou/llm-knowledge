# Enrichment Programs and Training Providers Guide

## Overview

This guide compares the major enrichment programs and training providers for competitive STEM students, from elementary curricula through olympiad-level coaching. Programs are organized by level and purpose.

## Elementary Curricula (Grades K-5)

### Beast Academy (AoPS)

Beast Academy covers grades 2-5 (Levels 2A-5D) in a comic-style format, available as online subscription or physical books. See [[cost-and-financial-aid]] for current pricing.

**Best for:** Gifted elementary students who need engaging, challenging material beyond school math. The comic-style format makes it feel like entertainment, not homework.

> **Epistemic note:** The following reflects patterns across multiple parent accounts
> (Davidson Gifted Forum, AoPS Forums, 2024-2026). Individual experiences vary. Official sources do not
> confirm or deny. Treat as directional signal, not verified fact.

Some parents on gifted education forums describe Beast Academy as generating spontaneous enthusiasm in gifted elementary students, with children asking for "the next book" rather than being told to do math.

### Singapore Math

Singapore Math uses the Standards Edition most commonly in the US. See [[cost-and-financial-aid]] for current pricing.

**Best for:** Building strong number sense and systematic problem-solving through the Concrete-Pictorial-Abstract approach. The bar model method is particularly powerful for word problems.

### Kumon / Mathnasium

Kumon uses a worksheet-based drill approach. Pricing varies significantly by location; see [[cost-and-financial-aid]] for approximate ranges.

**Best for:** Building arithmetic fluency and automaticity. These serve a different purpose from competition math preparation.

Some parents on gifted education forums report that drill programs (Kumon, Mathnasium) serve a different function from competition math preparation, building automaticity for arithmetic rather than problem-solving skills. Some families report using Kumon in early elementary for fluency, then switching to problem-solving-focused programs by grades 3-4.

## Competition Training Providers (Grades 5-12)

### AoPS (Art of Problem Solving)

See [[aops-art-of-problem-solving]] for full entity profile.

AoPS Online School is accredited by the Western Association of Schools and Colleges (ACS WASC). Most AoPS subject classes are NCAA approved, and two courses (Calculus and Physics 1: Mechanics) are College Board-approved AP courses. See [[cost-and-financial-aid]] for current course pricing.

According to AoPS, Alcumus is a free, adaptive online math practice tool.

**Best for:** Students pursuing competition math at any level. The books, online courses, and community forums form a comprehensive ecosystem.

### AwesomeMath

AwesomeMath offers online summer sessions (June-August) with a competition-prep focus (AMC/AIME/USAMO). International students are eligible. See [[cost-and-financial-aid]] for current pricing.

**Best for:** Intensive competition-specific preparation during summer months.

### OTIS (Olympiad Training for Individual Study)

According to his website, Evan Chen's OTIS (Olympiad Training for Individual Study) is a structured olympiad math training program targeting AIME and USAMO level students. Chen is a USAMO winner and IMO gold medalist.

**Best for:** Serious olympiad aspirants at the AIME+ level who need structured proof-writing training and problem sets.

### Expii / Po-Shen Loh Platforms

According to Expii.com, it is a free personalized learning platform founded by Po-Shen Loh, targeting middle school level (grades 5-9), aligned with MATHCOUNTS and AMC 8, covering algebra, geometry, combinatorics, number theory, and probability.

According to Expii.com, Po-Shen Loh is a professor at Carnegie Mellon University and served as the US IMO Team Coach, leading the US team to first place at the IMO in 2015 (for the first time in 21 years), 2016, and 2018.

According to a review, live.poshenloh.com offers math classes taught by student teachers (high school/college competition math participants) via live streaming, with classes running 60-90 minutes covering MATHCOUNTS through AMC 10/12 level content.

According to Expii.com, Po-Shen Loh's stated goal as IMO coach was to "maximize 20-year future outcomes" for students, not to maximize current wins. He has stated that competition math should develop curiosity and deep thinking, not just problem-solving speed.

**Best for:** Free or low-cost entry point for middle school students starting competition math.

### RSM (Russian School of Math)

> **Epistemic note:** The following reflects patterns across multiple parent accounts
> (Davidson Gifted Forum, AoPS Forums, 2024-2026). Individual experiences vary. Official sources do not
> confirm or deny. Treat as directional signal, not verified fact.

Some parents on gifted education forums describe RSM (Russian School of Math) as using mastery-based methods with structured in-person weekly classes and ability-based placement, with more practice volume than AoPS-style homework.

Some parents on gifted education forums characterize AoPS as targeting gifted students pursuing competition math, while RSM is described as catering to "high average to moderately gifted kids" preparing for standardized testing and AP Calculus readiness.

**Best for:** Students who benefit from structured, in-person instruction and want strong school math performance. Less competition-focused than AoPS.

## Comparison: When to Use What

| Student Profile | Recommended Programs |
|----------------|---------------------|
| Gifted K-2 wanting enrichment | Beast Academy Level 2+ |
| Elementary wanting foundations | Singapore Math + Beast Academy |
| Elementary wanting fluency drill | Kumon (switch to problem-solving by grade 3-4) |
| Middle school starting competitions | AoPS Introduction series + Expii (free) |
| Middle school MATHCOUNTS prep | AoPS courses + MATHCOUNTS handbooks |
| High school AMC/AIME level | AoPS Intermediate + WOOT |
| High school USAMO level | OTIS + AwesomeMath summer |
| Wanting structured in-person classes | RSM |
| Budget-constrained | Alcumus (free) + Expii (free) + library AoPS books |

## When Does a Student Need a Private Coach?

> **Epistemic note:** The following reflects patterns across multiple parent accounts
> (Davidson Gifted Forum, AoPS Forums, 2024-2026). Individual experiences vary. Official sources do not
> confirm or deny. Treat as directional signal, not verified fact.

Some parents on gifted education forums report that extrinsic motivation (parent pressure, grades) is associated with burnout, while intrinsic motivation is associated with long-term progress. Parents of USAMO qualifiers on these forums rarely describe hiring tutors early; most describe facilitating access to programs such as AoPS classes and math circles.

Private coaching becomes most relevant at the AIME+ level, where students need personalized feedback on proof writing. Before that level, structured programs and self-study with AoPS books are generally sufficient.

## Common Mistakes

- **Starting AoPS courses before readiness.** Complete Beast Academy or equivalent first. AoPS Prealgebra assumes strong arithmetic fluency.
- **Stacking multiple programs simultaneously.** One curriculum plus one competition activity is usually sufficient. More leads to [[burnout-prevention|burnout]].
- **Choosing programs based on parent peer pressure.** Match the program to the child's level and learning style, not to what other families are doing.
- **Ignoring free resources.** Alcumus, Expii, past AMC tests, and MATHCOUNTS handbooks are all free and high-quality.
