# Summer Math Programs Comparison Guide

## Overview

Selective summer math programs are a critical accelerator for competitive STEM students. They provide intensive mathematical immersion, peer community, and mentorship that are difficult to replicate during the school year. This guide compares the major programs by selectivity, cost, format, and fit.

## Prerequisites

- For most programs: completion of at least AoPS Introduction series or equivalent; AMC 10 score of 100+ or AIME qualification.
- For RSI: rising senior with strong academic record and research interest.
- For MathPath: ages 11-14 with demonstrated mathematical maturity.

## Detailed Profiles

### Ross Mathematics Program

Ross Mathematics Program 2026 runs 6 weeks (June 14 - July 24) at two simultaneous sites: Otterbein University (Columbus, OH) and Rose-Hulman Institute of Technology (Terre Haute, IN). The 2026 fee is $7,500.

According to Ross Program materials, the acceptance rate is approximately 15% (based on 2023 data), with approximately 60 first-year students accepted per site and 500+ applicants per cycle (both sites combined). Most students are ages 15-18.

**Pedagogy:** Ross uses a "think deeply about simple things" philosophy centered on number theory. Students work through carefully sequenced problem sets (not lectures) and are expected to develop proofs independently. See [[ross-program]] for full details.

According to Ross Program materials, sponsors include the American Mathematical Society, Jane Street, and Citadel.

### PROMYS

According to PROMYS, the 2026 PROMYS program costs up to $7,000 for 6 weeks at Boston University (June 28 - August 8, 2026). It is free for domestic students from families earning under $80,000/year, with need-based financial aid available at all income levels. The 2025 cost was $6,100.

**Pedagogy:** Similar to Ross — number theory-focused, problem-set-driven, emphasis on independent discovery. Founded by former Ross counselors. See [[promys]] for full details.

### Canada/USA Mathcamp

Canada/USA Mathcamp 2026 costs $7,500 (tuition, room, board, local transport) and runs 5 weeks (June 28 - August 2) at Champlain College in Burlington, Vermont. It is free for US/Canadian households earning under $100,000 annually. There is no application fee. The program serves students ages 13-18.

**Pedagogy:** Broader curriculum than Ross/PROMYS — students choose from many short courses covering diverse mathematical topics. More flexible, less focused on a single thread. See [[canada-usa-mathcamp]] for full details.

### HCSSiM

HCSSiM (Hampshire College Summer Studies in Mathematics) 2026 runs 6 weeks (June 28 - August 8) at Hampshire College in Amherst, MA. This is the 54th session. The 2026 tuition is $7,208, covering tuition, room, meals, local transport, and recreational activities.

According to HCSSiM, the daily schedule includes 4 hours of morning classes (Monday through Saturday) and 3-hour evening problem sessions, totaling approximately 7+ hours of active mathematics per day, 6 days per week. There are no hard age or grade cutoffs.

According to HCSSiM, the application is free and includes a letter of application, a sponsor letter, and the "Interesting Test" (a non-standard math problem set sent in March). Decisions are rolling, with a deadline for full consideration in late April.

### RSI (Research Science Institute)

According to the Center for Excellence in Education, RSI (Research Science Institute) is hosted at MIT and is a free 6-week program (tuition, room, and board all covered). RSI receives over 3,100 applications per year and accepts approximately 100 students (roughly 60-70 domestic), for an acceptance rate of approximately 2.5-3%. The application deadline is in December.

**Unique feature:** RSI pairs students with research mentors at MIT and Harvard for a 5-week research project. It is the only major summer math/science program that is completely free. See [[research-science-institute]] for full details.

### MOP (Mathematical Olympiad Program)

According to MAA and AoPS sources, approximately 60-80 students are invited annually to MOP, including top USAMO and USAJMO scorers, additional female students for EGMO selection, and some international participants. MOP is free and by invitation only.

### Other Programs

AdmissionsAngle lists MathPath (middle school program) 2025 cost at $6,300 (financial aid available), with dates June 29 - July 27, 2025, for ages 11-14, and an approximate acceptance rate of 30%.

AdmissionsAngle lists MathILy 2025 cost at $5,300 (financial aid available), running approximately 5 weeks with a curriculum covering discrete math, combinatorics, graph theory, geometry, and topology. The 2025 application deadline was April 29.

AdmissionsAngle lists SUMaC (Stanford University Mathematics Camp) 2025 residential cost at $8,575, with dates June 22 - July 18, 2025, and programs in Abstract Algebra & Number Theory (I) or Algebraic Topology (II). Financial aid is available. The 2025 application deadline was February 3.

AdmissionsAngle lists AwesomeMath online summer program 2025 at $1,275-$1,575 per course, with three concurrent sessions (June-August 2025) and a competition-prep focus (AMC/AIME/USAMO). International students are eligible.

## Steps

### Step 1: Determine Readiness
Most selective programs expect AMC 10 score of 100+ or AIME qualification. MathPath accepts younger students with lower thresholds.

### Step 2: Apply Early
Deadlines are typically January-March. RSI deadline is December. Start application essays and recommendation letters in the fall.

### Step 3: Apply to Multiple Programs
Acceptance rates are 15-30% for most programs (2.5-3% for RSI). Apply to 2-3 programs to improve odds.

### Step 4: Secure Financial Aid if Needed
All major programs offer need-based aid. PROMYS and Mathcamp are free for families below income thresholds. RSI is free for all accepted students. See [[cost-and-financial-aid]].

## Private Coach vs. Self-Study vs. Group Classes

A common RO4 question is: when does a student need a private coach instead of self-study or group programs?

- **Self-study (with AoPS books + past tests):** Sufficient for most students through AMC 10/12 level. The combination of a textbook, Alcumus (free), and past competition problems covers the material. Self-study develops the independent problem-solving habit that competitions reward.
- **Group classes (AoPS online courses, AwesomeMath, math circles):** Add value when the student needs instructor feedback, peer discussion, or structured pacing. Most effective at the Introduction through Intermediate level (AMC 10/12 → AIME). See [[enrichment-programs-guide]] for program comparisons.
- **Private coaching:** Becomes most relevant at AIME+ level, where students need personalized feedback on proof writing — a skill that self-study and group courses address less effectively. Before AIME level, private tutoring rarely outperforms self-study with AoPS books for well-motivated students.

The key decision rule: if the student is progressing steadily with self-study, do not add coaching. If the student is stuck at a plateau (same AMC score range for 2+ years) or needs proof-writing feedback, that is when structured coaching or a group class adds value. See [[enrichment-programs-guide]] for the "When Does a Student Need a Private Coach?" section.

## Common Mistakes

- **Applying to only one program.** Even strong students get rejected. Apply broadly.
- **Waiting until junior year.** Many students attend Ross, PROMYS, or Mathcamp as rising 10th graders and return for a second year.
- **Underestimating the social component.** The peer community at these programs is often as valuable as the curriculum.
- **Choosing based on prestige alone.** Ross/PROMYS suit depth-oriented learners; Mathcamp suits breadth-oriented learners. Fit matters more than ranking.
