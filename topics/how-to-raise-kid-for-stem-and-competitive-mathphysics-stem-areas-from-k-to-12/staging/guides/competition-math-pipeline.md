# Competition Math Pipeline: AMC to IMO

## Overview

The American Mathematics Competitions (AMC) pipeline is the primary pathway for US students from initial math competitions to the International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO). The AMC competition pipeline proceeds: AMC 8 -> AMC 10/12 -> AIME -> USAMO/USAJMO -> MOP -> IMO.

This guide details each stage, including format, qualification thresholds, and key dates.

## Prerequisites

- Familiarity with [[competition-math-vs-school-math|how competition math differs from school math]].
- For AMC 8: strong arithmetic and basic problem-solving skills (see [[k12-stem-roadmap|Grade 6-8 section]]).
- For AMC 10/12: completion of AoPS Introduction series or equivalent.

## The Pipeline

### Stage 1: AMC 8

The AMC series comprises three competitions: AMC 8 (grade 8 and below, maximum age under 15.5), AMC 10 (grade 10 and below, maximum age under 17.5), and AMC 12 (grade 12 and below, maximum age under 19.5).

AMC 8 is a 25-question, 40-minute multiple-choice competition.

**Format:** Multiple-choice; no penalty for wrong answers. Covers pre-algebra, basic geometry, basic counting, and basic number theory.

**2025-2026 dates:** For the 2025-2026 cycle, the AMC 8 competition window was January 22-30, 2026.

**What it measures:** Whether the student can solve problems requiring insight, not just computation. A score of 20+ suggests readiness for AMC 10 preparation.

### Stage 2: AMC 10 and AMC 12

AMC 10 and AMC 12 are each 25-question, 75-minute multiple-choice tests.

AMC 12 covers the full high school mathematics curriculum including trigonometry, advanced algebra, and advanced geometry; calculus is excluded.

**2025-2026 dates:** For the 2025-2026 cycle, AMC 10 A and AMC 12 A were held November 5, 2025; AMC 10 B and AMC 12 B were held November 13, 2025.

**Scoring:** 6 points per correct answer, 1.5 points for blank, 0 for incorrect. Maximum score: 150.

**Key change:** For 2025-2026 onward, the former AIME qualification score caps of 120 (AMC 10) and 100 (AMC 12) were removed; cutoffs can theoretically reach 150.

### Stage 3: AIME (American Invitational Mathematics Examination)

AIME qualification requires finishing in the top 2.5% of AMC 10 participants or the top 5% of AMC 12 participants; approximately 6,000-7,000 students qualify annually.

As of August 2025, international students no longer affect AIME cutoffs.

The AIME is a 15-question, 3-hour exam with integer answers in the range 000-999.

**2025-2026 dates:** For the 2025-2026 cycle, AIME I was held February 5, 2026; AIME II was held February 11, 2026.

**Key rule:** According to ThinkAcademy, students may take the AIME only once per cycle; if they took both A-date and B-date AMC exams, both AMC scores are matched with the single AIME score and the higher qualifying index is used.

**Alternative qualification:** Strong USAMTS performance is one pathway to qualify for the AIME as an alternative to AMC 10/12. According to AwesomeMath, a score of approximately 68/75 or higher is needed. See [[entities/usamts|USAMTS]].

### Stage 4: USAMO / USAJMO

Approximately the top 11% of all AIME participants are selected for USAMO or USAJMO, totaling roughly 500 students.

According to ThinkAcademy, USAJMO qualification is based on AMC 10 + AIME scores, while USAMO qualification is based on AMC 12 + AIME scores. A student who qualifies for both is invited to USAMO.

**Qualifying Index:** For 2025-2026 onward, the USAMO/USAJMO qualifying index formula is (AIME score x 20) + AMC score. The previous formula (through 2024-2025) was (AIME score x 10) + AMC score.

**Format:** According to ThinkAcademy, the 2026 USAMO and USAJMO were held March 21-22, 2026, in 4.5-hour sessions with three problems per day. Problems require full mathematical proofs.

**Historical cutoffs (old formula):** Under the old formula (AIME x 10 + AMC), USAMO/USAJMO historical cutoffs were: 2024-2025 USAMO 237 (12A) / 249.5 (12B), USAJMO 233 (10A) / 243 (10B); 2023-2024 USAMO 242 (12A) / 251.5 (12B), USAJMO 233.5 (10A) / 241 (10B).

**Inclusivity:** According to ThinkAcademy, the MAA also invites top-scoring girl-identifying AMC students to the USAMO and USAJMO each year as part of its commitment to inclusivity.

### Stage 5: MOP (Mathematical Olympiad Program)

According to MAA and AoPS sources, approximately 60-80 students are invited annually to MOP, including top USAMO and USAJMO scorers, additional female students for EGMO selection, and some international participants.

According to AwesomeMath, MOP (Mathematical Olympiad Program) is held in June for top USAMO and USAJMO performers. AdmissionsAngle describes MOP as extremely selective and free. According to MAA and AoPS sources, approximately 60-80 students are invited annually (including additional female students for EGMO selection and some international participants). MOP has been held at various locations including Carnegie Mellon University and IMSA.

### Stage 6: IMO (International Mathematical Olympiad)

According to AwesomeMath, the International Mathematical Olympiad (IMO) is held in July; the US sends a team of 6 students; it is a two-day competition with six problems.

## The Funnel: By the Numbers

Approximately 300,000 students participate in AMC 10/12 annually; roughly 10,000 advance to AIME; approximately 500 qualify for USAMO/USAJMO combined. Fewer than 0.2% of initial AMC participants ultimately qualify for USAMO.

| Stage | Approximate Participants | Advancement Rate |
|-------|------------------------|-----------------|
| AMC 10/12 | ~300,000 | — |
| AIME | ~6,000-10,000 | Top 2.5% (AMC 10) / 5% (AMC 12) |
| USAMO/USAJMO | ~500 | Top ~11% of AIME |
| MOP | ~60-80 | Top USAMO/USAJMO performers |
| IMO Team | 6 | Selected from MOP |

## Common Mistakes

- **Focusing on AMC score maximization over mathematical understanding.** AMC scores are a means, not an end.
- **Neglecting proof writing until USAMO.** Start proof practice with [[entities/usamts|USAMTS]] in middle school.
- **Ignoring the new qualifying index formula.** The 2025-2026 change to (AIME x 20 + AMC) significantly increases the weight of the AIME score.
- **Taking both AIME dates.** Students may take the AIME only once per cycle.
