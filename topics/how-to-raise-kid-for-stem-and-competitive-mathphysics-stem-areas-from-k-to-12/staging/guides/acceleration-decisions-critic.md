## Must-Fix Before wiki/ Graduation

1. **RO5 must_answer item "What does research say about outcomes for accelerated vs. enriched students?" is partially unmet.** The article covers acceleration outcomes (SMPY longitudinal study, effect sizes for subject acceleration and grade skipping) but contains no information about enrichment-only outcomes for comparison. A parent choosing between "accelerate my child in math" and "keep grade level but add competition enrichment" cannot make this decision from this article. One paragraph — even a single attributed sentence from Davidson Institute comparing acceleration and enrichment outcomes, or explicitly acknowledging the research gap — is required to complete this must_answer item. Even a note that "the SMPY study did not systematically compare enrichment-only controls" would answer the question honestly.

## Should-Fix

1. **The caution note on SMPY research methodology is buried.** The article body includes a correct caution: "The SMPY study tracked self-selected participants. Acceleration effect sizes reflect populations that chose acceleration, not random assignment." This is the right epistemic note but it appears as a small indented block after the positive findings. Readers who skim will absorb the positive claims and miss the caution. Moving the caution to a more prominent position — or converting it to a note block formatted like the L4 epistemic notes — would ensure it is not missed.

2. **The magnet school section does not say how to apply for IMSA or TJHSST.** These are listed with descriptions but no application guidance. A P2 parent (grades 3-8) reading about IMSA would want to know: "Does my child need to start preparing now?" Adding one sentence per school — e.g., "IMSA admits for grade 10 entry; students typically begin preparation in grades 8-9" — would make this section actionable rather than purely descriptive.

3. **No cross-link to `is-this-right-for-my-child.md` for social-emotional readiness.** The article mentions social-emotional readiness in the steps section but has no wikilink to the article that most directly covers this topic. Adding `[[is-this-right-for-my-child]]` in Step 1 would connect the decision process to the aptitude framework.
