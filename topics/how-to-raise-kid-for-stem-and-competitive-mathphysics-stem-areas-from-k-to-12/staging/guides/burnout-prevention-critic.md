## Should-Fix

1. **"How do I avoid being an overbearing STEM parent?" could be strengthened beyond behavioral lists.** The "Parent Behaviors to Avoid" list gives eight specific actions to stop. This is good. But parents who are already doing these things often do not recognize themselves in the list. A brief paragraph on the underlying psychology — e.g., "Parents most at risk of over-involvement are often high achievers themselves who are reliving their own mathematical journey vicariously. If you notice that your child's competition results affect your own mood, that is the warning sign — not your behavior, but your emotional investment" — would give the parent a self-diagnostic that the behavioral list alone cannot provide. This could be attributed as an L4 pattern from forum discussions.

2. **The burnout statistic (83%) would benefit from a parenthetical directing P4 parents to apply their own judgment.** The article correctly notes that the "original survey methodology, sample size, and recruitment method are not specified." But this caveat may actually undermine the article's purpose for P4 parents who are already skeptical of competitive STEM. Adding a sentence like: "Even setting aside the unverified methodology, the prevalence of burnout as a topic in gifted education communities suggests it is common enough to warrant active prevention, regardless of precise rates" — would help P4 parents calibrate rather than either dismissing or catastrophizing the statistic.

3. **The "Recommended approach" for when a child wants to quit has no timeline guidance.** The article says "Offer to step back from formal competitions while keeping mathematical enrichment available." A parent who does this for one month and sees no improvement may wonder when to change course. Adding a rough timeline — "Most students who step back from formal competition for 6-12 months either return voluntarily or find another genuine passion; this timeline is not a failure" — would make the recommendation more actionable.

## Graduation Decision: READY

No must-fix items. Three should-fix items are enhancements that strengthen the article for P4 persona parents specifically but do not block graduation.
