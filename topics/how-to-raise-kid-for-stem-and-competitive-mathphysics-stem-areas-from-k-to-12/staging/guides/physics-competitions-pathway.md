# Physics Competitions Pathway: F=ma to IPhO

## Overview

The US physics competition pipeline proceeds: F=ma exam -> USAPhO semifinal -> US Physics Team Training Camp (University of Maryland) -> IPhO team (top 5 students).

This pathway is administered by the American Association of Physics Teachers (AAPT) and is shorter than the [[competition-math-pipeline|math competition pipeline]] — most students begin in grade 10 or 11 and can reach the national level within 1-2 years.

## Prerequisites

- Strong algebra and trigonometry skills (typically AMC 10 level math or higher).
- Completion of an algebra-based physics course (AP Physics 1 level).
- For USAPhO: calculus-based mechanics is strongly recommended.

## The Pipeline

### Stage 1: F=ma Exam

The F=ma exam is a 75-minute, 25 multiple-choice question exam focusing exclusively on classical mechanics.

**2026 date:** The 2026 F=ma exam was held February 12, 2026.

**Eligibility:** According to AAPT, F=ma eligibility includes US citizens, permanent residents, or students currently attending US schools who are physically located in the US during testing. USAPhO requires US citizenship or permanent residency.

**Qualification:** The F=ma qualifying score for USAPhO typically ranges from 14 to 18 out of 25; the 2025 cutoff was 15. Approximately 400 students advance to USAPhO.

**Preparation:** The key preparation resource is *Problems and Solutions in Introductory Mechanics* by David Morin. Past F=ma exams are available free from AAPT.

### Stage 2: USAPhO (US Physics Olympiad Semifinal)

The USAPhO semifinal is a 3-hour free-response exam divided into two 90-minute sections, covering all introductory physics topics including mechanics, electromagnetism, thermodynamics, relativity, nuclear/atomic/particle physics, waves, optics, and data analysis.

**Medal distribution:** According to AAPT, USAPhO medal distribution is approximately: Gold top 10-12%, Silver next 14-16%, Bronze next 20-22%, Honorable Mention next 24-26%. Cutoffs vary annually.

**Preparation:** Key books include:
- *Introduction to Mechanics* (Kleppner and Kolenkow) — MIT standard
- *Electricity and Magnetism* (Purcell and Morin)
- *Halliday/Resnick/Krane Physics* — comprehensive reference
- Past USAPhO exams (free from AAPT)

### Stage 3: US Physics Team Training Camp

Approximately 20 students are selected for the US Physics Team Training Camp, a 10-day camp at the University of Maryland. The top 5 are selected to represent the US at the International Physics Olympiad.

### Stage 4: IPhO (International Physics Olympiad)

The 56th International Physics Olympiad is scheduled for Bucaramanga, Colombia, July 4-12, 2026.

### Other Physics Competitions

According to AAPT, the Physics Bowl is a separate annual physics competition administered by AAPT, held March-April each year with 40 multiple-choice questions. This is a good warm-up competition that is less selective than F=ma.

## Steps

### Step 1: Build Mechanics Foundation (Grade 9-10)
Complete an algebra-based physics course. Start working through David Morin's problems book. Take the Physics Bowl for practice.

### Step 2: Attempt F=ma (Grade 10-11)
Register through your school's physics department or local AAPT section. Target score: 15+ to advance.

### Step 3: Prepare for USAPhO (if qualified)
Transition to calculus-based physics. Work through Kleppner and Kolenkow. Practice free-response writing — USAPhO problems require detailed solutions, not just answers.

### Step 4: Deepen for Training Camp Selection
Work through Irodov, Griffiths, and Kevin Zhou's handouts. This level requires university-level physics knowledge.

## Common Mistakes

- **Starting too late.** F=ma is in February; preparation should begin the previous summer at latest.
- **Underestimating the math requirements.** USAPhO problems require strong calculus skills. Concurrent [[competition-math-pipeline|math competition]] preparation helps.
- **Skipping free-response practice.** F=ma is multiple choice but USAPhO requires written solutions. Practice explaining your reasoning.
- **Not taking Physics Bowl first.** It provides useful calibration before F=ma.
