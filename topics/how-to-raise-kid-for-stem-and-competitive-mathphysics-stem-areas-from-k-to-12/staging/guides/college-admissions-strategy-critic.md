## Must-Fix Before wiki/ Graduation

1. **RO6 must_answer item "What is the right way to present competition achievements on college applications?" is not answered.** The article has one sentence: "List summer programs in the activities section with the acceptance rate or selectivity if appropriate." For competition math specifically, no guidance exists: Should an AMC score (without AIME qualification) be included? How should AIME scores be quantified so admissions officers understand their rarity? Should students mention specific AIME scores? The Step 4 paragraph ("Frame the Narrative") says to emphasize "why you love the subject" but does not say where in the application this narrative belongs (Common App essay, activities list, additional information section). This must_answer item requires at least one substantive paragraph giving specific framing guidance — even if properly attributed to admissions consultants as informed guidance, not official policy.

## Should-Fix

1. **Step 4 ("Frame the Narrative") is too abstract to be actionable.** "Applications should emphasize why you love the subject, not just what you achieved" is correct but offers no help with how to do this. Adding a concrete example — e.g., "Instead of 'I qualified for AIME with a score of 103.5,' write about the specific problem type that made you realize you loved number theory" — would make this step implementable. Admissions consultants regularly offer this kind of specific guidance and it could be attributed appropriately.

2. **Two generic Common Mistakes entries should be made specific.** "Overweighting competition scores" should be replaced with: "Applying only to MIT and Caltech as STEM reaches because AIME qualification seems exceptional — AIME qualifiers still face less than 10% admission rates at these schools, and the application needs a complete narrative beyond the math achievements." "Neglecting the personal essay" should be replaced with: "Writing an activities list that only names competitions and scores, with no essay content explaining the intellectual journey — admissions officers at MIT have stated that they look for evidence of intellectual curiosity beyond the credential list."

3. **The "What Is NOT Known" section is valuable but understated.** Currently it appears as a three-bullet list. The fact that no university publishes competition-specific acceptance rates is a point that RO6 parents need to fully absorb before reading the rest of the article. Moving this section to appear immediately after the "Important caveat" paragraph in the Overview — before the "What Is Known" section — would ensure parents calibrate their expectations before reading the attributed estimates.

## Graduation Decision: NEEDS-REVISION

One must-fix: the application presentation guidance is absent. The revision requires one specific paragraph on how to present competition achievements across different parts of a college application.
