## Overview

Ross Mathematics Program 2026 runs 6 weeks (June 14 - July 24) at two simultaneous sites: Otterbein University (Columbus, OH) and Rose-Hulman Institute of Technology (Terre Haute, IN). The 2026 fee is $7,500.

The Ross Program uses a "think deeply about simple things" philosophy. Students work through carefully sequenced problem sets centered on number theory, developing proofs independently with minimal lecturing. The emphasis is on mathematical maturity and independent discovery, not competition preparation directly.

## Selectivity

According to Ross Program materials, the acceptance rate is approximately 15% (based on 2023 data), with approximately 60 first-year students accepted per site and 500+ applicants per cycle (both sites combined). Most students are ages 15-18.

## Financial Aid

Financial aid is available. According to Ross Program materials, sponsors include the American Mathematical Society, Jane Street, and Citadel.

## Who It Is For

Ross is best suited for students who love depth over breadth — spending weeks on a single mathematical thread rather than sampling many topics. Students should be comfortable with independent work and productive struggle. Compare with [[canada-usa-mathcamp|Mathcamp]] (broader curriculum, more student choice) and [[promys|PROMYS]] (similar philosophy, Boston location).

## Common Mistakes

- **Applying without taking the qualifying problem set seriously.** Ross admission is primarily based on a qualifying problem set (available on rossprogram.org in late winter/early spring), not on transcripts, competition scores, or essays. Students who treat it as a standard application and submit a weak problem set will not be competitive, regardless of their AMC/AIME scores.
- **Choosing Ross for a student who wants topic breadth.** Ross's "think deeply about simple things" philosophy means students spend the entire six weeks on number theory. Students expecting a broad math curriculum covering geometry, combinatorics, and analysis will be surprised and may not thrive. For breadth-oriented students, see [[canada-usa-mathcamp|Mathcamp]].
- **Applying only to Ross when PROMYS and Mathcamp have similar selectivity.** A student who applies only to Ross has approximately a 15% acceptance chance. Applying to two or three comparable programs ([[promys|PROMYS]], [[canada-usa-mathcamp|Mathcamp]]) significantly improves the odds of attending at least one.
