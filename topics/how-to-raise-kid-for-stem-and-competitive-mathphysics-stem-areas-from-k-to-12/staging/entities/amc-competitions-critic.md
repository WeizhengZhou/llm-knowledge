# Critic Report — AMC Competitions (AMC 8, AMC 10, AMC 12) — 2026-04-10

## Overall Assessment

This entity article is a reference document for the AMC competition family, including AIME qualification, ARML, USAMTS, and Math Kangaroo. It is factually solid with L1 official sources throughout. Its scope is broader than a pure AMC entity article — it also covers ARML and USAMTS, which arguably should be separate entity articles. The article lacks a Common Mistakes section and any decision framing for parents choosing between AMC levels. The L4 community patterns from the fact-sheet relevant to AMC (forum descriptions of the AMC 8 to AMC 10 difficulty jump) are not present.

## Must-Fix Before wiki/ Graduation

1. **No Common Mistakes section.** Three specific mistakes identified: (a) taking AMC 12 as a 10th grader aiming for USAJMO — USAJMO uses AMC 10 scores, so students targeting USAJMO should take AMC 10 through 10th grade; (b) missing the separate AIME registration step after qualifying — many students (and parents) do not realize AIME is not automatic; (c) school-registration dependency — AMC competitions are administered through schools; students without a school willing to proctor must find an alternative site, and many parents only discover this problem in November when registration is closing.

2. **L4 synthesis is absent despite applicable community data in the fact-sheet.** The c124 claim (AIME-to-USAMO difficulty step, proof writing, peer community importance) is specifically about the AMC pipeline and belongs in this article. Add a "What Participants Observe" section with the epistemic note block at the appropriate confidence level.

## Should-Fix

1. **ARML section is oversized relative to its relevance to this article.** ARML gets more text than AMC 12. Consider collapsing ARML to two sentences and a wikilink if a separate ARML entity article exists or is planned. If no separate article is planned, the current coverage is acceptable but the formatting (four separate bullet paragraphs) should match the lighter coverage in other "Related Competitions" entries.

2. **Decision point for AMC 10 vs. AMC 12 is absent.** A 10th-grade student debating which exam to take cannot determine the answer from this article. One sentence — "Students in grades 9-10 should generally take AMC 10; students in grades 11-12 should take AMC 12. A student targeting USAJMO should take AMC 10 through grade 10" — would fill the most common parent and student decision gap.

3. **The USAMTS alternative AIME qualification threshold ("approximately 68/75 or higher") is attributed to AwesomeMath (L3) without noting it may vary by year.** Adding "approximate; threshold may change" prevents a student from treating this as a hard cutoff.

## Graduation Decision: NEEDS-REVISION

Two must-fix items: missing Common Mistakes section and absent L4 synthesis despite applicable community data in the fact-sheet. Both are additive revisions.
