## Overview

Canada/USA Mathcamp 2026 costs $7,500 (tuition, room, board, local transport) and runs 5 weeks (June 28 - August 2) at Champlain College in Burlington, Vermont. It is free for US/Canadian households earning under $100,000 annually. There is no application fee. The program serves students ages 13-18.

## How It Differs from Ross/PROMYS

Mathcamp offers a broader curriculum than [[ross-program|Ross]] or [[promys|PROMYS]]. Instead of a single number theory thread, students choose from many short courses covering diverse mathematical topics — algebra, topology, combinatorics, analysis, and more. This makes Mathcamp a better fit for students who want to explore many areas of mathematics rather than dive deeply into one.

The younger minimum age (13) also makes Mathcamp accessible to middle school students who are not yet ready for Ross or PROMYS.

## Financial Aid

Mathcamp offers strong financial aid. It is free for US/Canadian households earning under $100,000 annually. Need-based aid is available at higher income levels. The application itself has no fee.

## Application

Admission is primarily based on the Qualifying Quiz, a mathematical problem set available at mathcamp.org in late winter. The Qualifying Quiz is a non-standard problem set different from AMC-style competition problems — it tests mathematical creativity and reasoning rather than speed. Competition scores and transcripts are secondary to the quiz. There is no application fee.

## Common Mistakes

- **Submitting a weak Qualifying Quiz.** Mathcamp admission is based primarily on the quiz, not on competition scores, GPA, or extracurriculars. Families who submit strong transcripts but a hastily completed quiz will not be competitive.
- **Assuming Mathcamp is less selective because it accepts younger students.** The acceptance rate (~15-20%) is comparable to Ross. A 13-year-old who qualifies is exceptional; the lower minimum age does not indicate a lower bar.
- **Enrolling a depth-focused student who wants to spend weeks on one subject.** Mathcamp's student-choice, broad-topics format is the opposite of the [[ross-program|Ross]]/[[promys|PROMYS]] single-thread approach. Fit matters more than prestige — a student who loved a deep number theory experience at Ross may not enjoy Mathcamp's rotation of short courses.
