# Critic Report — Canada/USA Mathcamp — 2026-04-10

## Overall Assessment

This entity article is the best of the three summer program entity articles. It has a clear Quick Facts table, explains how Mathcamp differs from Ross/PROMYS with specific pedagogical detail (student choice, broad topics vs. single thread, younger minimum age), and provides financial aid thresholds. The main gap is the absent Common Mistakes section and the absence of application guidance — particularly for the Qualifying Quiz, which is Mathcamp's unusual admission mechanism.

## Must-Fix Before wiki/ Graduation

1. **No Common Mistakes section.** Three specific mistakes identified: submitting a weak Qualifying Quiz, underestimating selectivity because of the lower minimum age, and enrolling a depth-focused student in a breadth-focused program.

## Should-Fix

1. **The Qualifying Quiz is Mathcamp's most distinctive admission feature and is not mentioned at all.** Every other major summer program article mentions the primary application mechanism. The Qualifying Quiz — a non-standard problem set different from AMC-style problems — determines admission more than transcripts. Adding one sentence: "Admission is primarily based on the Qualifying Quiz, a mathematical problem set available at mathcamp.org in late winter; competition scores and transcripts are secondary" — would make this article actionable for applying families.

2. **"Diverse mathematical topics" is described but not illustrated.** The article says Mathcamp covers algebra, topology, combinatorics, analysis, and more — but a parent who does not know what topology is (the majority of P2 and P3 parents) cannot evaluate this. One example would help: "A student might spend one week studying knot theory, another week on combinatorial game theory, and a third week on complex analysis — none of which appear in the standard high school curriculum."

3. **L4 synthesis opportunity is high.** Mathcamp alumni are active in mathematics communities and their accounts of the program experience are widely available. A "What Participants Report" section would meaningfully differentiate this article and help parents make the Ross vs. Mathcamp vs. PROMYS decision.

## Graduation Decision: NEEDS-REVISION

Missing Common Mistakes section is a must-fix. The absent Qualifying Quiz information also significantly impairs the article's actionability, warranting the revision.
