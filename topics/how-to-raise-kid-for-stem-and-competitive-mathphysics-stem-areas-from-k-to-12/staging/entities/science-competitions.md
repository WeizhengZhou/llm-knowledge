# Science Competitions: ISEF, Regeneron STS, and Science Olympiad

## Quick Facts

| Competition | Type | Eligibility | Scale |
|-------------|------|-------------|-------|
| Regeneron ISEF | Science fair (project-based) | Grades 9-12 via regional fairs | ~1,700 finalists from 60+ countries |
| Regeneron STS | Application-based research | 12th graders (US only) | ~2,600 applicants; 300 Scholars, 40 Finalists |
| Science Olympiad | Team events | Div B: grades 6-9; Div C: grades 9-12 | Nationwide; school-team based |

## Regeneron ISEF (International Science and Engineering Fair)

The Society for Science describes Regeneron ISEF as "the world's largest pre-college STEM competition."

ISEF eligibility requires grades 9-12 students to first compete in a Regeneron ISEF-affiliated science fair (regional or state) and win the right to attend. Team projects may have no more than 3 members.

According to Rise Global Education, approximately 1,700 ISEF finalists from 60+ countries compete at the international fair each year, with hundreds of thousands competing at local/regional levels first.

### ISEF Qualification Pathway

Understanding the ISEF pipeline is critical for planning:

1. **Begin a research project (grades 9-11).** The project must be substantially complete before the regional fair submission deadline (typically February-March). This means research should begin at least 6-12 months before the regional fair.
2. **Find and register for a Regeneron ISEF-affiliated regional or state science fair.** ISEF requires winning the right to attend through an affiliated fair. These fairs are organized independently — search the Society for Science website for affiliated fairs in your region. Registration is separate from ISEF itself.
3. **Win at the regional/state level.** Only top projects from affiliated fairs advance to ISEF International. The number of slots allocated to each fair varies.
4. **Compete at ISEF International.** Finalists present their projects to judges over several days.

**Timeline implication:** A 10th-grade student who starts a research project in September may reach a regional fair in March of 11th grade and ISEF International in May of 11th grade — at the earliest. Students who start research in 11th grade have only one shot at the ISEF pipeline (regional in 12th grade, ISEF in May of 12th grade). Starting earlier provides more attempts and time to refine the project.

### Awards

The ISEF top prize is the George D. Yancopoulos Innovator Award ($100,000). Additional top awards include two Regeneron Young Scientist Awards ($75,000 each) and the Gordon E. Moore Award for Positive Outcomes for Future Generations ($50,000). Regeneron ISEF awards more than $9 million annually.

## Regeneron STS (Science Talent Search)

According to the Society for Science, the Regeneron Science Talent Search was started in 1942 as the Westinghouse Science Talent Search and is described as the nation's oldest and most prestigious science research competition for high school students.

STS alumni have earned thirteen Nobel Prizes.

### Eligibility and Format

Regeneron STS eligibility is restricted to high school seniors (12th grade) enrolled in a US high school at time of application. US citizenship or residency is required.

According to Rise Global Education, STS is entirely application-based (not a science fair). Materials include a research report (up to 20 pages), short essays, educator recommendations, academic transcript, and optional standardized test scores. Finalists travel to Washington, DC.

### Timeline

According to the Society for Science, STS applications open in June and are due in November of the student's senior year. Research must be substantially complete before senior year begins.

### Scale and Awards

According to Society for Science, approximately 2,600 students enter Regeneron STS annually (2,612 in the 2026 cycle, the largest pool since 1967). Of these, 300 are recognized as Scholars (each receiving $2,000) and 40 are selected as Finalists (each receiving at least $25,000, with a top prize of $250,000). Total prizes are approximately $1.8 million annually.

### Notable

According to Rise Global Education, a math project won a top prize in Regeneron STS 2025, described as unusual but signaling that theoretical work is welcome.

## Science Olympiad

According to the competition description, Science Olympiad is a nationwide team competition (15 students per team) held in spring with 23 events spanning earth science, biology, chemistry, physics, and engineering. Division B covers grades 6-9; Division C covers grades 9-12. Competition levels proceed: Invitational -> Regional -> State -> National.

According to the competition description, Science Olympiad requires school team registration; there is no individual application pathway.

Science Olympiad is distinct from ISEF and STS: it tests knowledge and skills across 23 events rather than evaluating a single research project. It is more accessible (school-team based, no individual research required) and covers a broader range of sciences.

## JSHS (Junior Science and Humanities Symposia)

According to multiple sources, the Junior Science and Humanities Symposia (JSHS), administered by the U.S. Army, Navy, and Air Force, was suspended as of October 1, 2025 until further notice.

## Common Mistakes

- **Starting a research project in 11th grade expecting to reach ISEF International.** The ISEF pipeline requires winning a regional affiliated fair first, which typically means doing the research in one year and submitting to regionals the following spring. Starting in 11th grade leaves only one shot (regional fair in 12th grade). Starting in 10th grade provides two opportunities and more time to refine the project.
- **Submitting to STS a project that was not the student's primary intellectual contribution.** STS reviewers evaluate independent thought. Projects done entirely under mentor direction, where the student served as a technician rather than an investigator, score poorly on the STS research narrative components. The research report should demonstrate that the student identified the question, designed the approach, and interpreted the results.
- **Trying to compete in Science Olympiad individually or with a partial team.** Science Olympiad requires a full 15-person school team. Students who want to participate but whose school has no team need to start a new team, which typically takes a full school year before the team is competition-eligible. Plan ahead.
