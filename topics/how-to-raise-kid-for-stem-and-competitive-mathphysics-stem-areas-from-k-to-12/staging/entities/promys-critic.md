# Critic Report — PROMYS (Program in Mathematics for Young Scientists) — 2026-04-10

## Overall Assessment

This entity article is thin — significantly shorter than the Ross article and shorter still than the Canada/USA Mathcamp article. It conveys the essential facts but provides less information than the summer programs guide comparison table. The "Who It Is For" and "Financial Aid" sections add marginal value over what the comparison table already contains. No Common Mistakes section, no application guidance, and no distinguishing detail beyond "similar to Ross, at Boston University." The article does not help a parent decide between PROMYS and Ross — the primary decision it should enable.

## Must-Fix Before wiki/ Graduation

1. **No Common Mistakes section.** Three specific mistakes identified: missing the application window (early February to mid-April), not applying for need-based aid above the free-attendance threshold, and missing the counselor application path for returning students.

2. **Article does not differentiate PROMYS from Ross meaningfully.** A parent reading both entity articles side-by-side will see nearly identical "Who It Is For" descriptions. The article needs at least one concrete distinguishing fact: e.g., PROMYS's location at Boston University means students are embedded in a research university environment; PROMYS has a greater proportion of students from East Coast states; PROMYS's counselor-to-student ratio or program structure differs in X way. If the sources do not contain this information, the article should honestly state: "The educational experience at PROMYS is closely parallel to Ross; the primary practical differences are location (Boston vs. Ohio/Indiana) and the Boston University university environment."

## Should-Fix

1. **The 2025 cost is listed as $6,100 alongside the 2026 cost of $7,000.** Including the year-over-year price increase (from $6,100 to $7,000, a 15% increase) without context could mislead families budgeting for future years. Either note that prices typically increase annually, or remove the 2025 comparison figure.

2. **Acceptance rate is listed as "~15-20% (estimated)" in the Quick Facts — less precise than Ross's "~15% (2023 data)."** If the actual acceptance rate data is unavailable, noting the source of the estimate ("based on comparison with similar programs") would be more honest than presenting the range without attribution.

3. **No application guidance or deadline information.** Unlike Ross (which has no deadline listed either, noted in its own critic report), PROMYS applications have a specific opening and closing window. Adding the application deadline from official PROMYS sources would make this article actionable for families planning their spring application cycle.

## Graduation Decision: NEEDS-REVISION

Two must-fix items: missing Common Mistakes section and failure to differentiate from Ross. The PROMYS article as written is a reduced-length duplicate of the Ross article rather than a distinct entity profile.
