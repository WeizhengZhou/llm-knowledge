## Must-Fix Before wiki/ Graduation

1. **No "Common Mistakes" section.** This article directly serves RO1 ("Decide whether to pursue competitive STEM at all") for P1 and P4 personas — the highest-stakes decision in the entire topic. The omission of a Common Mistakes section means parent errors that derail this decision go unflagged. Required mistakes to cover: (a) conflating school math performance with competition readiness (the child who gets 100s on tests but freezes on AMC problems), (b) treating early-start pressure as showing commitment rather than risking early burnout, (c) mistaking a child's compliance with parental math enthusiasm for intrinsic motivation. These are specific, non-obvious errors that real parents make and that the article's own body text identifies — they belong in a dedicated section.

## Should-Fix

1. **RO1 must_answer item on learning styles is thin.** The "Warning Signs" section names four behavioral red flags but does not affirmatively describe the learning style profile that succeeds in competition math — specifically: tolerance for ambiguity, preference for open-ended over closed-form problems, intrinsic vs. extrinsic motivation orientation. Expanding the "Positive Aptitude Signals" section with 2-3 sentences on learning style fit (not just behavioral observation) would fully answer this must_answer item.

2. **The time commitment table covers levels but not ages.** RO1 asks "what does the child give up?" The table shows weekly hours but not which age band those apply to. A 4th grader doing AMC 8 prep at 2-4 hrs/week displaces different things than a 9th grader at the same level. Adding a column "Typical age" or a parenthetical note per row would make this table more actionable.

## Graduation Decision: NEEDS-REVISION

The missing Common Mistakes section is a must-fix that blocks graduation. The article is otherwise strong and requires only additive work, not structural revision.
