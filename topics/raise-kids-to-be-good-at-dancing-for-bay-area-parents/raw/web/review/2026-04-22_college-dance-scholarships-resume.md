# College Dance Programs: Scholarships, Resumes, and Audition Portfolios

## Dance Resume Components for College Auditions

From USC Glorya Kaufman School of Dance admissions guidance:

### What to Include on a Dance Resume
- Current contact information
- School and graduation year
- Dance training history (studios, teachers, years trained, styles)
- Performance experience (productions, companies, concerts)
- Awards and scholarships received
- Summer intensives attended (especially prestigious programs: ABT, SAB, Alvin Ailey, SF Ballet)
- Other extracurricular activities — "admissions committees want to get to know you as more than just a dancer"
- Leadership positions

### Format
- One page maximum
- Most recent experience first
- Professional formatting

## Audition Portfolio Requirements

### Video Requirements (for pre-screening)
- Upload to YouTube or Google Drive with public viewing permissions
- Typically 1-minute original solo showcasing individual dance skills
- May need multiple clips showing different styles

### Live Audition Components
Scholarship auditions typically include:
- Ballet class (technique demonstration)
- Jazz class
- Contemporary class
- All dancers required to participate in all three genres

### Additional Documents Often Required
- Statement of Purpose (2-3 pages): addresses interests in dance, reasons for pursuing degree, future goals
- Letters of recommendation from dance teachers
- Headshots and dance photos
- Academic transcripts

## How to Build a Strong College Dance Resume During High School

### Years 9-10 (Build Foundation)
- Commit to a primary style and achieve intermediate-advanced level
- Begin attending regional summer intensives
- Pursue performance opportunities (school productions, community theater, youth companies)

### Years 11-12 (Differentiate and Apply)
- Attend prestigious summer intensives (ABT, SAB, SF Ballet, LINES Dance Center, Alvin Ailey)
- Join a pre-professional youth company if available
- Enter notable competitions with strong results
- Work with a private coach to develop solo repertoire
- Film multiple audition videos

## Notable Summer Programs That Strengthen College Applications

(Bay Area students have access to these nationally)
- San Francisco Ballet School Summer Program
- ABT Summer Intensive (multiple sites)
- School of American Ballet Summer
- Alvin Ailey Summer
- LINES Dance Center (SF) — contemporary and modern focus
- Alonzo King LINES Ballet — prestigious contemporary program based in SF

## College Dance Programs with Scholarships

From Scholarships360 and general research:

### University Programs with Dance Scholarships
- UC Santa Barbara (MFA program, some undergraduate funding)
- Cal Arts (strong art school focus)
- USC Glorya Kaufman School of Dance (talent-based scholarships)
- UCLA (competitive BFA program)
- San Jose State University (BA/BFA with some merit aid)

### Competition-Based Scholarships
- **NYCDA Foundation Scholarships:** Awarded at NYCDA nationals
- **California Dance Classics (CDC) Scholarships:** Awarded at CDC competitions
- **Youth America Grand Prix (YAGP):** International competition with scholarship awards to summer programs and schools
- **Princess Grace Awards:** For advanced pre-professional dancers

### How Scholarships Are Awarded in College Admissions
- Arts talent scholarships: separate audition/application process from general admissions
- Merit aid: often 10-50% of tuition for accepted dance majors at private universities
- Need-based + talent: combination packages at many programs

## Professional Dance Career Outcomes

**Realistic career paths post-training:**
- Professional company dancer: Highly competitive; most company jobs in the US pay $30,000-$80,000/year
- Dance teacher/studio owner: Most common career path for trained dancers
- Choreographer: Competitive; often supplemented by teaching income
- Broadway/commercial dance: Audition-based; unpredictable income
- Film/TV dance: Los Angeles market; session work
- Dance administration, arts management: Growing field for dance-trained individuals
- Physical therapy, Pilates instruction: Many dancers transition to related health fields

**The "dance is a short career" reality:** Most professional company dancers peak ages 25-35. Career planning should include secondary skills from the beginning.
