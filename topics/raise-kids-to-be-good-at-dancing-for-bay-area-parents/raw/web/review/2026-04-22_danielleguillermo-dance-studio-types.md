# Recreational? Pre-professional? Competitive? A Parent's Quick Guide to Dance Studio Types

Source: danielleguillermo.com (dance education blog)

## Recreational Dance Studio

**Focus:** Enjoyment and experience of dance
**Time Commitment:** Low — typically one or more one-hour classes weekly
**Student Demographics:** Heavily populated by elementary school and younger dancers
**Characteristics:** "The student's primary focus is the experience of dance for enjoyment"
**Advancement:** Limited progression to professional careers unless students transfer to more intensive programs
**Other Activities:** Dancers can participate in academics and other recreational pursuits
**Future Opportunities:** College minors in dance and campus dance organizations

## Competitive Dance Studio

**Definition:** Schools offering both recreational and competitive components
**Time Commitment:** Higher than recreational; dancers attend technique classes plus additional rehearsals and competition days
**Age Entry:** Competitions begin with dancers as young as 4-5 years old
**Benefits:** Can lead to scholarships, awards, and recognition
**Progression:** "Competitive dancers, depending on the quality of their training, may progress to college and professional levels"
**Flexibility:** Younger students may still balance other activities

## Pre-Professional Dance Studio

**Setting:** May include recreational departments; may or may not compete
**Educator Qualifications:** "The educators will have professional experience and/or degrees, or certificates"
**Time Commitment:** High expectations; multiple days and hours required starting in elementary school
**Summer Training:** "Summer will be a time for intensive training, rather than taking a three-month break"
**Teenage Requirements:** Summer intensives for training, visibility, and professional experience
**Career Path:** Alumni are "more likely to have the ability-level to pursue collegiate programs or professional careers"

## Key Parental Takeaway

Parents should seek quality education and avoid defaulting to the nearest school, evaluating each option carefully.
