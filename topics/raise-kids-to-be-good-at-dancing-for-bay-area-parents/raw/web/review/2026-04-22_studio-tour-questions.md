# Questions Parents Should Ask When Touring a Dance Studio

## The Core Five Questions (from Turning Point Dance)

1. **What styles of dance do you offer?**
   - Why it matters: Children may want to explore different styles as they grow more comfortable
   - Follow-up: Can students add styles or switch mid-year?

2. **Do you have competitive teams?**
   - Why it matters: Understanding the studio's competitive level ensures alignment with your child's goals
   - Follow-up: Is participation on comp team mandatory or optional? What ages qualify?

3. **How many recitals per year?**
   - Why it matters: Recitals demand significant time and financial investment (costumes, tickets, weekend schedule)
   - Red flag: Studios that have 3+ recitals annually with mandatory participation for all students

4. **What qualifications do your teachers have?**
   - Why it matters: Technical expertise AND communication skills with children and families both matter
   - Follow-up: Are teachers certified in any pedagogy (RAD, Vaganova, ABT)? Do they have performing or teaching experience?

5. **How do you group your dancers?**
   - Why it matters: Studios may organize by age, skill level, or competitive goals
   - Follow-up: Can my child advance to a higher level if they progress quickly?

## Extended Questions for Deeper Evaluation

### About the Studio Philosophy
- Do you allow parents to observe class? If not, when can parents watch?
- How does the studio handle bullying between students?
- What is the studio's policy on discussing body weight or dieting with students?
- How do teachers communicate with parents about child progress?
- Is there a body-positive philosophy, or does the studio use mirrors extensively with critique?

### About Safety
- What is your injury prevention protocol?
- How do you handle a child who dances through pain?
- What is your studio's child safeguarding/protection policy?
- Who has access to students outside of class (changing areas, etc.)?

### About Costs and Transparency
- What is the total annual cost estimate (tuition + costumes + recital fees + competition fees)?
- Are there any mandatory purchases (spirit wear, specific dancewear brands)?
- What happens if we need to miss classes? Are make-up classes available?
- Is there a refund policy if we need to withdraw mid-year?

### About Competition Teams
- At what age can students join competition team?
- Is there an audition for the competition team?
- What is the typical annual cost commitment for a competition team member?
- How many competitions per year? Are any mandatory?
- What is the studio's approach to win/loss mentality vs. artistic development?

### About Advancement
- How are students evaluated for level advancement?
- When does pointe work begin, and what criteria do you use?
- What pre-pointe assessment do you conduct?

## What Parents Often DON'T Know to Ask (from Brainy Ballerina and Ballet Help Desk)

**Information studios often don't share proactively:**
- Whether teachers have background checks
- Specific liability and insurance coverage
- Whether there's a written policy on age-appropriate choreography and costumes
- What happens to costume deposits if the student leaves the studio
- Whether competition team fees are refundable once paid

**Hidden costs parents miss:**
- Studio-branded uniform requirements (can be $50-$200/year)
- Mandatory team outings/retreats
- Photography or video packages at recitals
- "Volunteer hour" requirements that are actually mandatory
- Competition spirit wear (jackets, bags, warmups — can be $100-$300)

## Green Flags During Studio Visit

- Studio director answers questions directly and without defensiveness
- Clean, safe facilities with good flooring
- Teachers speak to children kindly and respectfully
- Class sizes are manageable (under 15 for most ages)
- Children in class look engaged and happy
- Studio has written policies on important topics
- Parents can observe (at least occasionally)
- No pressure to enroll on the spot

## Red Flags During Studio Visit

- Director deflects questions about costs or pushes for immediate enrollment
- Teachers are harsh or dismissive toward children
- No observable safety protocols
- Costumes or class content seem age-inappropriate
- No information about teacher qualifications offered
- Studio discourages or prohibits parent observation of classes
