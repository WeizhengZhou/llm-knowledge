# Why Competitive Dance Is Controversial: Ethical Concerns and Child Welfare

## Source 1: Markkula Center for Applied Ethics at Santa Clara University

### Documented Harms in Competitive Dance

**Lack of Regulation**
Private dance studios operate without standardized oversight. Each studio independently creates training guidelines and staff expectations. There are:
- No widely adopted standards for age-appropriate songs, steps, or costumes
- No enforceable safety framework broadly adopted across studios
- No mandatory background checks for instructors in many states

**Mental Health Harms**
The competitive dance environment creates specific psychological risks:
- Body image distortion through constant mirror-based self-critique during 6-30 weekly training hours
- Teacher comments perpetuating unhealthy food relationships (often unintentional)
- Sexualization of minors through revealing costumes and provocative choreography for children as young as 8-10 years old
- Research indicates the sexualization of females correlates with eating disorders and low self-esteem

**Eating Disorders**
Studies document:
- "Anorexia and non-specified eating disorders are more prevalent in ballerinas"
- Dancers "want to be thinner" despite understanding body proportions better than general populations
- The mirror-heavy environment of competitive dance training reinforces this

**Preventable Physical Injuries**
- "Over splitting" (forcing legs past 180 degrees) causes lasting joint damage
- "Dancing through the pain" culture discourages proper injury reporting
- Overuse injuries from repetitive drilling without adequate recovery
- Lack of preventative care education on warm-ups, cross-training, rest

**Vulnerability of Minors**
- Young dancers (ages 5-18) often cannot recognize harmful environments
- They depend entirely on parental oversight — parents must be the safety net

**What Parents Should Know (from SCU):**
- Research studios thoroughly before enrollment
- Monitor for concerning behavioral changes in your child
- Advocate directly with studio directors about concerns
- Balance must exist between artistic expression and athlete safety

## Source 2: PMC/BMJ Research — "High time to enhance dancer welfare"

This research (PMC11086178) is a call to action for improving safeguarding and abuse prevention in dance, noting:

- It is "not uncommon for children to face sexual harassment at dance school and competitions"
- Safeguarding and abuse prevention are growing concerns for dance sector organizations, teachers, judges, parents and dancers
- "Dancers are already an at-risk group for developing mental health problems due to the high physiological and psychological demands placed on them"
- Competitive dancers' social identities are shaped by high-pressure environments in ways that can be harmful

## What Dance Educators Say About Competition Criticism

From Chasta Hamilton's "Dangerous Rhetoric of Dance Moms" and dance teacher publications:
- The competitive dance circuit's emphasis on extreme technique at young ages conflicts with best practice in child development
- Judges at competitions often reward tricks (high kicks, contortion moves) over artistry — incentivizing physically risky training
- The media representation (Dance Moms, other reality TV) creates distorted expectations that many parents and studios then try to emulate
- Hypersexualization concerns are highest in jazz, musical theater, and hip-hop competition categories; lower in ballet competition

## The Regulatory Gap

In the United States:
- Dance studios are private businesses with minimal regulatory oversight
- IADMS (International Association for Dance Medicine and Science) provides voluntary guidelines that most studios never adopt
- Some states have child performer regulations for professional performance but not for dance class instruction
- Parents must function as the primary safeguard

## The Balanced Assessment

**Legitimate benefits of well-run competitive dance:**
- Strong work ethic, goal-setting, and performance experience
- Team bonds and lifelong friendships
- Technical skill development
- Exposure to professional-quality choreography and instruction

**Concerns requiring parental vigilance:**
- Studio culture and competition circuit pressures on body image
- Age-inappropriate content in choreography or costume
- Physical injury risk from extreme training
- Financial exploitation of families by unscrupulous studios
- Adult pressure overriding child's intrinsic motivation
