# Raise Kids to Be Good at Dancing — Bay Area Parent Guide

This wiki helps Bay Area parents navigate the full spectrum of children's dance education — from enrolling a toddler in a first creative movement class to supporting a serious pre-professional ballet student. It covers studio selection, dance styles, costs, the competitive circuit, injury prevention, and long-term development.

## Who This Wiki Is For

- **Parents of 3-7 year olds** deciding whether and where to start dance
- **Parents of 7-12 year olds** considering moving from recreational to more serious training
- **Parents evaluating competitive dance** and wanting to understand the real costs and culture
- **Parents of teens** exploring pre-professional training or college dance pathways

## Bay Area Dance Landscape

The Bay Area has a uniquely diverse dance ecosystem spanning all levels:

**Pre-professional ballet:** [[sf-ballet-school]] (San Francisco, oldest in America), [[city-ballet-sf]] (Vaganova method, SoMa), [[new-ballet-san-jose]] (only ABT Certified school on the West Coast), [[marin-ballet]] (full progression, San Rafael)

**Contemporary and multi-style:** [[odc-school]] (body-positive, contemporary/hip-hop, scholarships), [[shawl-anderson-dance-center]] (Berkeley, since 1958, inclusive)

**Cultural and specialty styles:** [[chhandam-school-kathak]] (Indian classical Kathak, multiple Bay Area locations), [[healy-school-irish-dance]] (Marin County, ages 4-15)

**Affordable access:** [[aileycamp]] (FREE summer program for ages 11-14)

**Recreational and competitive:** [[east-bay-dance-company]] (concrete tuition data, San Ramon Valley)

## Key Facts to Know

- The Bay Area dance market ranges from **free programs** (AileyCamp) to **$13,500/year** pre-professional ballet training (SF Ballet School Trainees, 2025-2026)
- Recreational dance typically costs **$88-340/month** depending on hours per week
- Competitive dance costs **$7,800-$25,300/year** (DanceParent101 estimate) — budget carefully before committing
- Most styles can start at **ages 3-4** for recreational; competitive track typically begins **ages 7-9**; pre-professional ballet auditions typically start **ages 8-10**
- **Pointe shoes** require a minimum age of 11-12 and 3-4 years of prior ballet training — responsible studios will not rush this
