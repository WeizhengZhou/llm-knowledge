# Pre-Professional Dance Pathway in the Bay Area

## Definition

The pre-professional pathway is the progression from serious recreational dance training through intensive study toward a professional dance career. In the Bay Area, this pathway is well-developed for ballet (through [[sf-ballet-school]], [[city-ballet-sf]], and [[new-ballet-san-jose]]) and contemporary dance (through [[odc-school]]).

## Typical Age Milestones

| Age | Stage | What Happens |
|-----|-------|-------------|
| 3-6 | Creative movement, pre-ballet | No competitive or intense work |
| 6-8 | Foundational technique | Weekly classes building basics |
| 8-10 | Entry to pre-professional programs | SF Ballet School accepts ages 8+ for screening |
| 10-12 | Increased frequency (3-4x/week) | Pre-pointe training begins |
| 11-13 | Pointe work (if physically ready) | Consider pre-professional programs seriously |
| 13-16 | Intensives and advanced levels | Summer programs; competition experience |
| 16-18 | Trainee/Studio Company | Pre-professional year-round programs; college auditions |

**Important caveat:** According to CPYB, a child who begins serious dance training at ages 10-12 can still reach pre-professional level by late teens if naturally gifted and well-trained.

## Milestones That Indicate Pre-Professional Potential

- Technical proficiency ahead of age-peers
- Physical attributes suitable for professional dance (flexibility, proportions, musicality)
- Self-motivation that exceeds parental encouragement
- Ability to accept and apply corrections immediately
- Artistic expression beyond technique
- Consistent work ethic over years, not just seasons
- Interest in attending summer intensives and auditioning for programs

### Contemporary

[[odc-school]] offers the strongest pre-professional track for contemporary and hip-hop through the Dance Jam and SEEDS performance companies. This is not a ballet pre-professional path but serves aspiring contemporary and hip-hop dancers.

## Training Intensity

According to Central Pennsylvania Youth Ballet (CPYB), pre-professional dance training daily schedules can include 5-10 hours of classes and rehearsals per day. This covers technique, pointe, men's work, repertoire, variations, pas de deux, contemporary, stretch and strengthening, and mental skills training.

According to New Ballet, the Senior Pre-Professional Division (Levels 4-6) requires audition and students typically train 4-5 days per week.

## Early Specialization vs. Diversification

According to the Royal Ballet School (2025) and IADMS, early diversification is preferred over early specialization; specialization in serious ballet/dance training is generally appropriate at ages 10-14.

Key findings from research:
- Athletes who delay specialization often enjoy longer, more sustainable careers
- Focusing on one activity on an immature body leads to increased injury risk
- Too much training time leads to social isolation from peers
- The most enduring elite performers often "sampled broadly" first

**Recommended model:** Multiple physical activities through ages 10-12 (swimming, gymnastics, other sports) alongside dance. Earlier specialization only justified at explicitly elite programs with world-class faculty.

## Summer Intensives

Summer intensives are a critical part of the pre-professional pathway. They provide exposure to different teaching styles, audition experience, and opportunities for program invitations.

### Bay Area Summer Programs

- **SF Ballet School Summer Program:** Audition-based; national audition tour
- **New Ballet San Jose Summer Intensive:** Open to students Levels 1B+ and external students ages 10-23; scholarship auditions available
- **ODC Summer Programs:** Eight-week summer weekend session; scholarship deadline May 29, 2026
- **AileyCamp Berkeley/Oakland:** Free; ages 11-14; June 15-July 24, 2026

### National Programs with Bay Area Relevance

- School of American Ballet (SAB) Summer Intensive
- ABT Summer Intensives (regional audition sites in California)
- Alonzo King LINES Ballet (San Francisco) -- contemporary focus
- Juilliard Summer Dance Intensive -- key pipeline for Juilliard applicants

## College Dance Pathways

### Selective Programs

According to multiple sources including College Transitions and USC Kaufman, Juilliard Dance BFA program typically accepts approximately 24 students per year (12 women and 12 men). College Transitions reports that Juilliard Dance BFA admission requires minimum 3 years experience in both ballet and modern dance, and currently training minimum 10 hours per week.

College Transitions reports USC Kaufman accepts approximately 30-40 students per year; live sources indicate the number is generally in the high 20s to low 30s. An audition is required.

### California Options

- USC Kaufman School of Dance (Los Angeles)
- UC Irvine Dance Department
- UCLA Department of World Arts and Cultures/Dance
- UC Santa Barbara Department of Theater and Dance
- CalArts (Valencia)

### Building a College Dance Resume

**Years 9-10:** Commit to a primary style at intermediate-advanced level. Begin attending regional summer intensives. Pursue performance opportunities.

**Years 11-12:** Attend prestigious summer intensives (ABT, SAB, SF Ballet, LINES). Join a pre-professional youth company. Enter notable competitions. Develop solo repertoire with a private coach. Film audition videos.

### Scholarship Pathways

- YAGP (Youth America Grand Prix) -- scholarship awards to summer programs and schools
- NYCDA Foundation scholarships (at nationals)
- California Dance Classics scholarships
- Direct merit scholarships from university dance programs

## Professional Career Outcomes

According to a USC Kaufman source, most professional company dance jobs in the US pay $30,000-$80,000 per year. According to a USC Kaufman source, most professional company dancers peak between ages 25-35.

Career paths beyond performance include teaching, choreography, dance administration, physical therapy, Pilates instruction, and arts management.

## Common Mistakes

- **Pushing for pre-professional too early.** According to the Royal Ballet School (2025) and IADMS, early diversification is preferred over early specialization; specialization in serious ballet/dance training is generally appropriate at ages 10-14. A 7-year-old does not need to be on a pre-professional track. Keep them in multi-style recreational classes and let talent and motivation reveal themselves naturally.

- **Assuming pre-professional ballet is the only serious path.** Contemporary dance (through [[odc-school]]), competitive dance, and college dance programs all offer serious training and career possibilities. According to a USC Kaufman source, most professional company dance jobs in the US pay $30,000-$80,000 per year. The career landscape is broader than classical ballet company placement.

- **Not attending summer intensives.** Summer intensives are where pre-professional students get seen by program directors, experience different teaching methods, and build their resumes. A student who trains year-round at one school but never attends a summer intensive is missing a critical pathway component. SF Ballet School, New Ballet San Jose, and national programs (SAB, ABT, LINES) all offer summer options.

- **Overlooking the financial aid available at pre-professional programs.** SF Ballet School 2025-2026 financial aid applications open January 1; current family deadline is May 1; new family deadline is upon acceptance notification. According to New Ballet, the Studio Company is potentially tuition-free for exceptional dancers with stipends possible. According to ODC, the Dance Jam Scholarship Fund covers a full year of tuition, participation fees, and a stipend for ancillary expenses such as travel and dancewear for one student. Pre-professional dance is not only for wealthy families.
