# Critic Report — East Bay Dance Company — 2026-04-22

## Overall Assessment
The best-executed entity article in the set from a financial transparency standpoint. East Bay Dance Company's granular tuition table and cost planning example are genuinely useful for RO3. The article's limitation is that it is almost entirely a tuition sheet — it says nothing about what the studio teaches, who teaches it, what ages it serves best, or whether it is recreational or competitive, which are the questions a parent evaluating a studio actually needs to answer.

## Must-Fix Before wiki/ Graduation

1. Missing "Common Mistakes" section (D3 = 0 blocks graduation). East Bay Dance Company-specific mistakes: (a) Missing the no-refund policy when planning around a child's likely absences — the article states "the studio does not provide refunds for any reason including illness, injury, or missed classes" but buries it in Policies; this is a financial trap for families who enroll and then have their child get sick for a month. The Common Mistakes section should flag this explicitly: "Do not enroll expecting to pause or pause-and-credit during injury or illness — this studio does not offer credits." (b) Forgetting to budget for the registration fee when using the $88/month figure to compare studios — the $35 annual fee and 10-month payment structure means the true annual cost is $915 before any costumes or recital fees, which parents overlook when seeing "$88/month." (c) Adding extra classes mid-year without checking the 8+ classes pricing — the table shows a flat $340 cap at 7 classes/week but then $12/additional class beyond 8; families who add a second style mid-year may not realize they exit the flat-fee zone.

2. Add a brief Programs or Overview section stating what styles the studio teaches and what age range it serves. A parent cannot evaluate this studio without knowing whether it offers ballet, hip-hop, or both, and whether it has a competitive program. Even three sentences would transform this from a price sheet into a useful entity article.

## Should-Fix

1. The article says this studio is "a useful benchmark for understanding dance costs in the region" — this is a claim about the studio's role in the KB, not about the studio itself. This meta-comment belongs in the bay-area-dance-costs guide, not the entity article. Remove it or rephrase as "the studio publishes detailed tuition schedules, which is unusual among Bay Area studios."
2. The late payment fee and credit card surcharge are buried in the Discounts and Policies section. Elevate the 2.49% credit card fee to the main Tuition section as a note — most Bay Area parents pay by card and will be surprised by this fee when they receive their statement.
