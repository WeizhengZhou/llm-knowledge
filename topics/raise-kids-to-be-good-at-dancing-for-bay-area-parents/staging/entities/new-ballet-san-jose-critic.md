## Must-Fix Before wiki/ Graduation

1. Missing "Common Mistakes" section (D3 = 0 blocks graduation). New Ballet-specific mistakes: (a) Assuming the summer intensive scholarship audition dates are posted in advance — the article says "Bay Area dates to be announced" with video option available; parents who wait for a specific date may miss the window entirely. They should contact info@newballet.com by January each year to get on the notification list. (b) Conflating "ABT Certified" with "ABT's school" — New Ballet uses the ABT National Training Curriculum but is an independent school, not an ABT facility; parents sometimes assume attendance guarantees ABT Summer Intensive placement or special access, which is not stated anywhere in the source material. (c) Treating the Studio Company's "potentially tuition-free" status as guaranteed — the article itself qualifies this with "potentially" and "stipends possible"; these are exceptional placements, not standard outcomes.

## Should-Fix

1. Tuition gap: for any division where tuition is not public, state the specific contact method and state that the school's Schedule and Tuition document is available via the website link. At minimum, note whether the Primary Division is in a similar price range to competitors or notably cheaper/more expensive, since this affects the decision for South Bay families comparing New Ballet to Marin Ballet or SF Ballet School.
2. The Junior Pre-Professional Division says "placement class required (not a full audition)" — this is a meaningful and parent-friendly distinction that should be called out more prominently. Many parents will avoid applying to programs they believe require formal auditions. Consider adding a sentence: "If your child has prior ballet experience and is ages 8+, a placement class (not a competitive audition) determines level placement."
3. Add a "Who New Ballet Is For" summary at the end: South Bay families; children starting ABT-aligned training from age 2.5; advanced dancers seeking a West Coast ABT-certified pathway. This one paragraph would convert the article from a description into a decision tool.
