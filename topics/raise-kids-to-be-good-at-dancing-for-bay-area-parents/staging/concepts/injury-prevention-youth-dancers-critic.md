## Must-Fix Before wiki/ Graduation

1. Missing "Common Mistakes" section (D3 = 0 blocks graduation). Injury-prevention-specific mistakes parents make: (a) Relying on the studio to enforce pointe readiness criteria without verifying the studio's actual policy — the article explains the criteria but parents often assume any accredited studio applies them automatically. Parents should ask their studio directly: "What specific criteria do you use to determine pointe readiness, and who makes that assessment?" A studio that cannot answer this question specifically is a red flag. (b) Treating growth spurt timing as predictable and adjusting training accordingly — the article correctly notes growth spurts last 18-24 months and increase injury risk, but parents may not know that the safest response is to temporarily reduce training intensity during growth spurts rather than maintain or increase it. Add the action: if your child grows more than 2 inches in 6 months, discuss a temporary reduction in weekly training hours with the studio instructor. (c) Dismissing the training hour guideline as overprotective — the guideline (no more hours per week than the child's age in years) sounds extremely conservative to parents of competitive dancers; parents of an 8-year-old training 12 hours/week will dismiss it as impractical. The article correctly identifies it as an L3 guideline, but should also note that even if the specific numbers are debated, the underlying principle (training volume must not exceed recovery capacity) is medically grounded.

## Should-Fix

1. The cross-training section names Pilates, swimming, and strength conditioning with brief benefit descriptions. Add one actionable recommendation for Bay Area parents: "Many Bay Area dance studios partner with local Pilates studios for youth cross-training; ask your studio if they have a recommended Pilates provider, or look for youth Pilates classes at community recreation centers."
2. The Nutrition section is brief and generic. It contains no Bay Area-specific resources (dietitians who work with dancers, community centers with nutrition programs for youth athletes). Given the article's purpose is practical guidance for Bay Area parents, consider either expanding this section or explicitly noting that dance-specific nutrition guidance should come from a registered dietitian, not from general dancer snack recommendations.
3. The article's title in frontmatter is "Injury Prevention for Youth Dancers" but the body begins with "Dance injuries in children and adolescents are predominantly overuse injuries" — the Definition section frames this as a reference entry rather than a guide. The article's actual content is parent-facing and practical; consider changing the Definition header to "Why This Matters" or "Understanding Dance Injuries" to match the parent audience and action orientation.
