## Must-Fix Before wiki/ Graduation

1. Missing "Common Mistakes" section (D3 = 0 blocks graduation). Pre-professional-pathway-specific mistakes: (a) Confusing a child's enjoyment of dancing with pre-professional potential — the article's "Milestones That Indicate Pre-Professional Potential" list is useful precisely because parents cannot use "loves to dance" as a screening criterion; the common mistake is parents investing in pre-professional programs based on enthusiasm rather than the specific technical and psychological markers listed. Frame this as: "Do not enroll a child in a pre-professional program because they love dance — enjoyment is necessary but not sufficient. Look for the specific markers listed above, particularly self-motivation that exceeds parental encouragement and the ability to accept corrections immediately." (b) Choosing a summer intensive based on prestige rather than match — SAB and ABT Summer Intensives are nationally prestigious but highly competitive; a dancer who auditions unsuccessfully at SAB but would thrive at the SF Ballet Summer Program has made a worse decision than the reverse would imply. Parents should prioritize programs where the child has a realistic shot at meaningful training over programs whose names carry the most cultural cachet. (c) Treating a Trainee or Studio Company spot as a professional contract — the Trainee Program at SF Ballet School and Studio Company at New Ballet San Jose are pre-professional programs with tuition (or potential tuition-free status); neither is employment. Parents and dancers who frame these as "making it" may reduce the dancer's motivation to continue developing, or may feel betrayed when the program does not lead to immediate company placement.

## Should-Fix

1. The Professional Career Outcomes section states "$30,000-$80,000 per year" from a USC Kaufman source. Add the year of this data and note that Bay Area cost of living makes the lower end of this range difficult to sustain locally; this is relevant context for Bay Area parents evaluating the ROI of pre-professional investment.
2. The Building a College Dance Resume section (Years 9-10, Years 11-12) is structured as if the reader knows what "Year 9" means — it should be "Grade 9-10 (ages 14-16)" and "Grade 11-12 (ages 16-18)" for clarity. Parents do not speak in years-of-training; they speak in grades.
3. The Academy of Ballet SF entry in the Bay Area Pre-Professional Programs table lists "Director Zory Karah" and "Not published" for tuition. This entity is mentioned only in this article and has no entity article. Either create an entity article stub or remove the entry to avoid a dead reference.
