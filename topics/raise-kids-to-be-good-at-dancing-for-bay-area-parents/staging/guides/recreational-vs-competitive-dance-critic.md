## Must-Fix Before wiki/ Graduation

1. Missing "Common Mistakes" section (D3 = 0 blocks graduation). This guide's mistakes should be the most specific in the entire set given that it covers the highest-stakes decisions: (a) Signing up for competitive dance because the studio has a "pre-competitive" class without understanding that the full competitive track is what the pre-competitive class feeds — parents who enroll in "pre-competitive" to try it out are often pressured to commit to full competition within one season; ask the studio upfront what percentage of pre-competitive families continue to full competition, and what happens to those who opt out. (b) Using total competition cost estimates from national sources without applying Bay Area cost adjustments — travel costs, hotel rates, and registration fees around the Bay Area are higher than national averages; the $7,800-$25,300 range is a national L3 estimate, not a Bay Area audit. (c) Treating the "over $100,000 lifetime" figure as typical rather than worst-case — the DanceParent101 estimate runs from age 3 to 18 at high competition intensity; a family that competes for 5 years at moderate intensity will spend significantly less. Parents who see $100K and dismiss the entire cost question are making the same mistake as parents who accept it uncritically. (d) Allowing a child to specialize in pre-professional before age 10-12 in response to a studio's pressure rather than the child's own readiness — the Royal Ballet School and IADMS guidance cited in the article supports delayed specialization; parents should use this as protective framing when studios push for earlier commitment.

## Should-Fix

1. The "Decision age" row in the comparison table shows "Ages 7-10 optimal start" for competitive and "Ages 10-14 specialization" for pre-professional. These are two different concepts (starting competition vs. specializing in a style) compressed into the same row label. Split into two rows or add a clarifying footnote to avoid parents reading "Ages 7-10" as the required start age for competitive dance.
2. The Pre-Professional section cites Royal Ballet School (2025) and IADMS for the "ages 10-14 specialization" guidance. This citation mixes a UK institution's guidance with Bay Area programs. Add a sentence noting the Bay Area programs (SF Ballet School accepts from age 8, New Ballet from 2.5) and that the Royal Ballet/IADMS guidance is about the wisdom of early specialization, not the minimum age for program enrollment — these are different things.
