The age-by-age progression, style-specific starting age table, and developmental readiness markers together fully enable the RO1 decision. ## Must-Fix Before wiki/ Graduation

1. Missing "Common Mistakes" section (D3 = 0 blocks graduation). Starting-age-specific mistakes that Bay Area parents actually make: (a) Enrolling a 3-year-old in "ballet" when the studio offers pre-ballet (creative movement with ballet vocabulary) — parents who expect their 3-year-old to come home doing pliés and arabesques will be disappointed and may switch studios unnecessarily; the article describes this distinction but does not warn that the gap between parent expectation and studio offering causes a common dropout at the first recital. (b) Waiting until age 7 or 8 to start because "they need to be ready" when the child has been asking since age 4 — the article correctly says child readiness varies, but parents often err toward waiting too long out of concern about commitment; the developmental readiness markers should be presented as a green light, not a test to pass. (c) Starting pointe at 10 because "she's been doing ballet for 3 years" — the article covers pointe readiness in [[injury-prevention-youth-dancers]] but does not warn here that a child who started at 7 and is now 10 almost certainly does not yet meet pointe criteria (minimum age 11-12, plus physical criteria); the cross-reference should be accompanied by an explicit warning in this article since many parents will not follow the link.

## Should-Fix

1. The Ages 14-18 section is significantly thinner than earlier age bands — one sentence mentioning summer intensives, auditions, and college prep without the same concrete examples provided for earlier ages. Expand with at minimum one concrete Bay Area action: "By age 14-16, serious ballet students should have completed one summer intensive at a regional or national program (SF Ballet, New Ballet, or ABT California sites) and be training at least 4 days per week at their primary studio."
2. The CPYB citation for the Late Starter section ("a child who begins serious dance training at ages 10-12 can still reach pre-professional level by late teens if naturally gifted and well-trained") is the article's most important reassurance for anxious P2 parents. It is currently embedded in a paragraph without a callout. Consider placing it in a highlighted note: "Important for late starters: according to CPYB, beginning serious training at 10-12 does not close professional doors if the child is naturally gifted and well-coached."
