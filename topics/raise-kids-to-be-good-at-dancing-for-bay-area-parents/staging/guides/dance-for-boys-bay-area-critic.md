## Must-Fix Before wiki/ Graduation

1. Missing "Common Mistakes" section (D3 = 0 blocks graduation). Boys-dance-specific mistakes parents make: (a) Putting a boy in a class where he is the only male without preparing him — the article's "Navigating Stereotypes" section correctly identifies this as the primary concern, but the Common Mistakes section should frame it as an actionable error: before enrolling, call the studio and ask "how many boys are currently in this class?" rather than assuming the studio is boys-friendly based on the website. (b) Starting ballet for a 12-year-old boy who has had no prior dance because "it is the foundation" — a 12-year-old boy starting ballet in a class of 7-year-old girls who have been dancing for five years is the fastest route to quitting. Intermediate-level hip-hop or contemporary is a better entry point that builds confidence and can transition to ballet technique later. (c) Assuming hip-hop requires prior athletic ability while assuming ballet does not — parents of athletic boys often steer toward hip-hop while avoiding ballet because they perceive ballet as technically demanding and hip-hop as accessible; in practice, beginning hip-hop classes for children require no more prior skill than beginning ballet classes, and the coordination demands are comparable.

2. In the Groove Studios entry: either remove it from this guide (it is adult-focused and not confirmed as appropriate for the target age range), or revise the description to state explicitly: "In the Groove Studios is an adult studio that may offer some youth-accessible classes; contact before visiting to confirm age eligibility and whether the class environment is appropriate for a child." The current framing — listing it as an age 10+ option while noting "primarily adult-focused" — is not adequate protection for parents who may take their 10-year-old without calling ahead.

## Should-Fix

The Rhythm Room Dance Center (Lafayette) and Dance Theatre Arts (Hayward) are listed under "Studios with Male Instructors" with no descriptive content — not even a style, age range, or contact information. Either add sufficient information (style, age range, approximate tuition band, website) for these studios to be actionable, or remove them. A studio name with no supporting information only frustrates parents who try to research further.

The article would benefit from an explicit note in the Overview explaining why confidence is L3 and what that means for the studio recommendations: "Many studio recommendations in this guide are sourced from Bay Area parent forums rather than official studio publications; verify current programs and male instructor availability directly with studios before visiting."
