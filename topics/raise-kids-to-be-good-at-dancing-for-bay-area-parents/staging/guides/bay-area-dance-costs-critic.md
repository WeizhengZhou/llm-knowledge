## Must-Fix Before wiki/ Graduation

1. Missing "Common Mistakes" section (D3 = 0 blocks graduation). Cost-specific mistakes Bay Area parents make: (a) Using monthly tuition as the total annual cost — the article itself documents that monthly tuition at recreational studios is roughly 60-70% of total annual cost when registration, costumes, recital fees, and shoes are added. Frame this as: "Do not compare studios on monthly tuition alone. At most Bay Area studios, the annual add-ons (costume: $50-$150, registration: $35-$250, performance fee: $65-$300, shoes: $50-$135) add 30-50% to the headline monthly figure." (b) Not asking whether competition fees are per-routine or per-event — competitive studios typically charge entry fees per routine (solo, group, duo separately), which multiplies quickly. A dancer with one solo, one group routine, and one duo in a single competition might pay $150-$600 in entry fees for that one event, not one flat fee. (c) Missing financial aid deadlines because they open months before the program starts — SF Ballet School's financial aid opens January 1 for a program that starts in September; ODC's Fall scholarship deadline (August 29) is before the September 6 session start. Parents who discover these programs in October are a full year behind on financial aid.

2. Add a "Time Commitment by Level" table or section — even a short one — to address the RO3 must_answer item "How many hours per week does each level of dance require?" Suggest pulling data already present in the recreational-vs-competitive-dance article (1-3 hours/week recreational; 4-15+ competitive; 10-30 pre-professional) and presenting it here alongside the dollar costs so parents see both dimensions together.

## Should-Fix

1. The competitive cost range ($7,800-$25,300+) is cited to DanceParent101 and Second Stage Dance. Add a one-sentence caveat: "These estimates are from national dance community sources and are not audited Bay Area figures; Bay Area travel costs and venue fees may push the upper bound higher than these estimates."
2. The Affordable and Sliding-Scale Options section lists Sambaxe SF at $5 youth/$10 family. This is an excellent inclusion. Add a note on session frequency and address so parents can find it — currently the only guidance is "Fridays 5:30 PM to 7:00 PM" with no location or contact information.
3. The sibling discounts section is useful but brief. Add whether these discounts stack (can you get both the sibling discount and the multi-class discount at ODC?) — this is a practical question for multi-child dance families.
