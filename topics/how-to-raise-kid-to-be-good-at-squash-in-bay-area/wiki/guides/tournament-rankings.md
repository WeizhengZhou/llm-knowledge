# Tournament System and Rankings

## Overview

This guide explains how US Squash junior tournaments and rankings work, what NorCal tournaments are available, and how to get started with competitive play. Understanding this system is essential for families considering the competitive pathway.

## US Squash Junior Age Divisions

US Squash junior age divisions are U11, U13, U15, U17, and U19; players must be under the age limit through the tournament's final day to be eligible.

## Tournament Tiers

US Squash accredits approximately 100 bronze tournaments, 80 silver tournaments, 28 gold tournaments, and 5 JCT events annually.

| Tier | Approximate Count | Entry Fee Range | Competition Level |
|------|-------------------|-----------------|-------------------|
| Bronze | ~100 | $35-75 | Local/introductory |
| Silver | ~80 | $50-100 | Regional |
| Gold | ~28 | $100-200+ | Strong regional/national |
| JCT (Junior Championship Tour) | 5 | $100-200+ | National elite |

Junior tournament entry fees typically range $35-75 per draw for most junior tournaments; gold and JCT events cost $100-200+.

The US Squash USR Junior Circuit is a competitive pathway distinct from the JCT-based ranking system.

## How Rankings Work

US Squash junior rankings are calculated as an average of a player's top 4 points-earning tournaments played within the past 11 months.

### Requirements for a National Ranking

To obtain a US Squash junior national ranking, players must: (1) hold active US Squash membership, (2) pass an age-appropriate online referee exam, (3) be a U.S. citizen or resident, and (4) have at least 3 active tournament exposures in the same age group within the past 11 months.

### US Squash Membership

US Squash membership costs $149 per year.

### Referee Exam Requirement

US Squash junior referee exam requirements by age group: U13 = Level 1, U15 = Level 2, U17 = Level 3, U19 = Level 4; recertification is required every two years.

The referee exam ensures players understand the rules (including the let/stroke system) before competing in sanctioned events.

## Registration Platform

All US Squash tournament registration is handled through **ClubLocker** (clublocker.com). Parents create an account, link their child's US Squash membership, and browse available tournaments by region and date.

## Match Format

Squash matches are played in a best-of-five format; each game is won by reaching 11 points using point-a-rally (PAR) scoring; if tied at 10-10, play continues until a two-point lead is achieved.

Squash uses point-a-rally (PAR) scoring, where every rally produces a point regardless of who served.

## Getting Started with Tournaments

### Step 1: Join US Squash
Purchase a US Squash membership ($149/year) and designate a [[norcal-squash]] club as your home club.

### Step 2: Pass the Referee Exam
Complete the age-appropriate online referee exam before registering for sanctioned events.

### Step 3: Register on ClubLocker
Create a ClubLocker account and link your US Squash membership.

### Step 4: Start with a Bronze Tournament
Local bronze events are the entry point. Look for [[squash-zone]] Silver (March), Oakwood Winter Bronze (February), or Bay Club Fremont Winter Challenge (December).

## Common Mistakes

- **Entering too high a tier too early.** Start at bronze level. Losing every match at a gold tournament is demoralizing and teaches less than competitive matches at the right level.
- **Forgetting the referee exam.** Without it, tournament results do not count toward rankings.
- **Not tracking the 11-month window.** Rankings are rolling; spacing tournaments evenly matters.
