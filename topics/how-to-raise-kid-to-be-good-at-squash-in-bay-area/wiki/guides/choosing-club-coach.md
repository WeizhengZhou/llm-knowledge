# Choosing a Club and Coach

## Overview

This guide helps Bay Area parents evaluate squash clubs and coaches for their child. The Bay Area has three types of squash training environments: dedicated squash facilities, private athletic clubs with squash courts, and independent coaching services. Coach quality and program structure matter more than facility prestige.

## Types of Training Environments

| Type | Examples | Pros | Cons |
|------|----------|------|------|
| Dedicated squash facility | [[squash-zone]] | Full junior programming, multiple coaches, tournament hosting | Limited to one location |
| Private athletic club | [[bay-club-squash]], [[oakwood-athletic-club]] | Multi-sport amenities, convenient for families | Squash may not be primary focus; full club membership required |
| Independent coaching service | [[altius-performance-squash]] | Bespoke 1-on-1 attention, tailored development | No facility of their own; higher per-session cost |
| Academy | [[hisa-squash-academy]] | Comprehensive program (lessons, camps, leagues) | Pricing opaque |
| Urban squash program | [[squashdrive]] | Free, integrated academics | Eligibility-based; serves specific communities |

## US Squash Coaching Certification

US Squash coaching certification costs: Start Squash $200 ($250 late); Level 1 $450 ($500 late); Level 2 $750 ($800 late); Level 3 $900 ($950 late).

US Squash Level 3 coaching certification is the highest certification US Squash currently offers. According to US Squash, its certifications are the only official coaching certifications recognized by US Squash in the USA.

According to US Squash, fourteen professional players earned Level 3 coaching certification in 2025.

Coaches must hold a Coach Pass membership to coach at US Squash accredited junior tournaments or act as tournament director of an accredited tournament.

### What Parents Should Look For

- **Level 1 minimum** for any coach working with your child in structured programs
- **Level 2 or Level 3** for coaches developing competitive players
- **Junior-specific experience:** Superior playing ability alone does not guarantee effective instruction of young athletes. Teaching skills matter most.
- **Ask for parent references**

## Questions to Ask a Potential Coach

**Background:**
- How many years have you coached juniors specifically?
- What US Squash certifications do you hold?
- Can you provide parent references?

**Teaching Philosophy:**
- How do you structure lessons for beginners? (Listen for emphasis on fun, safety, and gradual skill building)
- What keeps young players motivated?
- How do you handle struggling or frustrated students?

**Practical:**
- Lesson rates and what is included?
- Facility locations and equipment availability?
- Scheduling flexibility and cancellation policies?
- Do you offer trial lessons?

## Green Flags

- Patient, age-appropriate communication
- Emphasis on safety (eyeguards, court awareness) from the start
- Prioritizes fun and engagement for beginners
- Progressive skill building with clear milestones
- Genuinely excited about teaching

## Red Flags

- Reluctance answering questions about methods or background
- Immediate focus on competition before assessing interest and ability
- Guaranteeing rapid results
- Impatience with student mistakes
- Unsafe practices or lack of eye protection emphasis

## Common Mistakes

- **Choosing a coach based on their former playing ranking alone.** A former PSA professional who has never coached children may deliver instruction calibrated for an adult competitor. A 9-year-old needs age-appropriate communication, patience, and fun-focused progression. Ask specifically: "How many years have you worked with juniors under 12?" and "Can you provide references from parents of young players?"
- **Selecting a club based on geographic proximity without checking junior program structure.** A club that offers only private lessons is a different product than one offering weekly junior group clinics with age-band groupings. Group clinics provide competitive match experience against peers; private lessons build technique in isolation. Most competitive juniors need both. Verify whether your nearest club runs structured junior group sessions before committing to membership.
- **Not checking whether the coach holds a current Coach Pass.** Coaches must hold a Coach Pass membership to coach at US Squash accredited junior tournaments or act as tournament director of an accredited tournament. A coach without a Coach Pass cannot provide court-side coaching at sanctioned events — this matters for competitive-track families who travel to tournaments.
- **Not matching coach certification to player level.** For beginners and development players (ages 5-12, unrated), Level 1 certification is adequate and appropriate. For competitive players targeting silver, gold, or JCT events, look for Level 2 or Level 3 coaches with documented junior tournament experience and player results.
