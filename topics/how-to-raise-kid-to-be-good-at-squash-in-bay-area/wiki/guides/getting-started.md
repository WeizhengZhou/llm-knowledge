# Getting Started with Junior Squash

## Overview

This guide is for Bay Area parents exploring whether squash is the right sport for their child. It covers what age to start, what to expect at a first lesson, what basic equipment is needed, and how to find a program.

## What Age to Start

[[squash-zone]] offers Beginner Clinics for ages 5-9, making that the earliest formal entry point in the Bay Area. Most children begin structured squash between ages 6 and 9. The key developmental readiness signs are basic hand-eye coordination, ability to follow instructions in a group setting, and comfort being in an enclosed court space.

For younger children (under 8), coaches focus on agility, balance, and coordination through movement games rather than squash-specific technique. Older beginners (13+) can progress more quickly to grip instruction, basic strokes, and court awareness.

## What to Expect at the First Lesson

A child's first squash lesson focuses on sparking interest rather than perfecting technique. Most facilities provide junior rackets and safety eyeguards, so upfront equipment cost is minimal.

**What to bring:**
- Clean, non-marking athletic shoes (required by all facilities)
- Comfortable athletic clothing allowing free movement
- Water bottle

**What happens:**
- **First 10 minutes:** Coach establishes comfort and safety, introduces court boundaries, explains the tin and service boxes, demonstrates basic equipment use
- **Core session:** For younger children, movement games and hand-eye coordination exercises; for older beginners, grip instruction and basic strokes
- **Safety:** Eye protection is mandatory. According to Squash Zone, eye protection is mandatory for all players 18 and under.

**Positive indicators to watch for:** Patient coaching, encouraging communication, child engagement, and progressive skill building. **Red flags:** Instructor impatience, excessive technical detail for a beginner, or an overwhelmed child.

**After the lesson:** Expect the coach to discuss performance observations and scheduling guidance. Equipment recommendations typically come after 3-4 lessons.

## Equipment Basics

A junior squash equipment starter kit (racket, eyeguards, court shoes, balls, bag) costs approximately $130-170 at the budget level or $200-300 at the mid-range level.

Squash eyeguards must meet or exceed the ASTM-F3164 standard; US Squash mandates eyeguards for all juniors in all sanctioned events. Basic certified eyeguards cost approximately $20-40.

According to my-squash.com, recommended junior squash racket sizing is: under 8 years: 23-inch frame (~180g); ages 8-11: 25-inch frame (120-150g); age 12+ or 150cm+ tall: full-length frames, remaining under 130g until strength and technique are proven.

According to my-squash.com, most junior squash rackets cost approximately $50; full-size rackets suitable for juniors are available under $120.

## Is Squash Right for My Child?

Consider squash if your child:
- Enjoys fast-paced, high-energy activities
- Is comfortable in enclosed spaces
- Has interest in racket sports
- Thrives on individual competition with a social community

See [[squash-vs-tennis]] for a detailed comparison with tennis, the most common alternative racket sport.

## Common Mistakes

- **Buying expensive equipment before the first lesson.** Most clubs lend rackets and eyeguards for beginners. Wait 3-4 lessons before investing.
- **Choosing a club based on proximity alone.** Junior programming quality varies enormously; see [[choosing-club-coach]] for evaluation criteria.
- **Expecting rapid visible progress.** Consistency matters more than frequency at the beginner stage.
