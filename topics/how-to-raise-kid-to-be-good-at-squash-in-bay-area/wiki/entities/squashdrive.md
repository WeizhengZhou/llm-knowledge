## Overview

SquashDrive is based in Emeryville, CA (PO Box 99165, Emeryville, CA 94662) and serves East Bay youth in grades 4-11. According to SquashDrive, the program is free for eligible participants; SquashDrive is a nonprofit funded by donations.

SquashDrive follows the urban squash model pioneered by programs like CitySquash (New York), SquashBusters (Boston), and SquashSmarts (Philadelphia), integrating academic tutoring, squash instruction, and character building.

## Program Components

### Squash Training
SquashDrive provides a minimum of 100 hours per year of squash and fitness instruction and 115 hours per year of homework help and academic enrichment. Competitive tournament opportunities and week-long camps are available; elite team members pursue national rankings.

### Academic Support
The program provides daily after-school academic support, STEM workshops, and career exposure. Goals include maintaining 3.0+ GPA and grade-level proficiency.

### Character Development
Participants complete 10 hours of community service annually, receive individual mentoring, and participate in cultural outings and travel experiences.

## School Partnerships

According to SquashDrive, the organization partners with 12 schools in the Oakland area; its primary school partner is Berkeley Maynard Academy (Aspire Public School in Oakland), where 70% of students receive free or reduced lunches.

## College and Scholarship Access

According to SquashDrive, the organization is part of the National Urban Squash Education Alliance (NUSEA), making participants eligible for the NAO/GO Scholarship Fund for private secondary school and college.

According to the US Squash LA28 page, the NAO/GO Scholarship Fund has historically awarded more than $91,400 in scholarships.

## How to Access

Contact SquashDrive directly through squashdrive.org for enrollment. The program partners with specific Oakland schools; enrollment typically starts through school partnerships.

## Common Mistakes

- **Assuming SquashDrive is open-enrollment for any Bay Area family.** SquashDrive's school-partnership model means eligibility is tied to attendance at a partner school, primarily Berkeley Maynard Academy and the broader Aspire network in Oakland. Families outside the partner school catchment should contact SquashDrive before expecting enrollment; do not assume your child qualifies based on geography or income alone.
- **Confusing SquashDrive with a recreational squash club.** SquashDrive provides a minimum of 100 hours per year of squash and fitness instruction and 115 hours per year of homework help and academic enrichment. This is an intensive after-school commitment integrating academics, squash, and character development — not a drop-in sports program. Families exploring casual recreational squash should look at [[oakwood-athletic-club]] or [[bay-club-squash]] instead.
- **Overlooking the college access pathway.** SquashDrive's NUSEA membership and NAO/GO Scholarship Fund access are significant long-term benefits that distinguish it from other programs. Parents of eligible students should understand this pathway early; see [[college-squash-recruiting]] for context on how urban squash programs connect to college admissions.
