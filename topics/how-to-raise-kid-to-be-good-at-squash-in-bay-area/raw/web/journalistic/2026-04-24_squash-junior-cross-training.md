# Strength & Agility Training for Junior Squash Players

## Key Training Components

### 1. Warm-up & Mobility
Dynamic routines including jumping jacks, arm circles, leg swings, lunges with rotation, and skipping rope.

### 2. Strength Training
"Functional strength for squash players":
- Lower body: squats, lunges, step-ups, calf raises, single-leg deadlifts
- Core: planks, Russian twists, leg raises, medicine ball work
- Upper body: push-ups, pull-ups, rows, kettlebell swings

### 3. Agility & Footwork
"Quick movements and rapid changes of direction are crucial in squash":
- Ladder drills
- Cone drills
- Ghosting (movement training without ball — closest simulation to actual squash demands)
- Shuttle runs
- Plyometric jumps

### 4. Cardiovascular Conditioning
- Interval sprints
- Rowing
- Skipping rope
- Court sprints
70% of conditioning work should take place on-court to maintain squash-specific foundation.

### 5. Flexibility & Recovery
Cool-down stretching and foam rolling prevent injuries and support muscle recovery.

## Ghosting as Training Method
Ghosting is movement training on the squash court without the ball, probably the closest simulation to actual demands of movement on the squash court without hitting. Two types:
- "Conditioning ghost" — endurance/fitness focus
- "Technical ghost" — movement pattern improvement

## What Other Sports Complement Squash
Cross-training exercises include:
- Skipping/jump rope: enhances footwork and timing
- Agility ladder drills: footwork speed
- Plyometrics: explosiveness
- Swimming: cardiovascular conditioning, low-impact
- Basketball/soccer: multi-directional movement patterns
- Running: general endurance base

Research on multi-sport participation: Multi-sport participation until puberty reduces injury risk, prevents burnout, and actually enhances long-term athletic development. Sports with similar movement demands (badminton, basketball, racquetball) transfer well to squash.

## Equipment for Cross-Training
Three simple pieces of equipment sufficient:
1. Jump rope (lightweight speed rope)
2. Agility/footwork ladder
3. Cones

## Training Philosophy
"Strength and conditioning enable squash players to perform at their peak" by improving efficiency, reducing wasted energy, increasing stamina while supporting injury prevention and mental resilience.

## Age-Appropriate Notes for Juniors
- Young players (under 12) should focus on movement patterns and fun over structured strength training
- Strength training appropriate from ~12-13+ with proper form guidance
- Gradual intensity increases critical to prevent overuse injuries
- Rest days are mandatory parts of any training program
