# Squash Technical Fundamentals from SquashSkills Blog

## Grip
The foundation of squash technique requires proper hand positioning:
- **V-Shape Formation**: "Place your thumb and forefinger in a V shape, resting on the open side of the racket"
- **Finger Control**: Position the grip in fingers rather than the palm for better maneuverability
- **Pressure Balance**: Hold with moderate firmness—"firm enough so it won't fly away, but not so tight that you crush it"
- Common error: Gripping too tightly reduces flexibility

## Forehand Technique
The forehand emphasizes fluid motion and timing:
- Maintain wrist position at approximately 45 degrees above horizontal
- Prepare the racket early upon anticipating ball direction
- Execute a flowing arm motion mimicking stone-skimming across water
- Drop elbow slightly during release for smooth execution
- Key mistake: Delayed racket preparation leads to rushed, inaccurate shots

## Backhand Technique
The backhand requires specific body alignment:
- Early racket preparation similar to forehand fundamentals
- "Form a diamond shape with your shoulder, elbow, wrist, and racket head"
- Rotate chest toward back wall before initiating swing
- Maintain proper alignment at contact point with all limbs aligned
- Follow through while controlling upper body rotation
- Common fault: Insufficient initial rotation causes excessive finish rotation

## Additional Elements
- **Follow-Through**: Direct the ball through controlled, purposeful extension aligned with target direction
- **Lower Body**: Use knees and hips as shock absorbers; execute proper lunges for stability and power
- **Integration**: Effective shots require coordinated whole-body mechanics, not isolated arm movement

## Footwork and Movement
- Always return to the T position (center of court) after every shot
- Stand with feet about shoulder-width apart; bend knees slightly
- Split-step on opponent's swing to maximize reaction time
- Ghosting (moving around court without ball) is key training tool

## Beginner Focus Areas
Beginners should focus on grip, stance, and learning basic shots like forehands and backhands. Five core skills: grip, serve, footwork, drives, and drop shots form the backbone of the game. As players improve, advanced techniques include volleys, boasts, and deception.

## Progression Stages
- **Stage 1 (Beginner)**: Grip, basic forehand/backhand, court awareness, safety
- **Stage 2 (Intermediate)**: Footwork patterns, T-position return, length game, serve/return
- **Stage 3 (Advanced)**: Tactical play, deception, volleys, boasts, mental game, physical conditioning
