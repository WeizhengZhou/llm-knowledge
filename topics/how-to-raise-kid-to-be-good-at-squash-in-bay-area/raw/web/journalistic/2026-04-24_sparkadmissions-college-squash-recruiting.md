# How to Get Recruited to College for Squash

## College Squash Is a (Small) Numbers Game

In the world of college squash, the competition is fierce due to the limited availability of roster spots. Varsity college squash teams are relatively small, typically consisting of 10 to 20 players. As a result, college coaches may recruit only one or two players per year, making every spot highly coveted.

There are only 37 colleges and universities in the United States with varsity college squash teams, compared to over 650 schools with NCAA football programs. This limited pool of programs means that squash recruiting is not only competitive but also exclusive. High school student athletes who want to play college squash at the varsity level need to be among the best in the nation to catch the attention of college coaches.

## Academic Factors in College Squash Recruiting

College squash is unique in that many of the schools with varsity squash programs are Ivy League universities and top-tier Division III institutions. Neither Ivy League schools nor Division III schools offer athletic scholarships, meaning that student athletes are often competing for admission based on both their athletic and academic achievements.

Aspiring squash players must maintain a high GPA and excel on standardized tests to meet the academic standards of these institutions. Being an outstanding squash player can enhance chances of admission, but it's not a "back door" into these competitive schools. Coaches and admissions officers will expect recruits to meet the same academic benchmarks as other students admitted to the institution.

Recruits will typically apply through Early Decision or Early Action to demonstrate the strength of their interest in the school.

## Preparing for College Squash Recruitment

### Build Your Ranking
One of the most critical factors in getting recruited for college squash is your ranking on the U.S. Squash leaderboard. College coaches heavily rely on these rankings to evaluate potential recruits. Regular participation in tournaments will not only improve skills but also help climb the rankings.

For international players, being ranked at the top of your country's national squash standings is equally important. Coaches are looking for players who consistently compete at a high level and demonstrate the ability to win under pressure.

### Play in the Right Tournaments
To maximize chances of recruitment, aim to participate in high-profile squash tournaments. Events such as the U.S. Junior Open, British Junior Open, and regional championships are excellent opportunities to showcase skills. These tournaments often attract collegiate level coaches scouting for talent.

Before attending a tournament, research which coaches will be present and send them an email introducing yourself.

### Network with College Coaches
Building relationships with college coaches is a vital part of the recruiting process. Start by researching squash teams at schools of interest and reaching out to coaches. Most colleges have recruitment forms on their athletics websites — fill these out and include athletic resume, highlight recruitment video, and academic transcripts.

### Balance Academics and Athletics
Schools with varsity squash teams expect recruits to demonstrate discipline, time management, and commitment to studies. Prioritize coursework, maintain strong GPA, prepare thoroughly for standardized tests, consider AP/honors courses.

## The Role of Club Squash Teams
Playing for a competitive club squash team is often a prerequisite for recruitment. Club teams provide high-level coaching, frequent practice sessions, and opportunities to compete in national and international tournaments. Many top junior players hone their skills through club teams, as high school squash programs may not offer the same level of competition or exposure.

## Exploring Non-Varsity Options
Many colleges and universities also offer club squash programs, providing a competitive yet more flexible environment.
