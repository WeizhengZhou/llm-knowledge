# Squash Injuries in Junior Players — Prevention and Safety

## Eye Injuries: The Primary Risk

### Magnitude of Risk
- Squash ball at 150 KPH = same energy impact as .22 bullet
- UK Royal Society for the Prevention of Accidents: ~2,000 eye injuries/year from squash balls; ~2,400 from racquets
- Classified as "high" injury risk by American Academies of Ophthalmology and Pediatrics, American Optometric Association

### Prevention: Mandatory Eyeguards
- **US Squash rule:** All players (and coaches) MUST wear protective eyewear in ALL US Squash accredited events
- Applies to juniors in ALL sanctioned events — no exceptions
- World Squash Federation: mandatory eyewear recommendation for all juniors since 1996
- Standard required: ASTM-F3164 certified eyeguards
- Regular eyeglasses are NOT safe
- Open eyeguards (no lenses) are NOT safe
- Estimated prevention: "almost totally preventable with appropriate protective equipment"

### Eyeguard Cost
- Budget: ~$20
- Mid-range: $35-50
- Premium: $50-100

## Other Common Squash Injuries

### Lower Body
- **Ankle sprains**: Most common; result of sudden direction changes
- **Knee injuries**: Patellofemoral syndrome from repetitive court movement
- **Achilles tendon**: Strain from explosive pushing off movements
- Prevention: Proper court shoes (squash-specific), warm-up, gradual training load increases

### Upper Body
- **Wrist strain**: From ball impact, especially backhand shots
- **Shoulder overuse**: In higher-volume training programs
- Prevention: Proper technique (coach-supervised), not gripping racket too tight

### General Overuse
- Stress fractures in growing bones — high concern in adolescents
- Prevention: Rest days, gradual load increases, cross-training

## Court Collision Risks
- Squash courts are small; two players moving at speed
- Etiquette rules (let/stroke system) designed to manage collision risk
- Referees enforce these rules; juniors are taught them as part of ranking exam

## Injury Prevention Checklist for Parents
1. Ensure certified ASTM-F3164 eyeguards are worn at all times on court
2. Invest in squash-specific court shoes (not running shoes)
3. Warm up properly before each session
4. Work with certified coach to establish correct technique early
5. Build training load gradually — no sudden volume spikes
6. Ensure adequate rest days
7. Take 4-12 weeks rest if overtraining symptoms appear
8. Cross-train rather than squash-only for developing players

## Comparison: Squash vs Tennis Injury Profile
- Squash: Higher acute injury risk (eye injuries, collision, sudden stops)
- Tennis: Higher chronic overuse injury risk (tennis elbow, shoulder)
- Both sports safer than contact sports
- Squash eye injuries essentially eliminated with proper eyeguards
