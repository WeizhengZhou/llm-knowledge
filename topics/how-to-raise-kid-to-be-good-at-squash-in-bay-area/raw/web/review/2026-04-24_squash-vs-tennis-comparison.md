# Squash vs Tennis vs Pickleball Comparison

## Game Style & Pace
- **Squash**: "Fast rallies, quick reactions, constant movement"
- **Tennis**: "Longer points, open court coverage, mix of baseline and net play"
- **Pickleball**: "Slower pace, easier rallies, social doubles play"

## Fitness Benefits
| Sport | Calories/Hour | Key Benefits |
|-------|---------------|--------------|
| Squash | 600–900 | Cardio, agility, mental focus |
| Tennis | 400–700 | Endurance, strength, coordination |
| Pickleball | Lower | Agility, reflexes (lighter cardio) |

## Best For (Player Types)
- **Squash**: Players who love intensity, quick bursts of energy, and year-round indoor play
- **Tennis**: Players who enjoy outdoor competition, strategy, and high-profile sports culture
- **Pickleball**: Families, casual athletes, and seniors

## Cost Comparison (from multiple sources)
- **Squash**: Higher club/court costs in Bay Area; fewer public courts than tennis
- **Tennis**: More public/free courts; wider coaching options; more tournaments
- **Pickleball**: Lowest barrier to entry; rapidly growing availability

## Access & Infrastructure (Bay Area)
- Tennis has most extensive infrastructure (public parks, USTA NorCal programs)
- Squash has ~15-20 court locations in the Bay Area, all private/club
- Pickleball rapidly expanding in parks and recreation centers

## Learning Curve
- Squash: Faster early skill development (smaller court, easier to get rallies going)
- Tennis: Harder initial learning (open court, service complexity)
- Pickleball: Easiest initial entry

## Injury Risk
- Squash: Eye injury risk (mandatory eyeguards for juniors in all sanctioned events); sudden stops/starts; wall collision risk
- Tennis: Tennis elbow; knee/hip stress over decades
- Pickleball: Generally lower injury rates; some Achilles/ankle risk

## Eye Injuries in Squash
According to the Royal Society for the Prevention of Accidents: ~2,000 eye injuries per year from squash balls, ~2,400 from racquets. However, nearly all preventable with ASTM-F3164 certified eyeguards. US Squash mandates eyeguards for ALL juniors in all sanctioned events.

## College Opportunities
- Squash: 37 varsity programs (all elite schools: Ivy League, top D3); ~60 spots per gender per year; no athletic scholarships
- Tennis: 900+ NCAA programs; D1 scholarships available; far more spots
- Pickleball: No varsity programs (emerging)

## Which to Choose for Kids
Choose squash if:
- Living near a squash club (Bay Area has sufficient access)
- Want high-intensity interval fitness
- College admissions pathway to elite schools is a goal
- Want faster early skill development

Choose tennis if:
- Want lower-impact long-term sport
- Want more local training partners
- Want outdoor/social play
- Want more tournament volume

## Squash Fitness vs Tennis Fitness
Squash players need fitness equivalent to 1500-meter runner; tennis players need endurance of 10,000-meter runner. Squash is more high-intensity interval in nature.
