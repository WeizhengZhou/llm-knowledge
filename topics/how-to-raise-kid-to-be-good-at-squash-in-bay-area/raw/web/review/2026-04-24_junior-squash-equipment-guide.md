# Junior Squash Equipment Guide

## Racket Size Selection by Age

**Under 8 years:**
- Frame length: 23 inches
- Weight: ~180g
- Recommended: Karakal CSX 60 Junior — "bullet-proof starter frame" for beginners
- Best for: New players still developing swing control

**8-11 years:**
- Frame length: 25 inches
- Weight: 120-150g unstrung
- Options: Tecnifibre Carboflex X-Top Junior V2 (150g) or Dunlop Hyperfibre XT Revelation Junior (120-125g, lightest)

**12+ years or 150cm+ (5ft) tall:**
- Can transition to full-length frames
- Should remain ≤130g unstrung until strength and technique are proven
- Transition to adult frames typically 8-11 years old depending on size

## Racket Price Points
- Most junior squash rackets: ~$50
- Full-size rackets for juniors: under $120

## String Tension
- Juniors need "factory multifilament around 20-24 lbs" (lower than adult tension)

## Eyeguards (MANDATORY for Juniors)
- **US Squash rule:** ALL juniors must wear protective eyewear at all US Squash accredited events
- Must meet or exceed ASTM-F3164 standard
- Regular eyeglasses NOT safe for squash
- Open eyeguards (no lenses) NOT safe for squash
- Cost: approximately $20-40 for basic certified eyeguards
- Risk: Squash ball at 150 KPH has same energy impact as .22 bullet; ~2,000 eye injuries per year from squash balls (ROSPA data)

## Squash Balls
- **Introductory/junior balls**: Yellow or blue dot (bouncier, slower, suitable for beginners and young players)
- **Intermediate**: White or red dot
- **Advanced**: Single yellow dot or double yellow dot (least bouncy, used in adult play)
- Junior-specific balls: ProPenn Mi Ball, Dunlop Progress series

## Court Shoes
- Squash-specific non-marking court shoes required at most clubs
- Should not use running shoes (wrong grip/ankle support)
- Budget: $50-$120 for youth court shoes (Asics, Salming, Head brands offer squash-specific)

## Upgrade Frequency
- Rackets: upgrade approximately every 2-3 years as player grows (size changes) or skill level advances
- Shoes: replace when worn (sole wears down, typically 1-2 years of regular play)
- Eyeguards: replace if damaged; otherwise last several years
- Balls: replace when worn/cracked; a tube lasts weeks to months depending on play frequency
