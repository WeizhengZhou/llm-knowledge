# Critic Report — Injury Prevention and Safety — 2026-04-24

## Overall Assessment
This guide covers the non-negotiable safety content (eyeguard mandate, ASTM standard) with appropriate urgency and provides a useful injury type catalog and prevention checklist. Its main weakness is missing a Common Mistakes section and being thin on court-specific collision guidance that parents actually need when their child first enters competitive play.

## Must-Fix Before wiki/ Graduation

1. **No Common Mistakes section.** Guide articles require one. Non-obvious mistakes to add: (a) Allowing a child to wear regular glasses instead of certified squash eyeguards — the article notes "regular eyeglasses and open eyeguards are not safe substitutes" but does not name this as a common parent error. It is the most common mistake. (b) Using running shoes instead of squash-specific court shoes — the article mentions "squash-specific court shoes" in the prevention checklist but not as a mistake. Running shoes lack lateral support for the side-to-side squash movement pattern and increase ankle sprain risk; this is a frequent first-session error. (c) Letting the child skip warm-up because the session is short — short junior clinics (45 minutes) often tempt parents and coaches to skip warm-up; this is when ankle and Achilles injuries most commonly occur.

## Should-Fix

1. **"The let/stroke system is designed to manage collision risk" — the court collision section assumes the parent knows what a let or stroke is.** Add two sentences: "A 'let' is a replay of the point when a player's path to the ball is blocked by their opponent; a 'stroke' awards a point when the obstruction was avoidable. Juniors learn these rules as part of the referee exam requirement. This system means that players call for a let rather than forcing a collision — it is the primary safety mechanism in a shared court."

2. **The Cross-Training section duplicates development pathway content.** Remove the three Onyx Squash Academy paragraphs from this article (they are already in [[development-pathway]]) and replace with: "Multi-sport participation until puberty reduces squash-specific overuse injuries; see [[development-pathway]] — Cross-Training section for the recommended 70/30 on-court/off-court split and age-appropriate strength training guidelines."

3. **The ROSPA statistics ("squash ball at 150 KPH, comparable to a .22 bullet")** are from a UK safety organization. These are striking but their provenance as UK statistics should be stated: "According to the UK Royal Society for the Prevention of Accidents (ROSPA)..."
