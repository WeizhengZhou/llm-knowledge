## Should-Fix

1. **RO3 must_answer "What equipment does my child need and how often do they upgrade?" is half-answered.** The starter kit breakdown is present, but there is no guidance on upgrade frequency. Add: "Junior rackets typically last 1-2 seasons at the recreational level, but competitive players may replace them annually. Shoes wear out faster than expected on squash courts — plan for replacement every 6-12 months at the competitive level. Eyeguards should be replaced if cracked or if the lens becomes scratched and impairs vision."
2. **RO3 must_answer "How do families balance squash training with school?" is not answered in this article.** Add a pointer sentence in the Time Commitment section: "For guidance on managing school schedules, tournament travel, and academic balance, see [[development-pathway]] — Balancing Squash with School section."
3. **The competitive cost breakdown uses Squash Zone private lesson rates ($145-175/hr) as the basis for the $7,540-$9,100 private lesson line item** but does not show the arithmetic. Add: "Private lesson calculation assumes 1 lesson/week × 52 weeks × $145-175/hr = $7,540-$9,100/year." This is important because Bay Area competitive families who use Bay Club or HISA coaches at different rates will get different totals.
4. **The "Cost Context vs. Other Youth Sports" table is useful but unsourced.** The youth basketball and club soccer figures appear to be editorial estimates. Label them "approximate figures" or cite the source.
