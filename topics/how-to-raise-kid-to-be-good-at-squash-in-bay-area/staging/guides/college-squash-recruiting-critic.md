## Should-Fix

1. **RO6 must_answer "What have Bay Area junior squash players achieved in college placement?" is not answered.** The article establishes that squash is an admissions advantage nationally but gives no Bay Area-specific examples. Add a short subsection ("Bay Area College Placement Examples") noting: the NorCal notable achievers from [[norcal-squash]] and whether any went on to college squash; that JCT #2 in Fremont is specifically the West Coast visibility event where college coaches see Bay Area players; and that the geographic disadvantage (fewer training partners, East Coast-centric talent pool) means Bay Area juniors who do reach top-20 nationally have demonstrated exceptional commitment. If no specific Bay Area college placement data is available, state this explicitly rather than omitting the must_answer item.

2. **The Harvard acceptance rate data (20x more likely, 83% for athletes rated 1-2) is attributed to xfactoradmissions.com citing OpenAdmits/Harvard data.** This is L2 confidence (single aggregator source). The current attribution ("Note: these figures are Harvard-specific and should not be generalized to all Ivy League schools") is appropriate but does not say how old the data is. Add: "(These figures are drawn from a study of historical Harvard admissions data; current admissions rates may differ.)"

3. **"Nearly 90% of Ivy League squash players attended private high schools" (xfactoradmissions.com, L2).** This is a striking socioeconomic claim that could discourage public school families. Add a forward-facing note: "This does not mean public school players cannot reach Ivy squash — it reflects the historical feeder-school structure, which Bay Area players can partially offset through programs like JCT #2 in Fremont and nationally-ranked tournament play."
