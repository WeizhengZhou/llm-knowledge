## Should-Fix

1. **RO1 must_answer "vs tennis, badminton, or other racket sports" — badminton is absent.** Add a short paragraph or table row for badminton: Bay Area badminton infrastructure is extensive (many public courts, community centers, large Asian-American player base); badminton shares squash's enclosed-court intensity but is played on much larger indoor courts with a very different movement pattern; badminton does not have a college varsity pathway comparable to squash. This addition directly addresses a real P1 parent consideration in Bay Area demographics.

2. **The "When to Choose Squash" and "When to Choose Tennis" lists do not address the child already playing tennis.** Add: "If your child already plays tennis and is considering squash, know that grip technique differs significantly (squash uses a continental grip with wrist snap; the muscle memory is not directly portable). Older tennis converts (13+) can make the switch relatively quickly; younger children are more flexible. The court/fitness benefits of squash can complement tennis training in the off-season."

3. **The college value section duplicates and re-litigates the program count dispute.** Replace the second paragraph of the College Value section (which repeats the 34/31 vs 37 dispute) with: "For full recruiting details including ranking requirements and timeline, see [[college-squash-recruiting]]." Keep only the comparison-relevant insight: squash offers a concentrated advantage due to the small nationally ranked pool.

4. **No Common Mistakes section.** While not hard-required for concept articles, this article's decision purpose benefits from one. Non-obvious mistakes: (a) Assuming squash is cheaper because court time is shared — squash club membership is required in the Bay Area (no public courts), making even recreational squash more expensive per hour than public-court tennis. (b) Switching from tennis to squash expecting skill transfer — squash and tennis grips and swing mechanics are different; parents should not expect a competitive tennis player to immediately produce competitive squash.
