# Squash vs Tennis

## Definition

A comparison of squash and tennis as youth sports for Bay Area families, covering fitness, cost, accessibility, college value, and development speed. This is the most common decision point for parents considering racket sports.

## Head-to-Head Comparison

| Factor | Squash | Tennis |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Fitness intensity | Higher (interval-based, ~600-900 cal/hr) | Moderate (~400-700 cal/hr) |
| Learning curve | Faster early skill development (smaller court, easier rallies) | Harder initial entry (open court, service complexity) |
| Bay Area access | Limited; all courts at private clubs/membership facilities | Extensive; public parks, USTA NorCal programs |
| Annual cost (competitive) | $10,000-20,000+ | $5,000-15,000 |
| Annual cost (recreational) | $2,000-4,000 | Lower (public courts available) |
| College programs | 34 men's + 31 women's varsity (2025-26) | 900+ NCAA programs |
| Athletic scholarships | None (Ivy/D3 only) | Available at D1 programs |
| Weather dependency | None (indoor year-round) | Moderate (outdoor courts affected) |
| Injury profile | Higher acute risk (eye injuries, collision) | Higher chronic risk (tennis elbow, shoulder) |

## Fitness

According to my-squash.com, squash burns approximately 600-900 calories per hour compared to 400-700 for tennis; actual figures depend on individual, intensity, and session type.

Squash players need fitness equivalent to a 1500-meter runner (high-intensity interval); tennis players need endurance of a 10,000-meter runner (sustained effort).

## Access in the Bay Area

Tennis has the most extensive infrastructure in the Bay Area, with public parks, free courts, and USTA NorCal programs widely available.

The exact number of Bay Area squash court locations is not definitively documented. NorCal Squash references 20+ facilities; an L3 aggregator estimates 15-20 locations. All known Bay Area squash courts are at private clubs or membership facilities. There are no public squash courts in the Bay Area.

## College Value

Squash offers a concentrated college advantage: very small nationally ranked pools (approximately 142 BU19 and 104 GU19 players) competing for spots at elite schools. Tennis offers far more programs (900+) and D1 scholarship opportunities, but with correspondingly more competition.

For the 2025-2026 season, the College Squash Association lists 34 men's and 31 women's varsity programs. Some sources cite 37 total programs, which may reflect an earlier season or different count methodology.

Ivy League and Division III schools do not offer athletic scholarships for squash. Tennis has D1 scholarship programs available.

## When to Choose Squash

- Living near a squash club with junior programming
- Want high-intensity interval fitness (indoor, year-round)
- College admissions pathway to elite schools (Ivy/D3) is a goal
- Want faster early skill development
- Child enjoys fast-paced, enclosed-court play

## When to Choose Tennis

- Want lower per-year cost and public court access
- Want more local training partners and tournament volume
- Want outdoor/social play
- Want D1 athletic scholarship eligibility
- Broader geographic flexibility (courts everywhere)
