# After-schooling plan for motivated math kid

## Post 1 - jckdw (09/19/17)
Parent describes a 9-year-old who has self-taught through high school geometry via library books. The child's 8th-grade brother in Algebra 2 thinks the younger one knows comparable math. Child dislikes AoPS pre-algebra contest problems, preferring topics like "graph theory, complex numbers, formal logic, game theory, different dimensions, probability, topology." Self-directed interests include "logic gates with dominoes and finding the volume of polyhedra." The child wants to learn calculus and prove Goldbach's conjecture.

Parent noted knowledge gaps--the child understood the quadratic formula's purpose but couldn't factor until taught. Parent seeks systematic teaching while maintaining engagement.

## Post 2 - aeh (09/22/17)
Recommends Singapore secondary curriculum (Discovering Math/Dimensions Math), progressing through algebra II/trig, followed by Discovering Additional Math reaching univariate calculus. Suggests MIT OpenCourseWare.

## Post 3 - BenjaminL (09/22/17)
Suggests Elements of Mathematics and eimacs. Points out difference between AoPS Alcumus (online problems) and textbooks, which are inquiry-based and formal.

## Post 5 - jckdw (09/22/17)
Child enjoyed Julia Robinson problems. Math circles weren't satisfying; the child "talked too much and had to be shushed," and would benefit from older peers (mentions unaffordable epsilon camp). Child solves some AoPS challenge problems but isn't especially strong. Prefers learning from "Life of Fred" and "Cartoon Guide to Whatever" and popular math books. Motivated by entertainment value and feelings of achievement.

## Post 6 - Quantum2003 (09/25/17)
Encourages revisiting AoPS later with actual courses, especially Algebra 2, Geometry, Algebra 3, Pre-Calculus.

## Post 10 - jckdw (10/02/17)
Plans to identify appropriate AoPS level and retry. Notes child enjoys programming, having written JavaScript programs for mathematical purposes. Child's first proof involved rectangles demonstrating "(x+y)(x-y)=x^2-y^2" around age 6-7, noting the child is "very diagrammy."

### Trajectory Data Points
- Age 6-7: First mathematical proof (visual, rectangles)
- Age 9: Self-taught through high school geometry via library books
- Age 9: Interests include graph theory, complex numbers, logic, game theory, topology
- Age 9: Writing JavaScript programs for mathematical purposes
- Age 9: Comparable to 8th-grade brother in Algebra 2
- Curricula: Life of Fred, popular math books, Julia Robinson problems, AoPS (planned)
