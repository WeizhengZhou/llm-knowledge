# Is it really that unusual?

## Post 1 - Tallulah (11/05/10)
Kindergartener showing advanced math ability. Teacher (30+ years) claims math advancement is uncommon--typically sees advanced readers, not mathematicians.

## Post 2 - Grinity (11/05/10)
Framework: calculus/algebra in 1st grade = unusual; 4th-grade math in 1st grade = not unusual for gifted with exposure; 2nd-grade math in 1st = expected for ~15% of students.

## Post 3 - NCPMom (11/05/10)
Son (age 7, 2nd grade): attends 3rd-grade math 4x weekly, scored 96% 1st quarter, top group of ~60 2nd-graders.

## Post 6 - crisc (11/05/10)
Son (age 7): algebra concepts by pre-K/K/1st. WJ-III at age 5y1m showed grade 3 equivalents in computation. Minimal formal math instruction since age 5.

## Post 12 - kimck (11/07/10)
Son as preschooler demonstrated conceptual multiplication/division. Now in 4th grade doing algebra after intentional pacing delays.

## Post 13 - Mathboy (11/11/10)
Son (age 4): discussing square roots, reading/multiplication/division knowledge. Teacher initially misidentified as slow-thinking.

## Post 14 - Mathboy (11/11/10)
Son invented alternative long-division method (845/5 as 850/5-1 rather than standard algorithm).

## Post 15 - CourtneyB (11/12/10)
Son (age 6): completed 2nd-3rd grade math in kindergarten. Daughter (age 5): capable of 1st-grade math in K.
