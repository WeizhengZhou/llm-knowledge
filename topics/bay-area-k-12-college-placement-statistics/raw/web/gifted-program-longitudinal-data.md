# Longitudinal Gifted/Talent Development Programs: Research Summary

Research conducted 2026-04-18. Covers 10 programs that track or have tracked students from elementary/middle school through college and beyond. For each program: what longitudinal trajectory data exists, whether it is individual-level or aggregate, whether papers are published or just program descriptions, volume, years covered, and access method.

---

## 1. SMPY — Study of Mathematically Precocious Youth (Johns Hopkins / Vanderbilt)

### Program Description
- Founded September 1, 1971 by Julian Stanley at Johns Hopkins University
- Moved to Iowa State University 1986, then to Vanderbilt University (Peabody College) 1998
- Currently co-directed by Camilla P. Benbow and David Lubinski at Vanderbilt
- SMPY informed the creation of Johns Hopkins CTY (Center for Talented Youth)

### Identification Method and Age
- Participants identified by age 13, primarily in 7th–8th grade (ages 12–13)
- Selection criterion: SAT score of 700+ (out of 800) on either mathematics or verbal section, taken before age 13 — an "above-level" test since the SAT is designed for 17-year-olds
- Ability ranges represented: from top 3% down to top 0.01% in quantitative or verbal reasoning
- Selection deliberately done at middle school age, not elementary school, because "regression to the mean effects are lessened" at that developmental stage
- NO K-8 elementary school data collected on participants prior to identification — the study begins at identification (age 12–13)

### Cohort Structure (5 Cohorts)
- Cohort 1: Identified 1972–1974 (707 males, 452 females — ~1,159 total)
- Cohort 2: Identified 1976–1978 (intellectually talented 13-year-olds)
- Cohort 3: Identified 1980–1983
- Cohort 4: Identified by age 13 through talent searches (specific years not confirmed in sources)
- Cohort 5: 714 first- or second-year graduate students from top U.S. math/science programs, identified in 1992
- Total across study: more than 5,000 intellectually talented individuals identified over 25-year period (1972–1997)

### Follow-Up Structure and Data Points
- Follow-ups conducted at 5, 10, 20, and 35-year intervals after identification
- Additional follow-ups at ages 18, 23, 33, 50 (McCabe et al., 2020 — age 50 follow-up for Cohort 5), and an age-65 follow-up currently planned
- Five-year follow-up participation rate: over 91% (1,996 of 2,188 in Cohort 1)
- Data tracked across follow-ups:
  - High school course-taking, grades, standardized test scores
  - College enrollment (including which institutions)
  - Major selection, early graduation rates, graduate school attendance
  - Career development and occupational outcomes
  - Personal relationships and life satisfaction
  - Cognitive and personality assessments
  - Health and well-being measures
  - Publications, patents, creative works

### Published Output
- Seven books and over 400 articles published based on SMPY data
- Key published papers:
  - Lubinski & Benbow (1994): "The First Three Decades" — 1972–1994 overview
  - Lubinski & Benbow (2006): "Study of Mathematically Precocious Youth After 35 Years: Uncovering Antecedents for the Development of Math-Science Expertise" — Perspectives on Psychological Science — covers 20-year follow-ups for Cohorts 1, 2, 3 and 5- or 10-year findings for all five cohorts
  - Kell et al. (2013): Midlife follow-up findings
  - Lubinski et al. (2014): Midlife follow-up — Psychological Science Vol. 25(12) pp. 2217–2232
  - McCabe et al. (2020): Age-50 follow-up of math/science graduate students (Cohort 5)
  - Lubinski et al. (2023): Additional midlife follow-up (funding document for age-65 follow-up)
  - Benbow, Lubinski, Shea & Eftekhari-Sanjani (2000): Cohort 1, 2, 3 findings through age 33
- Many recent articles available as PDFs on Benbow and Lubinski's individual Vanderbilt web pages

### Key Findings Relevant to Trajectory Research
- Talented youth pursue careers aligned with their cognitive profile (math-heavy → STEM; verbal-heavy → humanities/law)
- Sex differences observed in career trajectories among high-ability youth
- High spatial ability, investigative interests, and theoretical values (beyond math scores alone) predict exceptional STEM careers
- SAT-M scores at age 12–13 predict outcomes 35–50 years later with remarkable reliability
- Those stronger in math vs. verbal entered science and engineering at higher rates

### K-8 Trajectory Data Availability
- NONE. SMPY begins at identification, which is age 12–13 (7th–8th grade)
- No data on what participants were doing in elementary school (grades K-6)
- No data on early childhood assessments, preschool programs, or elementary activities
- The study captures from middle school forward only
- This is a fundamental design choice: Stanley believed regression to the mean made earlier identification unreliable

### Individual-Level vs. Aggregate
- Individual-level data exists in the research database but is NOT publicly available
- Published papers report aggregate findings, not individual participant data
- Anonymized data not publicly released; access requires formal research collaboration with Vanderbilt

### How to Access
- Published papers: gwern.net/smpy hosts a comprehensive bibliography with links to many PDFs
- Vanderbilt SMPY website: https://my.vanderbilt.edu/smpy/
- Bibliography contact: rachel.a.morgan@vanderbilt.edu
- David Lubinski's publication list: https://my.vanderbilt.edu/smpy/publications/david-lubinski/
- Camilla Benbow's publication list: https://my.vanderbilt.edu/smpy/publications/camilla-benbow/
- Julian Stanley archival materials: University of North Texas Julian C. Stanley archive
- ERIC database contains some early publications
- For raw/anonymized data: contact Vanderbilt directly; no public data release known

### Assessment
SMPY is the most important longitudinal dataset for gifted development, but its identification point (age 12–13) means it captures no K-8 elementary trajectory data. It is the gold standard for middle school → adulthood trajectories. Individual-level data is gated behind Vanderbilt research access. Aggregate findings in 400+ published papers are freely accessible via gwern.net and Google Scholar.

## 2. CTY Study of Exceptional Talent (SET) — Johns Hopkins

### Program Description
- CTY (Center for Talented Youth) founded 1979 by Julian Stanley at Johns Hopkins
- SET (Study of Exceptional Talent) is CTY's longitudinal research arm, an extension of SMPY
- CTY Talent Search operates for students grades 2–8 using above-grade-level tests (SAT, ACT, SCAT, STB)
- Serves students ages 6 to 17 in enrichment programs
- Total historical scale: over 1.5 million students participated in CTY's Talent Search as of 2016; ~28,000 program enrollments annually; over 165,000 CTY alumni worldwide

### Identification and Age for SET
- SET specifically tracks students who score 700–800 on SAT-Mathematics or SAT-Verbal before age 13 (top 1 in 10,000 ability level)
- This is a subset of CTY Talent Search participants — the most extreme performers

### SET Longitudinal Data
- SET has tracked over 5,000 intellectually talented participants across multiple cohorts since 1971 (combined with SMPY)
- One published follow-up tracked N=320 students identified before age 13 over 10 years (Brody, 2005 — Linda E. Brody, "The Study of Exceptional Talent," High Ability Studies, Vol. 16, No. 1, 2005)
  - Result: pursued doctoral degrees at rates 50 times base-rate expectations
  - Several participants had created noteworthy literary, scientific, or technical products by their early 20s
- Blackburn and Brody (1996): "The Study of Exceptional Talent: A longitudinal study" — Technical Report No. 24, Johns Hopkins Center for Talented Youth

### Published Recent Research (2026)
- Helal, Parra-Martinez, Djita, Wai, McKenzie (2026): "Gifted Students' Later Outcomes: College-Going Rates and Selectivity" — Gifted Child Quarterly, DOI: 10.1177/00169862251350155
  - This is the most recent published longitudinal outcome study connecting talent search identification to college outcomes
  - Full text gated behind Sage paywall

### K-8 Trajectory Data Availability
- CTY Talent Search begins as early as grade 2 (age 7–8) — this is the youngest identification point of any major program surveyed
- Students who participate starting at age 7 and continue through multiple years of CTY summer programs represent a potential 5+ year longitudinal trajectory within the program
- However, CTY does NOT publish individual multi-year participation data showing student progression year-by-year
- No publicly available dataset of "student X participated in CTY age 7, 8, 9, 10, 11" format
- Aggregate data exists within CTY's research files

### Notable Alumni
- Sergey Brin (Google co-founder)
- Mark Zuckerberg (Facebook founder)
- Lady Gaga
- Terence Tao (Fields Medal)

### How to Access
- SET research program: https://cty.jhu.edu/testing/study-exceptional-talent-set
- Published papers via ERIC, Google Scholar, and Tandfonline
- Brody 2005 paper: https://www.epfl.ch/education/education-and-science-outreach/wp-content/uploads/2019/08/Brody_StudyExceptionalTalent2005.pdf
- 2026 paper: https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00169862251350155 (gated)

### Assessment
CTY/SET has the youngest identification age of any program (grade 2, age 7) and the longest potential individual trajectory window (grade 2 through high school graduation). Published longitudinal data exists but is sparse — one major 10-year study of 320 students. The most recent 2026 paper focuses on college-going rates. Raw multi-year participation data is internal to CTY and not publicly released.

## 3. Duke TIP (Talent Identification Program)

### Program Description
- Founded 1981 at Duke University
- Operated talent searches for grades 4–6 and grade 7 (using SAT/ACT for young students)
- Over 40-year history: benefited over 3 million students, ~100,000+ applying per year

### Programs Offered
- Grades 4–6: Academic Adventures, eInvestigators, CRISIS
- Grades 7–10: Summer Studies, Field Studies, Scholar Weekends, eStudies
- Eligibility: 95th percentile on standardized tests, or 125+ IQ

### Program Closure
- Programs suspended in 2020–2021 due to COVID-19 pandemic
- Summer Studies and Academic Talent Search PERMANENTLY CANCELLED beginning 2022
- Students redirected to Duke Continuing Studies from 2022 onward
- Alumni network maintained at: https://alumni.tip.duke.edu/

### Longitudinal Data Published
- Research has been conducted on TIP participants (Wikipedia references published research but without specific citations)
- No specific published longitudinal college-placement datasets identified in open sources
- ResearchGate lists: "The Duke University Talent Identification Program" (publication, no full text available without access)
- Duke TIP's closure means no new longitudinal data will be generated

### K-8 Trajectory Data Availability
- Grades 4–6 identification is K-8 adjacent (ages 9–11)
- No publicly available dataset showing individual student trajectories from grade 4 through college
- Program closure in 2022 makes future data collection impossible

### How to Access
- Wikipedia article: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talent_Identification_Program
- ResearchGate publication entry: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/262888807_The_Duke_University_Talent_Identification_Program
- Alumni network: https://alumni.tip.duke.edu/

### Assessment
Duke TIP is now defunct (officially ended 2022). Whatever longitudinal data was generated is archival and held internally at Duke. No significant published dataset has been identified in open sources. The grade 4–6 identification window (ages 9–11) is closer to the K-8 range than SMPY's age-13 floor. Lowest research value of the academic talent search programs due to closure and lack of published outcomes.

## 4. Davidson Institute — Young Scholars and Davidson Fellows

### Young Scholars Program
- Accepts students ages 5–18 with "profound giftedness" in the United States
- More than 4,000 Young Scholars currently enrolled nationally
- Students receive personalized counseling, educational advocacy, and connection to resources
- At age 18, Young Scholars transition to Alumni program
- NO published formal longitudinal outcome studies tracking Young Scholar alumni trajectories
- Available data: individual anecdotal testimonials (e.g., alumni reporting PhD completions)
- One relevant paper hosted on davidsongifted.org: "Exceptionally Gifted Children: Long-Term Outcomes of Academic Acceleration and Nonacceleration" — discusses external research, not Davidson-specific data

### Davidson Fellows Scholarship
- Awarded annually to students 18 and under who complete a significant original project
- Three tiers: $100,000, $50,000, and $25,000
- Scholarship has run since 2001; past Fellows listed at davidsongifted.org by year (2001–2025)
- Published information per Fellow: name, location, project category (Science, Engineering, Technology, Mathematics, Literature, Music, Outside the Box)
- Individual profile pages exist for each Fellow with project descriptions
- No age/grade-level data published in directory format
- No "where are they now" alumni tracking published in aggregate form
- CEE.org press release documents overlap: several RSI alumni are also Davidson Fellows

### K-8 Trajectory Data Availability
- Young Scholars accepts from age 5 — potentially the youngest entry point of any program
- No individual-level longitudinal data published
- No aggregate outcomes report published (e.g., "X% of Young Scholars attended Ivy League institutions")
- To access any outcome data: direct contact with Davidson Institute required

### How to Access
- Young Scholars: https://www.davidsongifted.org/gifted-programs/young-scholars/
- Past Fellows directory: https://www.davidsongifted.org/gifted-programs/fellows-scholarship/fellows/current-and-past-fellows/
- 2025 Fellows: https://www.davidsongifted.org/gifted-programs/fellows-scholarship/fellows/current-and-past-fellows/2025-fellows/
- Press kit / timeline: http://presskit.ditd.org/Davidson_Institute_Press_Kit/ditd_timeline.htm

### Assessment
Davidson Institute has potentially the richest K-8 data of any program (accepts from age 5) but publishes essentially none of it in aggregate or individual-level form. The Fellows scholarship database is a searchable list of ~20-30 recipients per year since 2001, with project categories and names — no developmental backstory or college outcome data in the public directory. The highest-potential source for K-8 trajectory data if a formal data-sharing agreement could be established, but currently the lowest data accessibility.

## 5. Stanford Pre-Collegiate Studies (formerly EPGY)

### Program History
- EPGY (Education Program for Gifted Youth) was Stanford's gifted distance/residential program
- EPGY reached over 950,000 students globally since its inception
- Transitioned to Stanford Pre-Collegiate Studies (SPCS) — website: https://spcs.stanford.edu/
- Stanford Online High School (OHS) is the residential/online descendant for grades 7–12

### Relevant Programs
- Stanford University Mathematics Camp (SUMaC): for 10th and 11th graders — NOT K-8
- Stanford Math Tournament (SMT): high school participants — NOT K-8
- SPCS Summer Institutes: residential programs for grades 8–11

### Published Outcome Data
- EPGY published efficacy data: "EPGY has a positive effect on achievement for K-8 students in both mathematics and English language arts" (Institute of Education Sciences study, Study #89696)
- Alumni outcomes claim: "92% scoring 4 or [above on AP exams]" — partial data, not fully accessible
- Stanford OHS alumni report pursuing graduate study, authoring books, founding nonprofits, becoming doctors — anecdotal listing
- No published individual-level longitudinal dataset connecting EPGY participation at age 8 to college outcomes

### K-8 Trajectory Data Availability
- EPGY served K-8 students in its math and ELA programs — this is a rare K-8 coverage program
- IES evaluation (Study #89696) covers K-8 student achievement but focuses on test score outcomes, not longitudinal trajectory through college
- No published dataset showing: student enrolled in EPGY at grade 3 → followed through grade 12 → college placement

### How to Access
- Stanford SPCS: https://spcs.stanford.edu/
- IES study: https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/Study/89696
- Wikipedia (EPGY history): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_Program_for_Gifted_Youth

### Assessment
EPGY/SPCS served K-8 students but published no longitudinal dataset connecting K-8 participation to college outcomes. The IES evaluation covers K-8 achievement effects only. The most relevant longitudinal data (if it exists) would be internal to Stanford and has not been released publicly. Lower research value for the specific K-8-to-college trajectory question.

## 6. Art of Problem Solving (AoPS) — Beast Academy and AoPS Curriculum

### Program Structure
- Beast Academy: math curriculum and online platform for grades 2–5 (ages 7–10)
- AoPS Online: curriculum for grades 6–12 (pre-algebra through olympiad-level)
- AoPS Academy: in-person locations including multiple Bay Area sites
- Pipeline: Beast Academy (elementary) → AoPS Online/Academy (middle/high) → competition math

### Published Outcome Data
- WestEd independent evaluation (published July 2024): 1,027 students in grades 2–4 in Mankato Area Public Schools over two years
  - Students using Beast Academy ~30 min/week gained 1–2 grade levels on National MAP Assessment vs. control group
  - More lessons attempted = greater MAP score increase
  - More positive math attitudes on 6 of 6 domains measured
  - Source: https://wested2024.s3.us-west-1.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/11172759/WestEd-Beast-Academy-Evaluation-Brief_FINAL-ADA.pdf
- AoPS claims: "Since 1993, AoPS has helped hundreds of thousands of passionate students succeed"
- AoPS wiki tracks competition math results by individual competitors — searchable forum posts link named students to AoPS accounts

### Multi-Year Individual Trajectories on AoPS Forums
- AoPS community forums contain thousands of individual student posts sharing competition histories
- Search terms "my AoPS journey" or "Beast Academy then AoPS" yield community threads
- Example trajectories exist in the community: students who started Beast Academy grades 2–4 and posted progress through MATHCOUNTS, AMC, AIME
- These are self-reported, community-level data (L4 reliability) — not published program outcomes

### Bay Area AoPS Academy Locations
- AoPS Academy has locations in the Bay Area (specific city locations not confirmed in this search)
- No published outcome data specific to Bay Area AoPS Academy locations

### K-8 Trajectory Data Availability
- Beast Academy covers grades 2–5 — this is core K-8 territory
- The WestEd study covers grades 2–4 but measures short-term achievement, not long-term college trajectory
- No published dataset connecting Beast Academy participation at age 7–10 to later outcomes
- The AoPS community forum contains de facto longitudinal data in narrative form (individual posts over years) but it is:
  - Self-selected (only active forum participants)
  - Self-reported
  - Not systematically collected

### How to Access
- Beast Academy outcomes page: https://beastacademy.com/educators/outcomes
- WestEd evaluation brief: https://wested2024.s3.us-west-1.amazonaws.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/11172759/WestEd-Beast-Academy-Evaluation-Brief_FINAL-ADA.pdf
- AoPS community forums: https://artofproblemsolving.com/community

### Assessment
AoPS is the richest source of informal K-8 competition math trajectory data, through community forum posts. The WestEd study is the only peer-reviewed evidence and covers only 2 years of K-2/4 achievement data. No longitudinal dataset connecting Beast Academy/AoPS to college outcomes has been published. The forum community is large enough that systematic collection of user posts showing multi-year trajectories is feasible but would require scraping/manual collection.

## 7. Scripps National Spelling Bee

### Program Structure
- Annual national competition; participants are in grades K-8 (up through 8th grade)
- ~240-290 national finalists per year
- Qualifying pathway: classroom bee → school bee → regional bee → national

### Published Statistics
- spellingbee.com/statistics-previous-years publishes aggregate annual data
- Table format: Year, Spellers (at nationals), Rounds, Words spelled, Repeaters count
- Data from the published table (1991–2010, the "past two decades" table):
  - Range: 227–293 national spellers per year
  - Repeaters per year: 41–69 (about 17–24% of the national field each year)
  - 2010: 273 spellers, 56 repeaters
  - 2009: 293 spellers, 69 repeaters
  - 1991: 227 spellers, 41 repeaters
- More recent data from press releases:
  - 2024: 245 spellers — 57 returning from 2023, 65 had previously competed, 180 (73%) first-timers
  - 2025: 243 spellers
  - 2023: Six-year repeater Akash Vukoti made history as first-ever six-time national participant

### Multi-Year Individual Data
- The national field does include multi-year participants (documented in press releases)
- Examples of repeat participants:
  - Tarini Nandakumar: 5 consecutive national appearances; finished 9th in 2023
  - Navtaj Singh, Micah Sterling, Avinav Prem Anand: 4 consecutive national appearances
  - Akash Vukoti: 6 national appearances (historic first)
- Individual round-by-round results are listed on spellingbee.com for national competition
- BUT: no downloadable individual-level database across years is publicly available
- To match the same student across multiple years requires manual matching of names in annual results

### K-8 Trajectory Data Availability
- This is the only program in this survey where all participants are definitively in grades K-8
- Grade range: K-8, ages approximately 8-14
- Students may compete for up to 5 years (grades 4–8 is the typical window, though grade eligibility goes higher)
- Potential longitudinal window: 5 years of K-8 data (grade 4 through grade 8)
- Data exists but is not in a structured longitudinal database format
- Annual results pages list participant names, states, and schools — same-name matching across years is possible but not automated

### How to Access
- Statistics page: https://spellingbee.com/statistics-previous-years
- Annual competition results: https://spellingbee.com/competition
- spellpundit.com hosts some historical data: https://www.spellpundit.com/scripps-national-spelling-bee.php
- Wikipedia maintains a list of all national champions: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Scripps_National_Spelling_Bee_champions

### Assessment
Scripps Spelling Bee is the cleanest source of true K-8 longitudinal data in this survey — all participants are in grades K-8, repeat participation is common (17–24% per year), and annual results list individual names. The data is not aggregated into a longitudinal format; it exists as annual result pages that could be manually cross-referenced. Volume is modest: ~250 national finalists per year. This is a high-feasibility data construction project requiring manual matching of names across annual result pages.

## 8. MATHCOUNTS

### Program Structure
- Competition for grades 6–8 (middle school, ages 11–14)
- Three-tier structure: School → Chapter (county/regional) → State → National
- National competition: ~224 participants (4 students per state/territory team)
- Annual competition since 1983; cancelled only in 2020 (COVID-19)
- Competition format: Sprint, Target, Team, and Countdown rounds

### Published Historical Data
- AoPS Wiki "MATHCOUNTS historical results" page: https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/MATHCOUNTS_historical_results
  - Lists National Team Champions by year: 1984–2025 (state-level, not individual)
  - Lists Countdown Round Champions (individual national champions) by year: 1984–2025 with full names
    - Example entries: 2017 and 2018 — Luke Robitaille, Texas (two consecutive years, same individual)
    - 2019 — Daniel Mai, Massachusetts
    - 2022 — Allan Yuan, Alabama
    - 2023 — Channing Yang, Texas (also Written Test Champion 2024)
    - 2024 — Ben Jiang, Florida
    - 2025 — Nathan Liu, Texas
  - Lists Masters Round Winners (2009 and earlier)
  - Lists Written Test Champions: 1989–2025
  - Note: AoPS page is community-maintained; gaps exist (e.g., 2010–2011 team champion missing)
- mathcounts.org "National Competition Participants" page lists current-year national competitors
- mathcounts.org "Chapter & State Competition Search" tool exists for state-level lookup
- MATHCOUNTS annual reports available: e.g., 2021-2022 Annual Report at mathcounts.org
- Pennsylvania has a MATHCOUNTS Results Archive going back to 2007 (state-level detailed results)
- National competition results documents (full rankings with scores) available for recent years:
  - 2022 Results Answer Booklet: available on Scribd
  - 2023 Results: available on Scribd
  - 2025 Results: available on Scribd

### Multi-Year Individual Data
- MATHCOUNTS covers grades 6–8 — maximum 3 years of potential longitudinal data per student
- The same student can appear in Chapter, State, and National results across 3 consecutive years
- Luke Robitaille appeared in national results in both 2017 and 2018 (Countdown champion both years)
- No centralized database allows querying "show me all results for student X across years"
- Multi-year tracking requires: manual cross-referencing across annual result documents
- COVID created a gap: 2020 nationals cancelled; 2021 held online

### K-8 Trajectory Data Availability
- MATHCOUNTS is grades 6–8 only — this is middle school, not K-5 elementary
- Maximum individual trajectory window: 3 years (grade 6, 7, 8)
- The AoPS wiki records individual national champion names going back to 1984 — 40+ years of national champion names
- Full results documents (not just champions) exist for recent years with complete individual rankings
- State-level archives vary by state (Pennsylvania has detailed back-to-2007 data)

### How to Access
- AoPS Wiki historical results (champions only, 1984-2025): https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/MATHCOUNTS_historical_results
- MATHCOUNTS official results search: https://mathcounts.org/programs/chapter-state-competition-search
- National participants list: https://mathcounts.org/programs/national-competition-participants
- Past competition materials: https://mathcounts.org/resources/past-competitions
- Recent full results on Scribd: search "MATHCOUNTS National Competition Results"
- Pennsylvania archive: https://www.pspe.org/pspe-events/mathcounts/mathcounts-archive/

### Assessment
MATHCOUNTS provides the most structured individual competition data of the competition-based programs, with 40+ years of national champion names and recent full-field results documents. Maximum trajectory window is 3 years (grades 6–8). This is middle school data, not K-5 elementary. Constructing a longitudinal dataset requires cross-referencing annual result documents — feasible for recent years (where full results PDFs exist) but labor-intensive for historical years where only champion names are listed.

## 9. RSI — Research Science Institute (Center for Excellence in Education)

### Program Description
- RSI administered by the Center for Excellence in Education (CEE), Washington, D.C.
- CEE President: Joann P. DiGennaro; Executive VP: Maite P. Ballestero
- Held at MIT campus each summer
- Duration: 6 weeks in summer; ~80 students selected per year
- Eligibility: Juniors (rising) in high school, age approximately 15–16
- Acceptance rate: approximately 5% (1,600+ applications for 80 spots)
- Total alumni: approximately 3,000+ as of 2024

### Published Alumni Outcome Data
- From CEE website and Wikipedia:
  - 207 Regeneron (formerly Intel) Science Talent Search (STS) finalists who are RSI alumni
  - 754 Regeneron STS semifinalists who are RSI alumni
  - 12 first-place STS winners who are RSI alumni
  - 15 Rhodes Scholars among RSI alumni
  - 16 Marshall Scholars among RSI alumni
  - Fields Medalist Terence Tao attended RSI in 1989
  - Pinterest co-founder Ben Silbermann: RSI 1998
  - Stanford economics professor Gabriel Carroll: RSI 2000 (four-time Putnam Fellow)
- College admission rates for RSI alumni (from third-party college counseling sources, not CEE official):
  - MIT admits approximately 30–40% of RSI participants (vs. 4% overall)
  - Over 80% of RSI students admitted to top-choice school (unverified claim from counseling blogs)
  - Elite universities (Harvard, MIT, Stanford, Caltech, Princeton) heavily represented

### K-8 Trajectory Data Availability
- NONE. RSI targets rising high school juniors (age 15–16)
- This is the latest-in-the-pipeline program in this survey
- RSI is the endpoint of an academic trajectory, not a K-8 program
- Value for this research: RSI alumni outcomes can be used to calibrate what the "top of the funnel" produces — useful for back-calculation

### How to Access
- CEE website: https://www.cee.org/programs/research-science-institute
- CEE alumni honors: https://www.cee.org/about-us/alumni-honors (returned 403 error)
- Wikipedia: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_Science_Institute
- MIT RSI page: https://math.mit.edu/research/highschool/rsi/

### Assessment
RSI is irrelevant for K-8 trajectory data (participants are age 15–16). It is highly relevant as an outcome benchmark: students who reach RSI represent the top of the gifted pipeline. The published aggregate outcome data (Regeneron STS finalists, Rhodes Scholars) is authoritative. No individual-level longitudinal dataset connecting RSI participation to college outcomes is publicly available. The college admission rates cited in third-party sources are not verified by CEE directly.

## 10. First LEGO League / Destination Imagination / Odyssey of the Mind

### First LEGO League (FLL)
- Global STEM program for ages 4–16 (Explore: ages 4–6; Challenge: ages 9–16)
- Teams build and program robots, develop STEM projects
- No centralized public results database identified
- No published longitudinal outcome studies identified
- Team/school names appear in regional/state/world championship results but not in a unified searchable database
- No individual-level tracking across years in public sources

### Destination Imagination (DI)
- Non-profit organization; formed ~1999 from volunteers
- Team-based creative problem-solving competition
- Ages: elementary through college (grades K–12+)
- No centralized longitudinal results database identified
- No published longitudinal outcome studies identified

### Odyssey of the Mind (OotM)
- For-profit organization (contrast: DI is non-profit)
- Similar team creative problem-solving structure
- Ages: grades K-12
- No centralized longitudinal results database identified
- No published longitudinal outcome studies identified

### K-8 Trajectory Data Availability
- All three programs cover K-8 age ranges
- Team competition — results are team/school-level, not individual-level
- No mechanism exists to track the same individual across multiple years of competition
- Even school/team tracking across years is not available in a unified public database

### Assessment
These programs have no published longitudinal data and no accessible individual-level results databases. They are the lowest-value programs in this survey for constructing K-8 individual trajectories. They may be useful as context for school-level research (which schools consistently participate, how long they sustain programs) but individual-level tracking is not feasible from public data.

## Key Findings for the K-8 Trajectory Research Goal

### What exists and is accessible:
1. **SMPY published papers** (gwern.net/smpy): 400+ papers, all freely accessible as PDFs. Covers middle school (age 12–13) through midlife (age 50+). Best longitudinal STEM talent data available. Does NOT cover K-8 elementary years.

2. **MATHCOUNTS annual results** (mathcounts.org + Scribd): Full individual ranking results for recent years. National champion names back to 1984. Can construct 3-year grade 6–8 trajectories manually. Not K-5 but grades 6-8.

3. **Scripps Spelling Bee annual results** (spellingbee.com): ~250 national finalists named each year. Repeater counts published (17–24% of field each year). Can match same names across years manually. True K-8 coverage (contestants in grades K-8). The only nationally-scaled K-8 individual competition dataset accessible in any form.

4. **AoPS Wiki competition results** (AMC, AIME, USAMO): AoPS wiki maintains extensive historical competition results with individual names. Can trace the same student through AMC 8 (grade 8 and below), AMC 10/12, AIME, and USAMO. Not K-5 but covers grades 6-12.

5. **CTY/SET Brody 2005 paper**: N=320, 10-year follow-up, identification before age 13. Published. Accessible via EPFL link.

### What does NOT exist in public form:
- Individual-level K-8 data for any of the programs (SMPY, CTY, Davidson, EPGY)
- A structured multi-year individual participation database for any program
- Published longitudinal paper connecting participation at age 7–10 to college outcomes
- Any database that can be downloaded or queried showing "student X did A at age 7, B at age 9, C at age 12"

### The Fundamental Gap:
The most important K-8 talent research (SMPY) starts identification at age 12–13, making it inherently a middle-school-forward study. Programs that identify at younger ages (Davidson: age 5; CTY: grade 2; EPGY: K) have not published longitudinal datasets. The only structured K-8 individual data accessible without institutional partnerships is competition results from Spelling Bee and MATHCOUNTS (grades 6–8 for the latter).

### Practical Research Paths:
1. **Contact Vanderbilt SMPY** (rachel.a.morgan@vanderbilt.edu): request access to cohort data for research collaboration — best chance of getting individual-level data
2. **Manually construct Spelling Bee trajectories**: Cross-reference annual spellingbee.com results pages for repeated names — feasible for 10-15 years of data
3. **Construct MATHCOUNTS chapter-to-state-to-national progressions**: Recent full results PDFs (2022, 2023, 2025) include complete individual rankings — grades 6–8 trajectories constructable
4. **Mine AoPS forum posts**: Search community posts for self-reported multi-year competition trajectories — yields L4 community data but high volume
5. **Contact Davidson Institute directly**: They may share aggregate Young Scholars outcome data without publishing it — the only organization with genuine K-8 (ages 5+) longitudinal records
