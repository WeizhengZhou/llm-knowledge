# K-8 Trajectory Creative Sources Survey

## Background and Search Objective

The standard college placement data in this project captures outcomes at grades 9-12. This survey attempts to find sources documenting what children do BEFORE high school — when they started piano, what math enrichment they pursued, when they entered their first competition, and what extracurriculars they tried and dropped. This is the most valuable and least available stratum of the K-12 development picture.

All 13 source categories from the brief were searched. Findings are organized by category, with assessment of actual data yield per source type.

## Category 4: Math Competition Entry-Level Results

### Source: Math Kangaroo USA
- URL: https://mathkangaroo.org/mks/national-and-state-winners/
- Tier: L1-official

**What was found:**

Math Kangaroo is the most extensive public dataset of elementary-age math competition participation available:
- Serves grades 1-12 (ages approximately 6-18)
- 2026: 1,682 registered centers; California alone contributed approximately 21,000 participants
- Grade 1-4 students account for over 32,000 participants nationally
- Grade 4 has the highest enrollment; grades 1-3 closely follow; enrollment significantly decreases from grade 6 onward
- 2024: 4,801 students ranked in top 20 nationally; 1,761 students achieved top-3 state ranking
- Public winner lists published annually by grade (grade 1 through grade 12) and by state
- 2025 winners listed at: https://mathkangaroo.org/mks/national-and-state-winners/grade-1-national-winners/

**Critical limitation:** The public lists show top-20 national and top-3 state winners only. Individual student names and rankings below this threshold are not published. No cross-year tracking of individual students is available in the public interface. There is no downloadable database; results appear as HTML pages per grade per year.

**Data volume:** Top winners only (20 national + approximately 3 per state per grade per year = roughly 170 data points per grade level per year); participation counts by grade available in aggregate
**Accessibility:** Public, no login required
**Language:** English
**Extractability:** HTML tables, web-scraping feasible for top winner data
**K-8 trajectory quality:** LOW for individual trajectories (only top performers); MODERATE for aggregate grade-level participation statistics showing when students typically begin competing

### Source: MOEMS (Math Olympiads for Elementary and Middle Schools)
- URL: https://moems.org/
- Tier: L1-official

**What was found:**

MOEMS serves grades 4-8 (some participation from K-3). Recent participation: 84,530 students with 74,857 recording scores; 17,888 students from grades 6-8. Monthly olympiad format (November through March); results submitted by school coordinators. Honor Roll available for purchase as a 32-page booklet. School-level statistics accessible to participating school coordinators through a secure portal. Public aggregate statistics available; individual student public database does not exist.

**Data volume:** Aggregate statistics only; no public individual database
**K-8 trajectory quality:** LOW for individual trajectories; provides grade-level entry-point norms

### Source: AMC 8
- URL: https://amc-reg.maa.org/reports/generalreports.aspx
- Tier: L1-official

**What was found:**

AMC 8 serves students in grade 8 and below (no lower age limit). 2024 participation: 272,035 students worldwide; 231,478 in USA; national average score 10.54/25. Achievement Roll recognizes 6th grade and below students scoring 15+. Honor Roll = top 5%; Distinguished Honor Roll = top 1%. AMC 8 participation data by grade available in aggregate through MAA but no public individual student database. The AoPS wiki maintains historical aggregate statistics: https://artofproblemsolving.com/wiki/index.php/AMC_historical_results (returned 403 on direct fetch; content confirmed via search).

**Key trajectory marker:** Students who excel at AMC 8 in grades 5-6 typically progress to AMC 10 in grades 6-7 and aim for AIME qualification in grades 8-9. The youngest documented USAJMO qualifier at RSM was a 4th grader. This suggests elite competition math acceleration starts by grade 3-4.

**Data volume:** Aggregate statistics public; no individual database
**K-8 trajectory quality:** LOW for individual trajectories; provides competition entry norms

### Source: MathCounts
- Serves grades 6-8 exclusively
- Chapter results: maintained by regional PE society chapters; not in a centralized searchable national database
- NOVA MathCounts publishes individual results: https://novamathcounts.weebly.com/results.html
- Data volume per chapter: moderate (school, student names, scores for chapter-level competitions)
- K-8 trajectory quality: Provides 6-8 grade data only; no elementary component

### Source: Continental Math League (CML)
- URL: https://www.cmleague.com/results/
- Serves grades 2-6
- Results accessible at cmleague.com after each meet; individual schools may post their own result pages
- No centralized public database of individual student scores
- Example of school-level data: https://www.lcps.org/Page/252526 (Loudoun County public schools publish CML results by student/grade)
- K-8 trajectory quality: MODERATE if school-level data can be collected; grade 2-6 coverage ideal for early trajectory

## Category 6: Youth Sports Progression

### Source: USA Swimming Individual Times Search
- URL: https://data.usaswimming.org/datahub/usas/individualsearch
- Tier: L1-official

**What was found:**

This is one of the strongest public databases for individual K-8 trajectory data. The interface supports:
- Search by swimmer name (individual swimmer profiles)
- Filter by competition year (data goes back to 1994)
- Filter by age from/to (allowing search for ages 6, 7, 8, 9, etc.)
- Filter by event and time standard
- View all times or best times only
- "PROGRESSION" button per swimmer showing time series of improvement
- Course types: SCY (short course yards), SCM, LCM

Age group categories: 8 & Under (Mini), 9-10 (Midi), 11-12 (Juni), 13+ (Maxi)

For 8-and-under swimmers: no qualifying times required; any swimmer with a registered USA Swimming membership can compete and have times recorded. California results accessible via Pacific Swimming (pacswim.org/swim-meet-results) which links to individual meet result HTML files and SD3 data files.

**Critical finding:** This database allows longitudinal tracking of individual named swimmers from age 6+ across multiple competition seasons. A swimmer who competed in 2020 at age 8 and in 2025 at age 13 would have a complete visible progression record. This is the closest thing to a public individual K-8 trajectory database found in this entire survey.

**Data volume:** Hundreds of thousands of individual swim times going back to 1994; 8-and-under data available
**Accessibility:** Public, no login required; individual swimmer profiles viewable
**Language:** English
**Extractability:** Web interface with structured data; requires individual name lookup or database download
**K-8 trajectory quality:** HIGH for swimming; records competition entry age, improvement progression, team affiliation

**Limitation:** Captures only swimmers; not generalizable to academic enrichment or music; USA Swimming membership required to compete (filters for organized sport participants)

### Source: Pacific Swimming (Bay Area / Northern California)
- URL: https://www.pacswim.org/swim-meet-results
- Tier: L1-official

**What was found:** Meet results are published as HTML files and SD3 zipped data files for each meet dating back to at least 2021. Individual meet sheets listing swimmer names, ages, clubs, and times are accessible without login via the linked HTML result files. This is the Bay Area-specific layer above the national USA Swimming database.

**K-8 trajectory quality:** HIGH for Bay Area swimmers specifically

### Source: SwimStandards.com
- URL: https://swimstandards.com/
- Tier: L3-aggregator

**What was found:** Mobile-friendly aggregator of USA Swimming data providing swimmer profiles, meet results, and rankings. Parents can claim swimmer profiles. Rankings available by age group (8 & Under, 9-10, 11-12, 13+). Detailed individual profiles reportedly available but returned 403 on direct fetch.

### Source: USTA Junior Tennis Rankings
- URL: https://tennislink.usta.com/tournaments/rankings/rankinghome.aspx
- Tier: L1-official

**What was found:**

USTA junior rankings begin at the 12U division (ages 12 and under) for national rankings. Rankings published weekly on Wednesdays. The 10U division is not included in the formal national ranking system. Individual player profiles show results from the past 52 weeks and ranking points earned. Tournament results accessible through TennisLink. The 12U minimum means this database begins tracking children at approximately age 10-11 (when they start competing in events with ranking points).

**K-8 trajectory quality:** MODERATE for 10-12 year olds; absent for younger children

### Source: AskFRED Fencing Results
- URL: https://www.askfred.net/results
- Tier: L1-official

**What was found:**

AskFRED maintains a comprehensive public database of fencing tournament results. Youth age categories: Y-10 (age 10 and under), Y-12, Y-14. Results searchable by tournament. Individual fencers have ratings and result histories. Y-10 category covers ages approximately 7-10, providing elementary-age competition data.

Example results pages found:
- https://askfred.net/Results/results.php?tournament_id=49213 (EGF November Individual Youth Tournament)
- https://www.askfred.net/tournaments/89544f17-2e0c-4640-a712-39992c79a6f2/results (NFA Youth 10-12-14 Saber)

**Data volume:** Tournament-level results with individual fencer names and ratings; historical data going back years
**Accessibility:** Public, no login required
**Extractability:** HTML results pages; moderately structured
**K-8 trajectory quality:** MODERATE to HIGH for fencing specifically; Y-10 category starts at approximately age 7

### Source: MeetScoresOnline (Gymnastics)
- URL: https://www.meetscoresonline.com/results
- Tier: L3-aggregator

**What was found:**

MeetScoresOnline is the primary gymnastics meet results aggregator. Covers Level 1-10 and Xcel divisions (Level 1-4 typically elementary age, ages 6-11). Real-time results require an active MSO All Access Pass (subscription). Historical results listing visible without login; detailed individual scores behind paywall.

**K-8 trajectory quality:** MODERATE if subscription obtained; gymnastics Level 1-6 is almost entirely an elementary-age sport

## Category 10: Published Books with K-8 Trajectory Data

### Source: Battle Hymn of the Tiger Mother (Amy Chua, 2011)
**What was found (via search summaries and book summary sites):**

Amy Chua's memoir contains a documented K-8 trajectory for two daughters:
- Sophia: started Suzuki method piano by age 5; by 8th grade was performing at Carnegie Hall
- Lulu: started piano simultaneously with Sophia (approximately age 3-4); switched to violin to mitigate sibling rivalry
- Practice schedule: 2 hours/day minimum; 3+ hours on Saturdays at home before group lessons
- Saturdays: 3 hours home practice, then Neighborhood Music School (individual violin lesson + group violin lesson + piano-violin lesson with sister)
- Lulu's rebellion occurred at age 13, after which she shifted to recreational violin and tennis

This is a fully documented K-8 musical trajectory with ages, instruments, practice hours, and program types. However, it represents one family's extreme experience and cannot be generalized.

**Extractability:** Book text (available via library, purchase); summaries available online
**K-8 trajectory quality:** HIGH for the specific documented case; LOW for generalizability

### Source: Deliberate Practice Research (Ericsson, Colvin, Gladwell)
**What was found:**

The Ericsson 1993 study documented elite violinists. Key finding: violinists began formal practice before age 5; by age 8 were accumulating approximately 200 hours/year; by age 20 had approximately 10,000 hours. This establishes a normative practice trajectory for elite music achievement. Case studies are embedded in academic papers, not in public databases.

The HBR article "The Making of an Expert" cites:
- Mozart: musical tutelage started before age 4; father was a famous music teacher
- Polgár sisters: started playing chess with parents at "a very young age"; home-schooled

These cases document K-8 trajectories but are highly exceptional and anecdotal.

## Category 13: Talent Development Programs (CTY, CTD, EPGY/Stanford)

### Source: Johns Hopkins Center for Talented Youth (CTY)
- URL: https://cty.jhu.edu/
- Tier: L1-official

**What was found:**

CTY serves students from grade 2 (SCAT test for grades 2-5) through grade 8 (above-grade-level SAT-based talent search). The talent search begins as early as grade 2 (ages 7-8). CTY does not publish individual student K-8 developmental trajectories or alumni data showing what students did before CTY or how CTY participation affected subsequent development. More than 165,000 alumni worldwide; notable alumni include Sergey Brin and Mark Zuckerberg, but no K-8 trajectory data is published for these individuals.

**K-8 trajectory quality:** ABSENT for publicly accessible individual data

### Source: SMPY (Study of Mathematically Precocious Youth) / Vanderbilt
- URL: https://my.vanderbilt.edu/smpy/
- Gwern bibliography: https://gwern.net/smpy
- Tier: L2-authoritative (academic research)

**What was found:**

SMPY is the most rigorous longitudinal study of mathematically talented youth ever conducted (50+ years, 5,000+ participants). However, the study identified students at ages 12-13 (grades 7-8) via SAT testing. K-8 pre-identification data is not systematically collected or published. The Gwern bibliography confirms: SMPY has "minimal systematic documentation of elementary school experiences, music lessons, sports, or earlier enrichment activities." The Gwern SMPY page notes that the ITYB (Intellectually Talented Youth Bulletin) and Precollege Newsletter archives are largely missing. Albert's 1980 study examined family variables but specific K-8 activity records are not reproduced in published literature.

One exception: Laycock 1979 contains "a long profile of a female SMPYer, 'Lisa Skarp'" describing her early childhood and joining SMPY — a rare individual K-8 narrative embedded in a book chapter.

**K-8 trajectory quality:** VERY LOW for publicly accessible data despite being the most relevant longitudinal study

## Summary Assessment: Sources With Actual K-8 Trajectory Data

Ranked by quality and accessibility of actual individual or aggregate K-8 developmental trajectory data:

### Tier A: Real, Accessible Individual Trajectory Data

**1. USA Swimming Times Database**
- URL: https://data.usaswimming.org/datahub/usas/individualsearch
- Domain: Swimming only
- Data: Individual swimmer name + age + times + competition dates going back to 1994; ages 6+ included
- Volume: Hundreds of thousands of data points
- Access: Public, no login
- Limitation: One domain only; requires knowing swimmer names to look up
- Bay Area layer: pacswim.org/swim-meet-results (HTML meet result files)
- Verdict: BEST available source for individual K-8 trajectory data in any domain; unique in the landscape

**2. Davidson Institute Gifted Issues Discussion Forum**
- URL: https://giftedissues.davidsongifted.org/BB/
- Domain: Math progression primarily; some reading/academic
- Data: Parent narratives with child age, grade, curriculum, math level, progression speed
- Volume: Dozens of individual cases across many threads; thousands of posts total
- Access: Public, no login required
- Limitation: Self-selected highly gifted population; not representative of typical students; math-dominated
- Verdict: SECOND BEST; rich individual K-8 math trajectory data in a searchable forum format

**3. AskFRED Fencing Database**
- URL: https://www.askfred.net/results
- Domain: Fencing only
- Data: Individual fencer names + ratings + tournament results; Y-10 category starts at approximately age 7
- Volume: Tournament-by-tournament; searchable by event
- Access: Public, no login required
- Limitation: Niche sport; not generalizable; small population
- Verdict: GOOD for fencing specifically; demonstrates the archetype of the individual competitive results database

### Tier B: Useful Aggregate or Limited Individual Data

**4. Math Kangaroo USA**
- URL: https://mathkangaroo.org/mks/national-and-state-winners/
- Domain: Math competitions
- Data: Top-20 national and top-3 state winners by grade (grades 1-12); participation counts by grade
- Volume: Approximately 170 individual named winners per grade per year across all grades; aggregate participation in tens of thousands
- Access: Public, no login
- Limitation: Only top winners; no individual tracking across years
- Verdict: GOOD for establishing grade 1-12 entry norms and tracking top performers; not useful for typical students

**5. RSM Competition Results Blog**
- URL: https://www.mathschool.com/blog/results/2024-2025-math-competitions-results
- Domain: Math (with some breadth to other competitions)
- Data: Named student achievers at top competition levels; aggregate participation numbers by competition
- Volume: Dozens of named students per year; rare K-8 anomalies like 4th-grade USAJMO qualifier documented
- Access: Public, no login
- Limitation: Only RSM students; top performers only
- Verdict: MODERATE; provides concrete K-8 math competition data points and rare early-grade achievement documentation

**6. Scripps National Spelling Bee — Meet the Spellers**
- URL: https://spellingbee.com/meet-the-spellers
- Domain: Spelling/vocabulary
- Data: Individual finalist profiles (243 per year); grades 1-8; school, state, grade information
- Volume: 243 individuals per year; multi-year return data available for approximately 50 returning spellers
- Access: Public, no login
- Limitation: National finalists only; not representative; no regional/state data accessible
- Verdict: MODERATE for top finalists only; could yield individual case study profiles

**7. Kumon Progression Data (Community Sources)**
- URL: Quora thread (blocked), forum posts
- Domain: Math and reading enrichment
