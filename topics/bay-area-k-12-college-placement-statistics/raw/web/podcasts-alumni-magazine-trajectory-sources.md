### Summary — Bay Area School Alumni Magazines

No Bay Area private school alumni magazine was found that systematically publishes detailed K-12 developmental trajectories — showing when students started activities, what grade they entered competitions, how interests evolved year by year. All five magazines reviewed (Harker, SHS, Menlo, Castilleja, Nueva) share the same structural limitation: alumni profiles are career-stage spotlights (X is now a CEO/doctor/professor) with a brief retrospective nod to school connection, not structured developmental narratives.

The closest signal found:
- Harker News profiles sometimes mention specific activities from high school (Andy Fang's three years in neural networks class) — but no grade ranges.
- Castilleja's "Portrait of an Alum" mentions Castilleja robotics as a foundation for AI career — no grade detail.
- Nueva's design thinking focus may yield slightly richer developmental language in profiles, but not confirmed.

**All magazines share these structural constraints:**
1. Alumni are profiled years or decades after graduation — narrative focus shifts to career.
2. The school's communications goal is to showcase alumni success, not document developmental arcs.
3. No magazine publishes a structured "here is what grade you started X, what competitions you entered in Y" profile format.

## KEY FINDINGS

**1. No podcast systematically covers K-12 developmental trajectories.** Despite the large college admissions podcast landscape (60+ shows), none is structured as a K-12 journey documentary podcast. "Aspire with The Ivy Dream" is the closest by stated format but delivers less in practice. Transcript extraction from audio episodes would require speech-to-text processing.

**2. Bay Area school alumni magazines do not contain structured K-12 trajectory data.** All five Bay Area private schools investigated (Harker, SHS, Menlo, Castilleja, Nueva) publish alumni spotlights that are career-stage snapshots, not developmental arc narratives. The structural reason is communications purpose: magazines celebrate alumni success, not document developmental history. The Harker News website is the most accessible and prolific (~15-20 profiles/year), with individual articles on news.harker.org fully text-accessible and linkable.

**3. "Try Harder!" (PBS, 2021) is the most directly relevant documentary** to this project's Bay Area scope. It covers 5 named students at Lowell High School (SF selective public) through their senior year application cycle. This is the only documentary identified with Bay Area students documented in a college-admissions context. The film does NOT cover K-8 development. It covers the final year of the K-12 journey.

**4. Medium "how I got into" articles are the most accessible student-generated K-12 trajectory content** but almost universally lack grade-level timing data. They describe activities by final leadership role, not developmental progression.

**5. The biggest gap remains the K-8 portion.** Even the richest sources found (PrepScholar Cheng profile, Try Harder! documentary) begin their timeline in 9th-10th grade or senior year. No publicly accessible source was found that systematically captures elementary or middle school origins of activities that later led to elite college admission.

**6. The most actionable finding for this project:**
- "Try Harder!" should be watched/transcribed for Bay Area qualitative context (5 Lowell High students).
- Harker News individual alumni articles (news.harker.org/category/alumni/) are fully text-accessible and linkable. Individual profiles may contain fragmentary K-12 activity mentions worth extracting.
- Castilleja's older PDF magazine issues (2013-2014 PDFs confirmed at castilleja.org) may contain longer-form profiles than the current Issuu flipbook format — worth checking.
- PrepScholar's Cheng profile remains the single best free public case study with grade-level timing.
- No podcast or alumni magazine warrants transcription or subscription expenditure for K-12 trajectory data specifically.
