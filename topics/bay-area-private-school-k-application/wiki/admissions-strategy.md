# K Admissions Strategy

## Overview

This guide covers strategic considerations for Bay Area private K applications: building a school list, the role of admissions consultants, sibling preference, and waitlist navigation. Much of the strategic advice in this domain comes from admissions consultants and community sources; official school sources focus on process rather than strategy.

## Building a School List

### Application Volume

> **Epistemic note:** The following is synthesized from admissions consultant marketing content
> and community sources (2025-2026). Individual experiences vary.
> Treat as directional, not definitive.

According to Cardinal Education (Bay Area admissions consulting firm), families should apply to 5-7 schools including reach, likely, and safety options. Cardinal Education advises starting the process 2-3 years early to spread workload and build authentic relationships (source: cardinaleducation.com, April 2026).

### Key Differentiators to Consider

When comparing schools, the verified data reveals several axes of differentiation:

**Age cutoffs vary significantly:**
- July 15: [[school-profiles-sf|Convent & Stuart Hall]], [[school-profiles-sf|Town School for Boys]]
- August 1: [[school-profiles-sf|SF Day School]], [[school-profiles-sf|The Hamlin School]]
- August 31: [[school-profiles-peninsula-east-bay|Marin Country Day School]]
- September 1: [[school-profiles-sf|Children's Day School]], [[school-profiles-sf|Live Oak School]], [[school-profiles-sf|SF Friends School]], [[school-profiles-sf|CAIS]], [[school-profiles-sf|Presidio Hill School]]
- September 15 (flexible to September 30): [[school-profiles-peninsula-east-bay|Park Day School]]
- November 30: [[harker-school|Harker School]]
- December 2: [[challenger-school|Challenger School]], [[stratford-school|Stratford School]] (California state standard)

A child with a late-summer birthday will be eligible at some schools but not others. South Bay schools generally have later cutoffs than SF schools.

**Assessment type:**
- Playdate/observation: Most SF city schools (see [[assessment-playdate]])
- IQ test required: [[school-profiles-peninsula-east-bay|Nueva School]] (minimum 130), [[harker-school|Harker School]] (cognitive assessment)
- Placement test: [[challenger-school|Challenger School]]
- Teacher readiness assessment: [[stratford-school|Stratford School]]
- Assessment Day: [[school-profiles-peninsula-east-bay|Head-Royce School]]

**Single-gender vs. coed:**
- All-girls: [[school-profiles-sf|The Hamlin School]]
- All-boys: [[school-profiles-sf|Town School for Boys]]
- Coed: all other schools in this research set

**Language immersion:** [[school-profiles-sf|CAIS]] (Mandarin), [[school-profiles-sf|La Scuola International]] (Italian). See [[language-immersion]].

**K cohort size matters for admission chances:**
- ~44 new students: [[school-profiles-sf|Live Oak School]], [[school-profiles-sf|SF Friends School]]
- ~8-10 new students: [[school-profiles-peninsula-east-bay|Marin Horizon School]]
- ~2-4 new students: [[school-profiles-sf|The San Francisco School]] (most students enter before K)

## Sibling Preference

According to ISSFBA, member schools do not encourage first-choice declarations or letters of recommendation. However, sibling preference is a separate matter handled by individual schools.

### School-Specific Sibling Policies

- According to Live Oak School, the priority deadline for enrolled families is October 8, 2025 (months before the general January 6 deadline).
- According to Marin Country Day School, sibling and faculty K applications are due October 10, 2025.
- According to La Scuola International School, sibling and staff applications are due December 1, 2025.
- According to Head-Royce School, the sibling K early decision deadline is December 5, 2025, with decisions by January 30, 2026.

> **Epistemic note:** The following is from community-tier sources (admissions consultant
> interviews on a real estate blog). Treat as directional, not definitive.

According to a community source (Ruth Krishnan blog), admissions consultant Paula Molligan confirms that "the schools do have strong sibling policies. So that is good news for parents with more than one child. It's not automatic, but there is a strong policy for acceptance." (source: ruthkrishnan.com, April 2026)

PrivateSchoolReview reports that sibling discounts nationally often range from 5-15% off tuition for the second child. This is general national data; specific Bay Area school policies vary.

## Admissions Consultants

Several admissions consulting firms serve the Bay Area private school market:

- **Aquino Admissions Consulting** (SF and Marin focus): Founded by Lisa Lau Aquino, who served as Director of Admission at The Hamlin School for 20+ years and was founding President of ISSFBA. Serves preschool through Grade 8. (source: aquinoadmissionsconsulting.com, April 2026)
- **Cardinal Education** (Bay Area-wide): Founded 2006 by Allen Koh. Offers tutoring, test prep, essay guidance, interview prep, and admissions strategy. (source: cardinaleducation.com, April 2026)
- **Bay Area School Choice** (SF, Marin, Peninsula): Private and public school placement and relocation services, K-12. (source: bayareaschoolchoice.com, April 2026)

No pricing information was found on consultant websites during this research cycle.

> **Epistemic note:** The following is from a community-tier source (real estate agent blog).
> Treat as directional, not definitive.

A community source estimates professional consultant fees at $3,000-$15,000 and application fees at $100-200 per school (source: ruthkrishnan.com, April 2026). These figures are unverified against consultant pricing pages.

## Waitlist Strategy

According to ISSFBA, once a family signs a contract with one ISSFBA school, other ISSFBA schools do not typically offer a contract. This means waitlist movement begins after the enrollment response deadline (March 24-26 for most schools in the 2025-26 cycle).

ISSFBA advises that families should contact schools directly about maintaining active waitlists after response deadlines, as policies vary by institution.

> **Epistemic note:** The following is from a community-tier source (admissions consultant
> interview). Treat as directional, not definitive.

According to a community source, admissions consultant Paula Molligan explains that waitlisted families were often initially considered for acceptance but displaced due to space constraints. Schools consider "zip codes, and cultural diversity, and ethnic diversity, and socioeconomic diversity" when making final decisions. To improve waitlist chances, families may contact admissions directly and seek recommendations from current school families. (source: ruthkrishnan.com, April 2026)

## South Bay vs. SF Strategy Differences

The South Bay admissions landscape operates differently from San Francisco in ways that affect strategy:

**Rolling admissions as a safety net:** [[challenger-school|Challenger School]] and [[stratford-school|Stratford School]] accept applications year-round. According to Challenger School, enrollment applications are accepted year-round; early submission is recommended as classes fill. According to Stratford School, applications are welcomed throughout the school year provided space is available; there is no published application deadline. Families can apply to these schools at any time, including after receiving SF decisions in March.

**No ISSFBA single-contract constraint:** According to ISSFBA, once a family signs a contract with one ISSFBA school, other ISSFBA schools do not typically offer a contract. South Bay schools that are not confirmed ISSFBA members may not follow this policy, potentially allowing families to hold multiple offers while deciding.

**Cognitive assessments require advance planning:** According to Harker School, a cognitive assessment is required for all TK through grade 2 applicants and must be administered by a psychologist from Harker's preferred list. According to Harker School, coaching or tutoring for the cognitive assessment disqualifies the application. Book a psychologist early in the admissions season.

**Later age cutoffs expand the applicant pool:** [[harker-school|Harker]] (November 30) and [[challenger-school|Challenger]]/[[stratford-school|Stratford]] (December 2) accept children who would be too young for most SF schools (August 1 or September 1 cutoffs). This is particularly relevant for fall-birthday children.

See [[south-bay-schools]] for the full regional guide.

## Common Mistakes

- **Applying to only 1-2 schools:** The competitive landscape makes a broader application strategy advisable.
- **Ignoring age cutoff differences:** A child eligible at one school may not qualify at another. Check each school's cutoff before investing time in the application.
- **Assuming first-choice letters help:** According to ISSFBA, member schools do not encourage first-choice declarations or letters of recommendation. According to The Hamlin School, declaring Hamlin as a first choice carries no admissions advantage.
- **Missing sibling priority deadlines:** Sibling deadlines (October-December) are months before general deadlines (December-January). Check your school's specific policy.
