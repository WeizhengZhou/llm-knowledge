# K Assessment Preparation

## Definition

Bay Area private K admissions assessments fall into three categories: **formal IQ testing** (required by gifted-focused schools), **playdate/observation assessments** (used by most SF schools), and **parent interviews** (nearly universal). This article covers the logistics and preparation for all three. For school-specific assessment requirements and formats, see [[assessment-playdate]].

## IQ Testing: Which Schools Require It

Bay Area schools requiring IQ testing for K admissions include Nueva School (FSIQ around 130 and above), Helios School (no published minimum FSIQ; holistic review), and Harker School (cognitive assessment required; type and cutoff not specified on official site).

### Nueva School

Nueva School requires all PreK-8 applicants to submit WPPSI-IV results (under age 6) or WISC-V results (age 6 and older). Older versions or alternative assessments are not accepted. IQ results are due January 8, 2026 (Round II Pre-K: March 24, 2026).

Nueva School states on its IQ assessment page that it seeks students with a Full Scale Wechsler IQ Score around 130 (98th percentile) and above. If results fall outside the target range, the application is withdrawn and the child must wait 12 months before retaking the assessment.

According to Nueva School, there are approximately 20-22 kindergarten openings annually.

### Helios School

Helios School requires the WPPSI-IV for applicants under age 6 and the WISC-V for applicants age 6 and older.

Helios School states it does not have a minimum Full Scale IQ requirement and evaluates the complete IQ report across all subtest indices rather than focusing solely on the FSIQ score.

According to Helios School, required WPPSI-IV subtests include Verbal Comprehension Index, Visual Spatial Index, Fluid Reasoning Index, Working Memory Index, Processing Speed Index, Full Scale IQ, and General Ability Index.

According to Helios School, exposing a student to IQ test content before the assessment would invalidate the test results.

Helios School supplemental documents, including the IQ report, are due February 2, 2026.

### Harker School

According to Harker School, a cognitive assessment is required for all TK through grade 2 applicants and must be administered by a psychologist from Harker's preferred list. According to Harker School, coaching or tutoring for the cognitive assessment disqualifies the application. See [[harker-school]] for full profile.

## WPPSI-IV / WISC-V Test Format

Bay Area IQ test provider bayareaiqtest.com describes the WPPSI-IV format for K-age children as a one-on-one session with a licensed psychologist lasting approximately 45-60 minutes, administered in a quiet private setting with the parent waiting outside.

The WPPSI-IV is administered to children under age 6; the WISC-V applies to children age 6 and older. Both are Wechsler-family instruments measuring cognitive ability across multiple domains.

Bay Area IQ assessment providers listed on Nueva School's resource list typically charge in the range of $850-$950, according to provider websites. Nueva School offers financial assistance vouchers for eligible families.

## Preparation: No Coaching

Both Helios School and Harker School explicitly warn parents against test-specific coaching or preparation for IQ assessments, stating it invalidates results. Standard preparation advice includes maintaining regular sleep and nutrition, reading daily, and doing age-appropriate puzzles.

This is a critical point: coaching does not help and may actively harm the application. Schools will reject results they believe were influenced by test preparation.

## Playdate Assessments: What Schools Observe

> **Epistemic note:** The following playdate criteria are sourced from a single L3 admissions consulting site (admissionsessayadvising.com). Individual schools may assess different skills or use different formats.

Admissions consulting site admissionsessayadvising.com reports that during K playdates, schools commonly observe ability to follow instructions, curiosity, group participation, emotional regulation, and communication skills. The site states schools generally do not expect K applicants to read or demonstrate academic pre-skills beyond age-appropriate norms. These criteria may vary by school.

School-specific assessment formats confirmed from official sources: Lycee Francais uses a Zoom parent interview and on-campus playdate; SVIS uses a parent conversation and playdate assessment in January/February; Helios uses a student workshop and parent meeting after IQ assessment; Nueva uses an Activity Session with group and individual activities observed by faculty, with parents not present.

For detailed school-by-school playdate formats and schedules, see [[assessment-playdate]].

## Parent Interview Preparation

> **Epistemic note:** Parent interview guidance below is sourced from a single L3 admissions consulting site. Actual interview questions and evaluation criteria vary by school.

Admissions consulting site admissionsessayadvising.com lists common K parent interview questions including: 'What brings you to our school?', 'Describe your child using three adjectives', 'How does your family approach academic challenges or frustration?', and 'What questions do you have about our school?'

Admissions consulting site admissionsessayadvising.com reports that during parent interviews, schools typically assess philosophy alignment, realistic expectations, collaborative communication style, and ability to articulate a child's strengths and challenges honestly.
