# Bay Area Private School K Application

## What This Is

Applying to private kindergarten in the San Francisco Bay Area is a structured, competitive process that typically spans September through March of the year before enrollment. Families navigate a landscape of 110+ independent schools across five sub-regions, each with distinct admissions timelines, assessment models, and educational philosophies.

This wiki is a fact-checked reference for parents navigating the 2025-26 admissions cycle (for Fall 2026 enrollment).

## The ISSFBA Framework

According to ISSFBA, the organization covers 110+ independent schools across the SF Bay Area. The Independent Schools of the San Francisco Bay Area (ISSFBA), operating as BADA (Bay Area Directors of Admission), coordinates admissions across member schools. According to ISSFBA, BADA was incorporated as a 501(c)(6) nonprofit in 2013.

Key coordinated policies:
- According to ISSFBA, the 2025-26 K-8 electronic notifications are sent March 19, 2026, with family responses due March 26, 2026.
- According to ISSFBA, the 2025-26 PreK/TK/JK electronic notifications are sent March 11, 2026, with family responses due March 17, 2026.
- According to ISSFBA, once a family signs a contract with one ISSFBA school, other ISSFBA schools do not typically offer a contract.
- According to ISSFBA, most member schools have application deadlines in January of the enrollment year.
- According to ISSFBA, member schools do not encourage first-choice declarations or letters of recommendation.

Not all Bay Area schools are ISSFBA members. Notably, South Bay schools such as [[harker-school|Harker]], [[challenger-school|Challenger]], and [[stratford-school|Stratford]] operate outside the ISSFBA coordinated timeline. See [[issfba-bada]] for full details.

## Regions at a Glance

**San Francisco:** The densest cluster of ISSFBA-member schools. Predominantly playdate/observation-based assessments, January deadlines, March 19 coordinated decisions. According to KQED, 30% of K-12 students in San Francisco attend private schools (2023-24 data). See [[school-profiles-sf]].

**Peninsula (Palo Alto, Hillsborough, Menlo Park):** Mix of ISSFBA and independent schools. Includes gifted-focused programs requiring IQ testing (Nueva School, minimum 130). See [[school-profiles-peninsula-east-bay]].

**East Bay (Oakland, Berkeley):** Several progressive and independent schools including Head-Royce and Park Day. See [[school-profiles-peninsula-east-bay]].

**Marin (Corte Madera, Mill Valley):** Smaller market with schools like Marin Country Day and Marin Horizon. See [[school-profiles-peninsula-east-bay]].

**South Bay (San Jose, Sunnyvale, Saratoga):** Distinct admissions culture -- rolling admissions more common, cognitive/placement assessments rather than playdates, no ISSFBA coordination. Includes large multi-campus operators (Challenger, Stratford) alongside selective single-campus schools (Harker). See [[south-bay-schools]].

## Two Assessment Models

Bay Area private K admissions use two fundamentally different assessment approaches:

**Playdate / Observation (most SF and ISSFBA schools):** Children participate in supervised play sessions where teachers observe social-emotional readiness, curiosity, and adaptability. According to the SF Standard (December 2025), SF city schools such as Hamlin, CDS, SF Day, and Live Oak do not require IQ tests for kindergarten admission.

**Cognitive / IQ Testing (select Peninsula and South Bay schools):** Formal Wechsler (WPPSI) assessment administered by a licensed psychologist. According to the SF Standard (December 2025), a Wechsler (WPPSI) IQ assessment costs approximately $850 per test. Schools in this category include Nueva (minimum 130), [[harker-school|Harker]] (cognitive assessment required), and Helios. [[challenger-school|Challenger]] requires a placement test for K applicants.

See [[assessment-playdate]] for full details on both models.
