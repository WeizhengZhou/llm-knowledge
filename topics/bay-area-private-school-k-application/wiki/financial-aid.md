# Financial Aid for Bay Area Private K Schools

## Overview

Most Bay Area independent schools offer need-based financial aid, often branded as "indexed tuition," "sliding scale tuition," or "adjusted tuition." The primary application platform is Clarity (by Blackbaud). Some schools also use SSS by NAIS (School and Student Services). Aid participation rates range from 28% to 45% of families at schools where data is available.

## How It Works

### Clarity Platform

Several schools use the Clarity financial aid platform. According to SF Day School, the Clarity financial aid application costs $65, waived for low-income applicants. According to Children's Day School, the Clarity application costs $65, waived for families below income thresholds.

The Clarity application collects detailed financial information (income, assets, expenses) to calculate a family's ability to pay. Schools then set their own tuition based on this assessment.

### Indexed / Sliding Scale Tuition

Rather than a single tuition figure with separate "aid," several schools publish tuition as a range, with each family's rate determined by their financial profile:

## School-Specific Financial Aid Data

### SF Day School

According to SF Day School, 2025-26 annual tuition is $48,577. According to SF Day School, the average tuition adjustment is $28,800, with a minimum family contribution of 5% of tuition. According to SF Day School, tuition adjustments range up to 95% reduction, with a minimum family contribution of 5%. According to SF Day School, the adjusted tuition budget for 2024-25 was $3.1 million.

SF Day School's key dates page lists the financial aid (Clarity) deadline as January 12; the tuition page lists January 13 for returning families and January 12 for new families.

### Children's Day School

According to Children's Day School, 2026-27 sliding scale tuition ranges from $3,500 to $48,895 (PreK-Grade 4) and $3,500 to $49,830 (Grades 5-8). According to Children's Day School, 30% of CDS students receive sliding scale tuition support. According to Children's Day School, the financial aid budget is $4.5 million, representing 20% of the operating budget.

According to Children's Day School, the Clarity financial aid application opens October 1, 2025; the deadline for new families is January 20, 2026.

### The San Francisco School

According to The San Francisco School, 28% of families pay reduced tuition through the indexed tuition program. According to The San Francisco School, the Clarity financial aid deadline is January 9, 2026.

### The Hamlin School

According to The Hamlin School, the 2025-26 financial aid budget is $3,140,000.

### Park Day School

According to Park Day School, indexed tuition ranges are K-5 $1,900-$37,980, Grades 6-8 $2,000-$40,620, and TK $28,500. According to Park Day School, approximately 45% of families participate in the indexed tuition program.

### Nueva School

According to Nueva School, financial aid vouchers are available for IQ assessment costs. According to Nueva School, the financial aid application deadline is February 3, 2026.

## Does Applying for Aid Hurt My Chances?

This is the highest-anxiety financial question for applicant families. The short answer: at most Bay Area schools, the impact is unknown because schools do not disclose their process. At one school, the answer is confirmed.

### Confirmed Need-Blind: Nueva School

Nueva School employs need-blind admissions: the admissions committee reviews applications without knowledge of financial assistance requests, and a separate committee determines financial assistance eligibility.

However, there is an important nuance: According to Nueva School, budget constraints may prevent admitting otherwise qualified applicants when the school cannot meet their financial need, making this a modified need-blind approach at the margins.

### The Wider Bay Area

Berkeley Parents Network (BPN), a community forum, states that 'many Bay Area private schools have a policy of blind admissions.' However, the only Bay Area school confirmed to use need-blind admissions from an L1 official source is Nueva School. The claim that 'many' schools follow this practice is unverified beyond Nueva.

> **Epistemic note:** The claim that most Bay Area schools are need-blind
> appeared on Nueva School's official website as a BPN quotation. An L4
> claim does not inherit L1 confidence by appearing on an L1 page (mof015).
> Do NOT generalize Nueva's confirmed need-blind policy to other schools
> without L1 confirmation from each school.

According to Almaden Country Day School, admissions is need-blind (C269 -- single official source).

### Practical Guidance

There is no income limit that automatically makes a family ineligible for financial aid at Bay Area private schools. Nueva School demonstrates this by providing meaningful aid to families earning above $250,000 (58% of aided families). Schools set their own guidelines.

The pragmatic advice: apply for aid if you need it. The risk of enrolling without aid and being unable to sustain tuition over 13 years is greater than any unquantified risk from a need-aware admissions process. ### Market Context

According to KQED, private school annual tuition in San Francisco ranges from $10,000 to $65,000. PrivateSchoolReview lists the average SF county elementary private school tuition at $24,345, across 90 schools with 19,713 students. This average includes religious schools with significantly lower tuition. PrivateSchoolReview lists the average SF private school tuition at $26,461 across all K-12 schools (CA average: $18,062). This average includes religious and non-selective schools and does not represent typical top-tier independent school K tuition.

According to KQED, 30% of K-12 students in San Francisco attend private schools (2023-24 data).

## Common Mistakes

- **Missing the aid deadline:** Financial aid deadlines are typically the same as or earlier than the application deadline. Missing the Clarity deadline can mean paying full tuition even if you qualify for aid.
- **Confusing full tuition with typical cost:** Schools with indexed tuition (CDS, Park Day, SF School) have much lower effective costs for many families. The "sticker price" is the maximum.
- **Assuming aid is not available:** Even at high-tuition schools, 28-45% of families receive tuition adjustments. Always apply.
