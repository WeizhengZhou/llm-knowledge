# Chapter 2: The Year at a Glance — What Happens When

---

It usually happens in late October or early November. You're at a dinner party, talking to a preschool parent you barely know, and they mention — casually, as if confirming the weather — that they've already been to four open houses. That their sibling application went in last week. That their psychologist appointment for IQ testing is next month.

And you think: wait, what?

The private school admissions cycle doesn't announce itself. It starts quietly in September while you're still getting your kid settled into a new preschool year, and by the time most families realize they're behind, they've missed the easiest part of the process.

This chapter exists so that never happens to you. What follows is the full map of the year — what schools are doing, what you need to be doing in parallel, and where the traps are. By the end, you'll have everything you need to build your own calendar and stop letting this live as an unnamed dread in the back of your mind.

---

## The System Running Underneath Everything

The Independent Schools of the San Francisco Bay Area — ISSFBA, operating through a committee called BADA (Bay Area Directors of Admission) — coordinates admissions across 75+ member schools. Its most important contribution to your life is this: on March 19, all member schools release decisions simultaneously. On March 26, families must respond. Everyone knows what everyone else knows at the same moment, which prevents schools from pressuring families into early commitments. Per ISSFBA policy, member schools do not encourage first-choice declarations — so don't invest time writing one.

A few important schools sit outside this framework. Nueva School (Hillsborough) and Harker School (San Jose) release decisions March 20. BASIS Independent Silicon Valley releases March 18. Challenger School and Stratford School (South Bay) have no fixed decision date — they operate rolling admissions year-round.

## Three Systems, Three Timelines

Bay Area private elementary schools fall into three distinct admissions patterns. Which ones are on your list shapes everything else in this chapter.

| | **SF / Bay Area Playdate Schools** | **IQ-Testing Schools** | **South Bay Rolling** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Examples** | SF Day, Hamlin, SF Friends, Live Oak, Convent & Stuart Hall | Nueva, Harker, Helios | Challenger, Stratford |
| **How they assess** | Supervised group playdate (30–90 min) | WPPSI-IV or WISC-V administered by licensed psychologist | Placement assessment (in-house) |
| **Application deadlines** | Dec or Jan (varies by school) | Jan 8 (Nueva); Jan 23 (Harker TK); Feb 2 (Helios) | Year-round; recommend applying by Nov–Dec |
| **Decision date** | March 19 (ISSFBA) | March 19–20 | Rolling; typically weeks after application |
| **The timing risk** | Missing the December deadline wave | Not booking a psychologist by September | Not knowing these exist as an option |

Most families applying to San Francisco schools will only deal with the playdate system. If your list includes Nueva, Harker, or Helios, Chapter 4 covers IQ testing in detail — and your September calendar looks different from everyone else's.

## May Through August: The Research Window

Most families don't start thinking about kindergarten admissions until September. That means you can get a real head start by doing your research in spring and summer. A few schools hold open houses in May or June — these events are smaller and often give you better access to administrators than the crowded fall events will.

**If your list might include a school requiring IQ testing:** this is the window to research psychologists. Appointment slots at established providers fill fast — in some cases within days of opening in late summer. Look up Nueva's Psychologist Resource List if Nueva is a possibility, and identify 2–3 Bay Area providers you'd contact. Don't book yet — just know who you'll call. Chapter 4 covers the IQ testing process in full.

Use May through August to read school websites, attend any spring events, and talk to families whose kids are already enrolled.

---

## September: Three Tracks Start Simultaneously

On or around September 2, the Ravenna Hub platform opens for most ISSFBA schools. SF Day School specifically lists September 2 as its portal opening date; other schools follow on a similar timeline. Ravenna is the common application platform for 11+ Bay Area private schools — one account, one teacher evaluation form that routes to all your schools. Create your account early.

Open house season begins in earnest. Schools hold weekly or biweekly events from September through November. These aren't optional previews — they're how you decide which schools actually make your list.

**Recommendation letters:** September is the moment to have a quiet, early conversation with your child's preschool teacher. Not a formal ask — just a signal: "We'll be applying to private K this cycle and will come back to you in November." Teachers who receive this signal have months to observe your child with that context in mind. Teachers who are asked in December tend to write under time pressure.

**IQ testing (if applicable):** Book your psychologist appointment in September. For Nueva's November 3 guarantee deadline, there is no slack — book in early September. For January deadlines, mid-October is the latest you should push it.

---

## October: Open Houses Peak — and the Deadline Nobody Talks About

Open house season is running at full speed. Take notes after each visit; the details blur together by November. Schools in your anchor set have meaningfully different approaches, and the differences only become clear in person.

> **Warning: Sibling Priority Deadlines**
>
> If you have a child already enrolled at a private school and are applying for a sibling, your deadline may be in **October** — not January.
>
> - Live Oak School: enrolled family priority deadline — **October 8**
> - Marin Country Day School: sibling and faculty K applications — **October 10**
>
> These deadlines are not prominently advertised. Missing them doesn't automatically disqualify a sibling application, but it removes priority consideration. Check your school's current sibling policy before anything else.

Financial aid opens in October. The Clarity portal (the main financial aid platform used by Children's Day School and others) opens October 1. Start gathering documents now if you're applying for aid. Chapter 8 covers financial aid mechanics.

---

## November: Narrow, Ask, and Draft

By November, you should know which schools you're actually applying to. Admissions advisors typically recommend six to ten applications — enough to spread risk without spreading your attention so thin that every application feels generic.

**Recommendation letters:** This is the month to make the formal ask. Have a direct conversation with your child's preschool teacher — in person if possible. Provide the ISSFBA Confidential Recommendation Form (available in your Ravenna account) and set a submission deadline of mid-December. One form covers all your Ravenna schools; your teacher doesn't write a separate letter for each school. Admissions advisors consistently recommend making the ask before submitting portal form requests — the personal heads-up matters.

**IQ testing (if applicable):** Results from testing done this month typically take 1–2 weeks to process. You'll need the written report before each school's specific submission deadline — confirm timing with your psychologist.

**Essays:** If you haven't started your parent essays, start now. Not December.

For families applying to Nueva: the Activity Session prerequisite deadline is November 3. This step unlocks the invitation to the assessment process — missing it removes the guarantee, not the possibility, but most families targeting Nueva should treat November 3 as a hard date.

---

## December: The Wave Most Families Miss

December is the most underestimated month in the admissions calendar. Several schools have application deadlines this month, not January:

- **Hamlin School:** Completed application and fee due in December (exact date varies; check their current admissions page)
- **Convent & Stuart Hall:** Application and evaluation form due December 12
- **SF Friends School:** Deadline December 19
- **SF Day School:** Deadline December 19
- Marin Country Day School: Deadline December 28

Families who heard "January deadlines" and assumed they had until mid-January are often blindsided by this wave. Check every school on your list individually. Do not assume.

**Recommendation letters:** Confirm that your preschool teacher has submitted the form before the holiday break. If you set a mid-December deadline, follow up politely in early December. Send a thank-you once it's in.

**Financial aid:** Aid deadlines cluster in December and early January. SF Friends School and Convent & Stuart Hall both have aid deadlines in this window.

Pay application fees before the holiday break.

---

## January: The Main Deadline Wave

This is the month most families have been tracking. Deadlines cluster in the first three weeks for the bulk of ISSFBA schools:

- Presidio Hill: January 5
- Live Oak: January 6
- Nueva, Harker (K), Helios: January 8
- Head-Royce (completed): January 9
- SVIS: January 13
- CAIS, Park Day: January 16
- Children's Day School: January 20
- The San Francisco School (application): January 23
- Harker (TK): January 23
- BASIS Independent SV: January 28
- Gideon Hausner (Round 1): January 29
- Marin Horizon (priority): January 30

Watch financial aid deadlines in parallel — they often don't match the application deadline. SF Day School's Clarity deadline is January 12, for example. Almaden Country Day's application deadline extends to February 1.

**IQ testing results:** If you're applying to Nueva (January 8) or Harker, your psychologist's report must be submitted with your application. Confirm it's in the portal.

After deadlines pass, schools begin reaching out to schedule child visits — playdates, assessment days, parent interviews. These invitations arrive by email. Watch your inbox.

---

## February: Playdates and Patience

February feels like a holding pattern, but it isn't. Playdates and parent interviews are actively happening. If you haven't heard from a school about scheduling a visit by mid-February, a brief, polite check-in with the admissions office is reasonable — not aggressive, just asking whether they need anything from you.

**Helios IQ results:** If you're applying to Helios, the supplemental document deadline — including the IQ report — is February 2. This is earlier than most families expect.

**Marin Country Day:** Teacher evaluation forms are due February 1. If you applied there, confirm your preschool teacher hasn't missed their deadline.

---

## March: Decision Day and What to Do Next

March 19 is the date. ISSFBA member schools release decisions simultaneously — decisions arrive by email and on the Ravenna platform between noon and 2:00 p.m. for most schools.

A few schools operate on slightly different dates: Live Oak releases March 17 at 4:00 p.m. BASIS Independent SV releases March 18. Nueva and Harker release March 20. If those schools are on your list, you'll have a slightly staggered sequence.

The ISSFBA enrollment response deadline is March 26 — seven days from decision day to accept one offer and decline the others. Some schools are stricter: SF Day and Live Oak require response by March 24; SF Friends requires response by March 25. Read each offer letter for its specific deadline.

**The post-decision decision tree:**

```
March 19: Decisions Released
│
├── ADMITTED to one or more schools
│   ├── One offer → Accept by your school's specific deadline (Mar 24–26)
│   └── Multiple offers
│       ├── You have 7 days — use them. Visit, call, compare
│       ├── Accept one offer; decline all others promptly
│       └── Note: SF Day (Mar 24), SF Friends (Mar 25), others (Mar 26)
│
├── WAITLISTED at one or more schools
│   ├── Within 48 hours: Contact admissions — confirm you remain actively interested
│   ├── Late March–early April: First movement wave (families decline offers)
│   ├── April–May: Second wave (families accept public school placements)
│   ├── June–August: Continued movement, less predictable
│   └── No movement by late July → Most families proceed as though public school
│       is the likely outcome for fall; some movement is still possible
│
└── NO OFFERS / DID NOT ADVANCE
    ├── Gideon Hausner Round 2: Apply by April 16; decisions April 27
    ├── Challenger / Stratford: Apply now; rolling decision, typically weeks
    └── Public school enrollment + reapply for K (next cycle) or 1st grade
```

## April Through August: The Waitlist Is Not Over

Real waitlist movement happens. Families change plans, accept public school offers, relocate — and spots open. Based on patterns advisors observe across cycles, April and May tend to see the most movement, as the March 26 response deadline triggers a cascade of declines. June and July see less, but not zero.

If you're on a waitlist:
- Confirm your continued interest in late March (brief email or call — not a daily check-in)
- Check in every 3–4 weeks through May; taper contact after that
- Be ready to decide quickly if an offer comes — schools may give 24–48 hours on waitlist offers

**If an offer arrives:** you are not obligated to accept immediately if you have not yet enrolled elsewhere, but do not leave a school waiting without communication. A prompt, honest response — even "we need 24 hours to discuss" — is appropriate.

**Gideon Hausner Jewish Day School** runs a formal second round: apply by April 16, decisions April 27. A real option if March results were disappointing.

**Challenger and Stratford** accept applications year-round. You can apply in April or May and potentially have a decision before summer. Advisors who work with Bay Area families often flag these as options worth knowing from the start of the cycle, not only as late-stage pivots.

**After you've accepted and enrolled:** expect enrollment contracts, deposit deadlines, summer orientation communication, and — for most schools — a new-family welcome packet in May or June. If you're coming off a waitlist acceptance in summer, reach out to the school directly to understand the onboarding timeline; some summer communications may have already gone to the confirmed class.

## Schools on Different Timelines

The South Bay operates on a different rhythm that's worth understanding clearly.

**Challenger School** and **Stratford School** accept enrollment applications year-round with no published deadline — they recommend applying early because classes fill. These schools use placement assessments rather than observation playdates, and there is no March 19 coordination. You can have acceptances from these schools in hand before March 19 if you apply in November or December — a position some families find useful for managing uncertainty.

**Harker School** operates independently of ISSFBA. Decisions come March 20 rather than March 19. The admissions process requires a cognitive assessment (WPPSI-IV or WISC-V) administered by a licensed psychologist. Harker explicitly states that coaching or tutoring for the cognitive assessment disqualifies the application.

## What If You're Starting Late?

If you're reading this in November and haven't started: you are not out of the cycle.

What you've probably missed: sibling priority deadlines (if applicable), the Nueva November 3 guarantee deadline, and some spring open houses. Those can't be recovered.

What's still fully available: every December and January deadline, all the playdates and interviews, the entire March decision process. Create your Ravenna account now. Identify your school list. Contact your preschool teacher immediately — explain the situation and ask directly if they can write an evaluation. The conversation will be uncomfortable for about ten seconds. It's worth it.

If you're reading this in January after some December deadlines have passed: apply to everything still open, and add Challenger and Stratford as year-round options.

The families who are genuinely too late are those who discover this in April with no applications filed. If that's you, the South Bay rolling-admissions schools and Gideon Hausner's April second round are your realistic immediate options. Then start the ISSFBA cycle properly next year.

## Your Next Step

The families who get through this process without panic are not better connected or better resourced. They just looked at the full year once, built a calendar, and stopped letting it live as unnamed dread.

Take the multi-track calendar above. Add every school's specific deadlines from their official admissions pages — not from this guide, because deadlines shift annually. Set reminders two weeks before each date. That is the whole system.
