## Dimension Scores

### D1: Reader Outcome Alignment — 2/2

The article directly serves multiple high-priority reader needs implicit in the research plan and confirmed by question coverage. Questions Q019 (IQ testing logistics), Q018 (playdate formats), and Q021 (parent interview preparation) are all addressed. The target reader — a parent navigating Bay Area private K admissions — must understand which schools require IQ testing, how to book a psychologist, what playdates assess, and how to prepare for parent interviews. This article covers all four. The IQ testing section in particular fills a gap not covered by the existing `assessment-playdate.md` (which focuses on formats and schedules rather than logistics and preparation). Score: **2**.

### D2: Decision Framing — 2/2

The article frames actionable decisions throughout:
- "Book by September–October" for Nueva's November guarantee deadline (concrete timing decision)
- The no-coaching warning is framed as a decision-consequence: coaching does not help and actively harms; families must resist the temptation
- The booking timeline table gives parents a clear deadline-driven action checklist
- The $850–$950 cost range frames a financial decision with specific provider options
- The distinction between WPPSI-IV (under 6) vs. WISC-V (age 6+) tells parents exactly which test applies to their child

The parent interview section is more informational (sample questions) but is framed within the context of what schools are actually evaluating, helping parents decide how to prepare. Overall the article leans decisional rather than encyclopedic. Score: **2**.

### D3: Common Mistakes Quality — 2/2

The no-coaching point is the standout non-obvious mistake: most parents would intuitively try to prepare their child for an IQ test, but doing so (a) has no benefit and (b) can actively invalidate the result or disqualify the application (Harker explicitly states this). This is precisely the kind of counterintuitive, high-stakes mistake a good critic article should surface.

The booking timeline warning (book September–October for November deadline) is also non-obvious — parents discovering IQ requirements in October would be at risk of missing the November guarantee window. The article correctly foregrounds this.

The playdate observation criteria note (schools do not expect pre-reading or academic pre-skills) counters a common parental over-preparation instinct. Score: **2**.
