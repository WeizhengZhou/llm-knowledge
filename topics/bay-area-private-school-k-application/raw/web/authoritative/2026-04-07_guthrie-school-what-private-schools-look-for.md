# What Do Private Schools Really Look For in Student Applicants?
Source: Guthrie School — guthrieschool.com

## Primary Assessment Criteria at K Level

**Academic Potential** — assessed not through grades (no K-age academic record) but through:
- Teacher recommendations (described as "equally important" alongside test scores)
- Observed behavior: admissions committees watch for children who are "well behaved," "attentive," and "engaged in the activity and showing eagerness to learn"

**Behavioral Observations During Assessment:**
- How the child interacts with parents present (signals respect for adults)
- How the child interacts with evaluators (strangers in authority roles)
- Whether child "shows his or her own desire to improve and grow" rather than waiting for parental prompting

**Independence Indicators:**
- Confidence in attempting tasks without immediate reassurance
- Ability to recover from a wrong answer and try again
- Willingness to engage with unfamiliar adults

## What "Good Fit" Means — Institutional Definition

Schools determine alignment between the child's "values align with the school's mission statement and will succeed both academically and socially." This is explicitly described as reciprocal — the school evaluating fit AND the family evaluating whether the school is right for their child.

## Holistic Approach — Not Just Test Scores

"Private schools employ a 'holistic approach' rather than relying solely on test scores or grades when evaluating young applicants."

For K applicants where there is no academic record, the holistic evaluation includes:
- Child's playdate or assessment behavior
- Parent interview impressions
- Teacher recommendation
- Application essays/parent statements
- Family cultural or demographic contribution to class composition

## What "Good Fit" Means Differently by School Type

The article does not define fit differently across school types (that differentiation was not in this source). However, it implies that what a school is "looking for" in a child is tightly linked to the school's specific educational philosophy and mission — meaning fit is school-specific, not generic.

## Parent Role in Demonstrating Fit

Schools also assess parents, not just children. Parent interviews evaluate whether the family:
- Understands and aligns with the school's mission
- Has realistic expectations about their child
- Will be collaborative partners in the child's education
