# Teacher Recommendations 101 — Private School Admissions
Source: School First NYC — schoolfirstnyc.com/blog/teacher-recommendations-101

## Why Recommendations Matter

Teacher recommendations offer admissions committees insight beyond what parents can report. They reveal how children approach challenges, engage with peers, and contribute to their classroom community — "the whole child" from a professional educator's perspective.

## Which Teacher to Ask for Younger Children

For kindergarten applicants, schools typically request the homeroom preschool teacher's recommendation. Some schools request two letters; if a child has only one teacher, a previous year's teacher may supplement.

For K applicants specifically: the classroom teacher who knows the child's daily behavior, peer relationships, and learning approach is the most valuable recommender.

## How to Ask — The Right Approach

**Ask in person when possible.** A face-to-face conversation communicates genuine respect for the teacher's time more effectively than an email.

**Contact before the form appears.** Send the teacher a heads-up before submitting electronic form requests through Ravenna or school portals. "Contact the teacher first with a heads-up before submitting electronic forms or portal requests."

**Be specific about deadlines.** Identify which platform each school uses. Clarify all deadlines. A gentle reminder before deadlines is acceptable but "always express appreciation rather than pressure."

## Timing
**Early to mid-November is ideal** — before the winter rush of report cards, conferences, and holidays. Asking in December creates time pressure on teachers who are managing end-of-term work.

## What Parents Should NOT Do — Critical Guidance

This is the most important finding from this source:

"Although this is considerate, schools prefer the teachers to complete these forms completely from the teacher's perspective in their classroom. **These letters are 100% confidential and a key part of the admissions process and parents' voices should not play a role in this aspect of the application.**"

Parents should NOT:
- Provide bullet points of what to highlight
- Suggest talking points or themes
- Send the teacher anecdotes to include
- Review the form before the teacher submits it
- Even well-intentioned summaries of the child's strengths can undermine the authenticity that admissions readers are evaluating

## What Makes an Effective Recommendation

Effective recommendations include:
- Specific classroom observations ("She noticed when a classmate was upset and asked the teacher how she could help")
- Descriptions of how the child handles challenge, frustration, and success
- Social dynamics: how the child relates to peers across different play styles
- Intellectual engagement: curiosity, follow-up questions, persistence with difficulty
- NOT a general summary of the child being "wonderful" — that tells admissions nothing they can't read in the parent statement

## Too Many Letters

"Too many letters does not make a stronger application!" One targeted letter from the primary preschool teacher is more valuable than multiple generic letters from high-status contacts.
