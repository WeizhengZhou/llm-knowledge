# Why Is Private Schooling So Popular in the San Francisco Bay Area?
Source: KQED — kqed.org

## Reasons Families Choose Private School

**Academic Support and Individual Attention**
Aaron Rothman worried his son would "fall through the cracks" in public schools, particularly for middle-performing students. He selected a Catholic high school offering "office hours in the morning" and "opportunity to retake tests."

**Small Class Sizes**
Alta Vista provides first-grade classes with two teachers for 16 students, compared to nearby public schools with 22 students and one teacher. This ratio allows personalized instruction.

**Faith and Values**
Ada Bajada: "We wanted just that additional support with good fundamentals, morals" aligned with Christian beliefs. Religious schools in the Bay Area tend to cost less than independent schools.

**Safety Concerns**
Bajada cited worry about "school safety" after a stabbing at her local high school. Her parents made private school a priority despite limited finances.

**Network Effect**
Researcher Julia Roehl found parents valued the "network effect" — belief that private schools create "opportunities for life" through alumni connections.

**Proximity and Community**
Some parents want walkable schools with neighborhood support for pickups and a sense of belonging.

## Reasons Families Opt Out or Stay Public

**Teaching Quality in Public Schools**
Kendall Fleming praised her public school teachers' "depth of knowledge" and responsiveness to individual children's learning needs.

**Diversity Benefits**
Advocates note diverse classrooms help students develop "critical thinking" and see peers as "individualized humans."

**Affordability**
Public school is covered by taxes — no tuition cost regardless of family income.

**Quality Public Schools Exist**
High-performing public districts — Orinda, Palo Alto, Piedmont, much of Marin — have small class sizes and parent-funded programs, operating on per-student budgets in the range of $10,000–15,000 per year with parent groups contributing another $2,000–3,000.

**SFUSD Lottery Uncertainty**
The SFUSD lottery system's unpredictability drives some families to private school as insurance — but also leads others to opt out of private school if they receive a satisfying public placement.

## Key Tension: Cost vs. Value
The article captures a Bay Area-specific tension: in communities with strong public schools, many parents conclude private school is not necessary. In communities with uncertain or underresourced public schools, private school feels like a safety net. Neither impulse is universal — the decision is highly context-dependent.
