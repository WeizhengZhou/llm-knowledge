# 5 Activities to Promote Kindergarten Readiness at Home
Source: New Horizon Academy — newhorizonacademy.net

## Five Key Skill Areas and Activities

### 1. Letter Recognition and Writing
- Play with refrigerator alphabet magnets
- Write letters in shaving cream, salt, sugar, or finger paint (multisensory approach)
- Goal: Letter recognition, not reading fluency

### 2. Number Recognition and Counting
- Count throughout daily activities (crackers at snack time, socks when folding laundry)
- Point out numbers in the environment (addresses, prices, signs)
- Play games involving finding objects by color and shape

### 3. Reading Readiness
- Run your finger under words while reading aloud to model left-to-right, top-to-bottom progression
- Play rhyming games to help children "hear similar sounds in words"
- Goal: Print awareness, phonological sensitivity — not decoding or reading words

### 4. Attention and Following Directions
- Give two- and three-step directions for routine tasks (bedtime, getting dressed)
- Play "Simon Says" with multi-step instructions
- Goal: Ability to hold and execute a sequence of instructions without visual cues

### 5. Social-Emotional Skills
- Help children identify and name their emotions
- Discuss what causes different feelings and how to communicate them
- Read books about characters experiencing emotions and discuss their responses
- Goal: Emotional vocabulary, self-regulation, empathy

## Assessment Results Context
New Horizon Academy reports: "More than 95% of New Horizon Academy pre-k children meet or exceed the standards for kindergarten preparedness in all learning domains." (2023–2024 internal assessments)

## Note on Purpose of These Activities
These activities build genuine developmental readiness — they are NOT test preparation or coaching. They develop the underlying skills (attention, curiosity, social-emotional regulation) that schools observe during playdates and assessments. The distinction: enriching daily life = appropriate. Drilling specific test-like tasks = coaching.
