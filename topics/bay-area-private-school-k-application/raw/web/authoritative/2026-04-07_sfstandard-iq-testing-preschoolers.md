# How Gifted Is Your 3-Year-Old? IQ Tests for Preschoolers Become the Norm in Silicon Valley
Source: SF Standard — sfstandard.com/2025/12/07/bay-area-preschooler-iq-test-admission/
Published: December 7, 2025

## Schools & Score Requirements

Five Peninsula schools dominate the Bay Area gifted education landscape requiring IQ testing for kindergarten admission:

- **Nueva (Hillsborough):** Most explicit requirement — minimum IQ score of 130 (approximately 98th percentile)
- **Synapse (Menlo Park):** Accepts IQ tests "if it will help us understand your child"
- **Helios (Sunnyvale):** Requires testing but does not publish a minimum score range
- **Harker (San Jose):** Requires cognitive assessment administered by a licensed psychologist
- **Tessellations (Cupertino):** Uses in-house assessment deemed "more equitable than IQ scores"

Tuition ranges from $45,000 for pre-K to $65,000 for upper grades.

## Testing Details

The WPPSI (Wechsler Preschool and Primary Scale of Intelligence) is the industry standard for this age group. Cost: approximately $850 per assessment. Testing lasts 1–2 hours and measures problem-solving, memory, verbal and visual abilities, and picture naming. Children are typically tested between ages 3–4, in advance of kindergarten application season.

Score expectations vary: Some schools (notably Nueva) publicly require 130+; others informally expect a minimum around 110.

## Parent Experiences

One Peninsula mother described anxiety during her 4-year-old daughter's test: "I felt nervous, as I wasn't sure if she would get upset leaving me." Her daughter scored above 130 and gained Nueva acceptance.

Parents report feeling "anxious and desperate" over limited spots (14–16 pre-K openings at Nueva annually), despite some initially being unsure about private school.

## Psychologist Perspectives

**Natalie Wager** (Mindboost Services): "It's steadily increasing from one year to the next" in demand for IQ assessments. Conducts over 100 assessments per year for Bay Area private school applicants.

**Rachel Holowicki** (Brain Waves Educational Psychology): Receives "five or six calls a week" from high-achieving families. Notes that 20% of her clients score 130+, reminding parents that an IQ test is "a snapshot in time."

**Frank Worrell** (UC Berkeley): Giftedness correlates strongly with socioeconomic status and opportunity access. IQ fluctuates; a 4-year-old's score may differ significantly by age 18. The scores measure current ability, not immutable potential.

## Retesting and Gaming the System

Neuropsychologists report families attempting to retake tests or hire coaches. **Natalie Wager** warns she disqualifies scores from children tested recently due to "practice effect," seeing a couple of such cases per year. Children who have seen test materials cannot reliably retest for at least 12 months.

**Tsunami Turner** (Calm Down Kids, a test-prep service): Parents arrive "highly anxious," sometimes with specific target numbers. When results disappoint, they request retests.

## Context: Why Now

California defunded Gifted and Talented Education (GATE) in 2013, making gifted programs discretionary per district. This shortage of public gifted programs drives the private school testing surge as families seek alternatives to depleted public options.

## Child Testing Experience (From Multiple Psychologist Accounts)
The assessment is conducted one-on-one with a licensed psychologist in a private office setting. The tasks are designed to feel game-like for young children — blocks, picture cards, puzzles. Duration: approximately 1–2 hours. Psychologists build rapport with children before beginning formal tasks. Most young children do not experience the session as a "test."
