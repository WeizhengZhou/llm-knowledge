# Wechsler Preschool and Primary Scale of Intelligence — Key Facts
Source: Wikipedia — en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wechsler_Preschool_and_Primary_Scale_of_Intelligence
(Used as factual reference for age ranges and structure, cross-referenced against authoritative sources)

## Age Ranges — WPPSI-IV vs WISC-V

**WPPSI-IV:**
- Age range: 2 years, 6 months through 7 years, 7 months (2:6–7:7)
- Standard assessment for preschool and early elementary cognitive evaluation
- Most commonly used for Bay Area private K admissions (4–5 year old applicants)

**WISC-V:**
- Age range: 6 years through 16 years, 11 months (6:0–16:11)
- For school-age children and adolescents
- Overlaps with WPPSI-IV for children aged 6:0–7:7

## Overlap Zone — Clinical Decision
For children aged 6:0–7:7, clinicians choose between WPPSI-IV and WISC-V. Research shows: "WPPSI-IV and WISC-V produce partly different cognitive profiles, and these differences are important to recognize when choosing which test to use and when interpreting the results of clinical assessments of children in this age group." (PubMed 35996965, FinSwed study 2022)

## For Bay Area K Admissions Context

**Nueva School** (from their published admissions page):
- Accepts WPPSI-IV for children between 3 years 10 months and 5 years 11 months
- Accepts WISC-V for children 6 years old or older

**Helios School** (from official assessments page):
- Under age 6: WPPSI-IV required
- Age 6+: WISC-V required

**Harker School** (from official test FAQ):
- TK applicants: WPPSI-IV only
- K applicants: WPPSI-IV or WISC-V depending on age at testing

## Practical Summary for Parents

The vast majority of Bay Area kindergarten applicants (age 4–5 at time of testing) will take the WPPSI-IV. A child would take the WISC-V for K admissions only if they are 6 years old or older at the time of testing — which applies to late K applicants, older sib-group applicants, or children repeating K who reapply.

## Retesting Policies (Cross-Referenced)
From multiple psychologist sources and Helios official policy: the standard waiting period after exposure to test content is 12 months. Practice effect is a documented psychometric concern — children who have seen test items perform artificially higher on retesting, rendering scores invalid for admissions purposes.
