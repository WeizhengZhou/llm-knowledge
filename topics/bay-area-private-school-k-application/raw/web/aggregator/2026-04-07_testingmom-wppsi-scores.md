# WPPSI-IV Index Scores — Parent-Facing Explanation
Source: TestingMom.com — testingmom.com/tests/wppsi-test/how-is-the-wppsi-scored/

## Five Primary Index Scores (Ages 4:0–7:7)

**Verbal Comprehension Index (VCI):**
Assesses "acquired knowledge, verbal reasoning and comprehension skills, and ability to pay attention to verbal stimuli." In daily life, a child with strong VCI speaks clearly, has a large vocabulary, answers "why" questions thoughtfully, and retells stories with detail. A child with weak VCI may struggle to find words or explain reasoning aloud, even when they understand concepts internally.

**Visual Spatial Index (VSI):**
Evaluates capacity to "attend to visual details, organize visual information, understand part-whole relationships." Measured through Block Design (recreating patterns with blocks) and Object Assembly (puzzles). Strong VSI in daily life: child is good at puzzles, Legos, building with blocks, noticing visual patterns. Weak VSI: difficulty with shape-based toys, puzzles, or drawing from a model.

**Fluid Reasoning Index (FRI):**
Measures "fluid and inductive reasoning skills, broad visual intelligence, simultaneous processing, conceptual thinking." Strong FRI: child figures out how things work without being told, finds patterns, applies learning to new situations. Weak FRI: struggles when the "rules" of a situation change, needs explicit instructions for every variation.

**Working Memory Index (WMI):**
Tests "visual working memory" and "the child's ability to resist proactive interference" using attention and concentration. In daily life, strong WMI: can remember a multi-step instruction ("first put on your shoes, then get your backpack, then wait by the door"). Weak WMI: loses track of multi-step sequences, easily distracted mid-task.

**Processing Speed Index (PSI):**
Assesses "ability to quickly and correctly complete scanning and sequencing questions." Strong PSI: fast, accurate completion of tasks requiring visual scanning. Weak PSI: child knows the answer but is slow to produce it; may make errors under time pressure. Important note: PSI weakness is common in gifted children who are perfectionists and check their work repeatedly.

## Four Ancillary Index Scores

**Vocabulary Acquisition Index (VAI):** Indicates "how quickly a child acquires new receptive and expressive vocabulary skills."

**Nonverbal Index (NVI):** "Estimates a child's cognitive ability using subtests that don't require a verbal response." Useful for English language learners or children with language delays.

**General Ability Index (GAI):** Estimates general ability "relying less on working memory and processing speed." Used when WMI or PSI scores are significantly depressed (often due to ADHD or learning differences) to get a more accurate picture of the child's reasoning capacity. A child with GAI significantly higher than FSIQ may have strong intellectual ability masked by attention or processing challenges.

**Cognitive Proficiency Index (CPI):** Reflects "how efficiently a student processes cognitive information."

## Standard Score Ranges

| Score Range | Classification | % of Children |
|-------------|---------------|----------------|
| Below 70 | Extremely Low | <2% |
| 70–79 | Borderline | ~7% |
| 80–89 | Low Average | ~16% |
| 90–109 | Average | ~50% |
| 110–119 | High Average | ~16% |
| 120–129 | Superior | ~7% |
| 130+ | Very Superior / Gifted | ~2% |

Average score is 100. Most children score between 85 and 115. Standard deviation is 15.

## Testing Format
- Duration: approximately 45–60 minutes for ages 4:0–7:7
- Format: one-on-one with a licensed psychologist
- For ages 4:0–7:7: up to 14 subtests administered across the five index areas

## FSIQ vs. GAI — The Key Distinction
The FSIQ is the overall composite score. The GAI is calculated when working memory and processing speed are substantially lower than other scores. If a child scores 130 on VCI/VSI/FRI but 100 on WMI/PSI, their FSIQ might be 118 while their GAI is 130+. Schools that require "FSIQ 130" may or may not accept GAI in lieu — parents should ask each school directly.
