# WPPSI-IV Testing Session — What Happens and How Long It Takes
Source: TestingMom.com — testingmom.com/tests/wppsi-test/

## Format and Duration

- "Typically takes less than an hour and is given one-on-one by a trained psychologist"
- For K-age children (4:0–7:7): approximately 45–60 minutes total
- All sessions are conducted one-on-one; no other children in the room

## Age-Based Test Structure

**For children ages 2:6–3:11 years:** Five subtests are administered:
- Receptive Vocabulary
- Block Design
- Information
- Object Assembly
- Picture Naming

**For children ages 4:0–7:7 years (standard K-applicant range):** These receive the initial five subtests plus nine additional ones:
- Vocabulary
- Picture Concepts
- Symbol Search
- Word Reasoning
- Coding
- Comprehension
- Picture Completion
- Similarities
- Matrix Reasoning

## What the Tasks Feel Like From a Child's Perspective

Subtests are designed to feel game-like:

- **Block Design:** The child recreates a pattern shown in a picture using colored blocks. Time-limited. Involves physical manipulation — the child uses their hands.
- **Zoo Locations:** The child views animal cards placed in a pattern for a set time, then must recreate the pattern from memory. Tests visual working memory.
- **Picture Concepts:** Child sees rows of pictures and selects one from each row to identify items that share a characteristic. No reading required.

The test includes "short, game-like tasks that engage young children" and "shorter, more game-like activities to keep the testing experience appropriate for this age group."

## What the Psychologist Does

The licensed psychologist:
1. Builds rapport with the child before formal testing begins (typically 5–10 minutes of warm-up conversation and low-stakes activities)
2. Presents each subtest using standardized materials and scripted instructions
3. Records responses; timing is built into certain subtests
4. Manages pace — psychologists are trained to recognize child fatigue and can adjust timing within professional standards
5. Does not coach or encourage specific answers during the test

## What Happens If a Child Refuses a Task

Professionally trained psychologists have standardized protocols for refusals. If a child refuses a subtest or becomes distressed, the psychologist records the response as a non-response and moves on. A consistently refused section may result in an incomplete index score, which limits the validity of that index.

## The Physical Setting

Conducted in a private office — not a school or group setting. Usually a comfortable, child-friendly room with a small table and chairs. Parents typically wait outside during the session.

## Purpose Statement from Multiple Sources

The WPPSI-IV provides schools with "a snapshot of the child's skills and developmental level at the time of assessment." Results can be shared with multiple schools (parent permission required for each school).
