# The 3 Most Common Kindergarten Admissions Mistakes Parents Make
Source: TestingMom.com / Karen Quinn — testingmom.com/blog/common-kindergarten-admissions-mistakes/

## Mistake 1: Pursuing Only "Best" Schools Instead of Right-Fit Schools

Parents focus exclusively on schools labeled "the best in town" rather than identifying their own priorities. Karen Quinn emphasizes: "The best school in town is one that fits your child and family."

Families should evaluate schools based on personal criteria like:
- Location and daily logistics
- School size and classroom feel
- Academic approach (progressive vs. traditional)
- Community diversity commitment
- Values of other families in the community

## Mistake 2: Limited Option Exploration

Many parents limit themselves unnecessarily. Example failures:
- Assuming private school is unaffordable without researching scholarships
- Dismissing co-ed vs. single-sex without visiting both types
- Only applying to 1–2 schools ("creating more school switches and destabilization for the child than necessary")

Consultants recommend: apply to 5–7 schools; plan for a realistic range.

## Mistake 3: Inadequate Test Preparation — The Controversial Coaching Line

This is the most provocative section of Quinn's guidance. Key quote:

> "Admissions directors will tell you not to do anything to prepare your child for the test, saying 'Just trust that through life and preschool, he's learned everything he needs to know.' Do not listen to them — other parents will be preparing their kids, and sending a child in cold for such an important IQ or intelligence test is putting him at a disadvantage."

Quinn's position: strategic, low-pressure practice helps children feel more comfortable on test day — not by teaching test items, but by reducing test-day anxiety and unfamiliarity with format.

## What This Means for the Coaching Line

The professional community is split:
- **Schools and most licensed psychologists:** Warn against any test preparation, citing practice effects and score invalidation risks
- **Some test-prep consultants (like Quinn):** Argue that familiarizing children with test-like tasks (blocks, puzzles) reduces anxiety without corrupting scores

The practical consensus: activities that build genuine skills (puzzles, blocks, vocabulary, memory games) are appropriate. Exposing children to actual WPPSI-IV test content or scoring their practice attempts = problematic coaching that can invalidate scores.
