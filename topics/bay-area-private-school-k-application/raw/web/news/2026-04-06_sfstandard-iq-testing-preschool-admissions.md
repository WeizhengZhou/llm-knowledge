# Bay Area IQ Testing for Private School Admissions (SF Standard, Dec 2025)

## Schools Requiring IQ Tests

- **Nueva (Hillsborough)**: Most explicit requirement — IQ score of at least 130 required
- **Helios (Sunnyvale)**: Requires IQ test but doesn't publish specific score range
- **Synapse (Menlo Park)**: Welcomes IQ tests "if it will help us understand your child"
- **Harker (San Jose)**: Requires cognitive assessment with psychologist; IQ test deadline January 23, 2026 (TK/K)
- **Tessellations (Cupertino)**: Uses in-house assessment instead of external IQ scores

Other elite Peninsula private schools use IQ testing as gatekeeping, though not all publicly advertise this requirement.

## Testing Costs & Logistics

**Test Cost**: $850 per assessment

**The Test**: Wechsler Preschool and Primary Scale of Intelligence (WPPSI)
- Duration: 1-2 hours
- Age range: Designed for ages 2+, typically administered to 3-4 year-olds
- Measures: Problem-solving, short-term memory, verbal/visual strengths, picture naming

**Demand**: Neuropsychologist Natalie Wager conducts over 100 assessments per admissions season (September-January)

## Parent Experiences

One Peninsula mother described accompanying her almost-4-year-old daughter to testing. The daughter, who read at age 2, scored above 130 and gained acceptance to Nueva. Mother noted "there's a lot of diversity" among accepted students.

## Expert Perspectives

**Frank Worrell** (UC Berkeley School Psychology): Giftedness represents "potential for outstanding accomplishments as adults" and correlates strongly with socioeconomic status. IQ fluctuates; a 4-year-old's score may differ significantly by age 18 based on educational access.

**Rachel Holowicki** (Brain Waves Educational Psychology): Reports receiving five or six calls weekly from high-achieving families. Among her clients, 20% score 130+, representing roughly the top 2% globally.

**Tsunami Turner** (Educational Psychologist): Observes parents arriving "highly anxious," often with specific target numbers in mind.

## Systemic Context

This phenomenon stems partly from California's 2013 decision to cut Gifted and Talented Education funding, making programs discretionary at district level. Many districts eliminated gifted programming entirely, prompting affluent families to pursue private alternatives costing approximately $45,000 annually for pre-K/kindergarten.

## Key Takeaway for Bay Area Families
- IQ testing ($850/test) is primarily required by Peninsula/Silicon Valley "gifted" schools
- Nueva: minimum IQ 130 explicitly required
- SF schools (Hamlin, CDS, SF Day, Live Oak, etc.) do NOT typically require IQ tests
- Most SF school kindergarten "assessments" are unstructured playdates/observations, not formal testing

Source: SF Standard (authoritative Bay Area news outlet), December 7, 2025
