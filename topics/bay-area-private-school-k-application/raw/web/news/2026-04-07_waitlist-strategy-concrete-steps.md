# How to Get Off a Private School Waitlist: Comprehensive Guide

## Sources

Primary: xceedprep.org (detailed guide)
Secondary: privateschoolreview.com, beyondthebrochurela.com, ahschool.com, spruce-advisors.com

## Understanding Waitlist Status

A waitlist decision means the admissions office found your child qualified, but seats filled with other applicants first. Think of it as "qualified but on hold" rather than rejection. Schools use waitlists for yield management — when accepted students decline offers, waitlisted candidates fill available spots.

## Realistic Acceptance Rates

- **Highly competitive schools**: approximately 5-15% of waitlisted students gain admission
- **Less competitive selective schools**: roughly 15-30% of waitlisted applicants succeed
- **privateschoolreview.com note**: "If she is waitlisted at a very selective school, probably not" will admission occur — treat as effectively equivalent to rejection for planning purposes

## Timeline for Bay Area Private School Waitlists (ISSFBA-specific)

ISSFBA framework (most relevant for Bay Area):
- Notification date: March 19, 2026
- Response deadline: March 26, 2026
- Waitlists move **primarily in the week between** notification (March 19) and response deadline (March 26), as families who received multiple acceptances choose one school and release others
- After March 26: contact waitlisted schools directly; some keep active waitlists, others close them
- Summer movement: continues through June-August as families change plans

Schools use two waitlist systems:
- **Ranked waitlists**: applicants ordered by preference
- **Wait pools**: candidates under consideration without specific ordering; selection based on class composition needs

## Four Proven Concrete Steps

**1. Confirm Immediately**
Respond promptly to confirm your interest in remaining on the list. Lack of response may result in removal.

**2. Send Letter of Continued Interest (LOCI)**
This should:
- Reaffirm strong interest (explicitly state this is your first-choice school if true)
- Highlight any new information since application (awards, new preschool assessment, updated records)
- Explain specific fit with school programs and mission
- Keep professional and concise — one thoughtful update beats multiple messages
- Avoid desperation or demands in tone

**3. Ask Strategic Questions**
Politely inquire whether the waitlist is ranked or pooled, and typical decision timelines. This signals genuine engagement and provides useful information.

**4. Attend Invited Events**
Participate in any campus programs or events schools offer to waitlisted families, demonstrating genuine commitment.

## What Admissions Offices Need

When seats open, schools fill them based on class composition needs:
- Zipcodes/neighborhoods underrepresented in current class
- Cultural/ethnic/socioeconomic diversity
- Gender balance
- Specific preschool backgrounds not already represented

A waitlisted family can improve chances by understanding what the school still needs and authentically demonstrating fit in those dimensions.

## What NOT to Do

- Do not pester admissions staff with repeated calls or emails
- "Don't call us. We'll call you" policy applies (privateschoolreview.com)
- Avoid aggressive parent networking to pressure decisions
- Do not threaten to withdraw interest if not quickly admitted

## ISSFBA-Specific Note (Bay Area)

From qE002 research: once a family signs with one ISSFBA school, other ISSFBA schools "do not typically offer a contract." This creates a brief window of movement (March 19-26) and then a slower secondary movement period.

## Backup Strategy (Required)

Maintain deposit at an accepted school while on waitlist. If waitlist offer arrives and you accept, you will forfeit the deposit at your backup school. This is ethically acceptable and expected.

## spruce-advisors.com — Bay Area Decision Week Context

If an admissions director tells you to accept a spot elsewhere, interpret this as an indication your child likely won't be admitted from the wait-list.

Schools expect responses within one week of March 19. "Do your homework now: run through decision trees in advance. You will think more clearly before the clock begins ticking."

## Probability Summary

Most waitlist movement: first 1-2 weeks after March 26 response deadline
Secondary movement: through June, as families' plans change
Late movement: August (last-minute withdrawals before school starts)
Overall acceptance from waitlist at top SF schools: 5-15%
