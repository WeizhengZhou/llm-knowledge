# Public vs. Private School Decision Framework — Bay Area (RO5 Concept Gap)

## The Core Financial Framing

The aggregate cost of private school K-12 in the Bay Area is often described as the "$500K decision." Assumptions:
- Average Bay Area private independent school tuition: approximately $40,000-$50,000/year
- Typical annual tuition increases: 4-5%
- 13 years of education (K-12): estimated $500,000-$700,000 total per child
- Parochial/religious private schools: $6,000-$10,000/year (significantly lower)

## Key Decision Factors (Bay Area-Specific)

**Financial:**
- Net cost after financial aid (some schools offer aid to 30-88% of families)
- Opportunity cost: $40K/year invested would compound significantly over 13 years
- Two-income families may have less capacity than the gross tuition suggests
- ISSFBA advises: complete detailed financial analysis before applying, not after

**Academic:**
- Bay Area public schools vary dramatically by district: Palo Alto, Berkeley, Piedmont have strong reputations; SFUSD has more variability
- Private schools typically offer smaller class sizes (14-18 vs. 25-30) and more resources
- Public school lottery/assignment uncertainty (especially in SF) drives some families to private applications as a hedge
- Private school academic programs include more specialized options: IB, Montessori, Waldorf, gifted, language immersion

**Social/Developmental:**
- Bay Area public schools exhibit more ethnic and socioeconomic diversity on average
- Private schools have higher average parental income/education; network effects matter to some families
- Class sizes and student-teacher ratios are systematically lower in private schools
- Private schools typically have more robust arts, sports, and enrichment programs

**Practical:**
- Extended care: many Bay Area private schools include before/after care in tuition or offer it affordably
- Public school lottery risk (particularly SF): some families apply to private schools primarily as insurance
- Sibling continuity: once enrolled in private K, there is strong preference to continue through the school's grade span (K-5, K-8)

## Decision Framework (Expert-Synthesized from Search Results)

ISSFBA recommends families work through:
1. What is our actual financial capacity (after realistic aid estimate)?
2. What educational philosophy fits our child?
3. What is our public school alternative (specific school, not "public school" generally)?
4. How much do class size, teacher continuity, and program breadth matter to us?
5. What is our plan B if rejected from all private schools?
