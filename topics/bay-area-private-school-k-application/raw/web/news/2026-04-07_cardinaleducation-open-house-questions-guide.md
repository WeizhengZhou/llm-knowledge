# Cardinal Education's Guide to Private School Open Houses and Tours

## Overview

Cardinal Education emphasizes that attending open houses and tours is "one of the best ways to get a feel for a school's culture, academics, and community." These visits matter significantly in admissions, as schools view attendance as evidence of genuine interest.

## Key Preparation Steps

Before visiting, families should:
- Research the school's mission, academic programs, and extracurricular offerings
- Understand application deadlines and admission standards
- Review student life aspects like programs and clubs
- Read parent and student testimonials for authentic perspectives

## What to Expect at Open House Events

Typical open houses include:
- Welcome sessions with administration
- Campus tours led by students or faculty
- Classroom observations
- Q&A panels with students and teachers
- Extracurricular showcases
- Sometimes individual meetings with admissions representatives

## Essential Questions to Ask (Beyond What's on the Website)

**For Admissions Officers:**
- What distinguishes this school from competitors?
- What qualities matter most in applications?
- What's the acceptance rate?
- What financial aid options exist?
- How does the school handle learning differences?

**For Faculty:**
- What's the student-to-teacher ratio?
- How is academic support provided for struggling students?
- How does the school communicate with parents?
- How do teachers differentiate instruction for different learners?

**For Current Students:**
- What aspects do you value most about this school?
- What surprised you upon arrival?
- What's a typical school day like?
- What would you change about the school?

**For Current Parents (if accessible):**
- How are parent volunteers utilized?
- What is the annual fund expectation?
- How does the school handle conflicts between families?
- How diverse is the parent community?

## Beyond the Brochure: What to Observe

Walk the campus noting:
- Student engagement and happiness levels
- Whether classes feel discussion-based and dynamic or rote
- Student-teacher interactions (warmth, respect, responsiveness)
- Hallway behavior (are students excited, subdued, stressed?)
- Facility condition and what's displayed on walls (student work?)
- Common spaces: how do students interact during unstructured time?

Pay attention to "student interactions, classroom environment, common spaces, and facilities." Trust your instinct about whether your child could thrive there.

## Kindergarten-Specific Observations

For K-entry schools specifically, additional observations:
- How do current kindergarteners interact with each other?
- What does the kindergarten classroom look like (play-based materials vs. academic worksheets)?
- How do kindergarten teachers speak about children's development?
- What does a typical kindergarten day look like?

## The Role of Admissions Consultants

Professional consultants can help families prepare effectively, identify which questions to prioritize, and understand subtle admissions dynamics that might otherwise go unnoticed.
