# Private School Choice in the Bay Area: KQED Analysis

## Enrollment Statistics

**Regional private school enrollment rates (2023-24):**
- San Francisco: 30% of K-12 students attend private schools
- Santa Clara County: 16%
- San Mateo County: 17%
- Marin County: 19%
- California statewide average: 8%

Comparisons:
- Los Angeles: 10.4% private
- Sacramento: 7.5%
- Fresno: under 3%

## Tuition Costs

Private school annual tuition ranges from $10,000 to $65,000 in San Francisco:
- Alta Vista (K-8): Over $41,000 annually
- Catholic high school: Approximately $30,000 yearly
- Financial aid available at some institutions; acceptance rates below two-thirds of applicants

## Why Families Choose Private Schools

**Academic Considerations:**
- Small class sizes: 16 students with 2 teachers (private) vs. 22 with 1 teacher (public)
- Individualized attention
- Support for middle-achieving students who might "fall through the cracks"

**Safety and Values:**
- Prioritizing "safe learning environment"
- Schools that "nurture intellectual, social, and emotional skills"
- Religious families value faith-based instruction and moral development

**Network Effects:**
- Parents believe private schools provide "network effect" benefits
- Community and neighborhood school feel

## Public School Concerns

Stanford economist Tom Dee: "concerns about the quality and stability" of public schools drive private enrollment. Some concerns aren't evidence-based; advocates emphasize rumors sometimes outweigh actual classroom performance.

## Historical Context

The high private school rate in SF traces to 1970s desegregation. When SF implemented busing for racial integration, "white flight" occurred — over 20,000 white students departed SFUSD. This created "a permanent shift away from public education" that persists today.

## Systemic Consequences

When 4,000 students left SFUSD over seven years, the district lost $80 million in per-pupil funding ($20,000 per child average). Fixed infrastructure costs plus declining enrollment forces budget cuts, eroding community confidence in a self-reinforcing cycle.
