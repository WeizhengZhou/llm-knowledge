# SF Private School Admissions Guide for Working Parents — Ruth Krishnan

## Time Commitment Reality

The admissions process demands significant dedication. Families should anticipate attending **20-35 events** across 5-7 schools between August and January, with each institution requiring 4-6 visits including information sessions, open houses, tours, and community events.

## Attending Events: What Actually Matters

**Missing events won't automatically disqualify applicants.** Most schools don't track attendance at open houses or optional community activities like sporting events. According to the guide: "Schools remember disruptive families more than absent ones." The critical factor is avoiding negative impressions — arriving late, interrupting presentations, or asking inappropriate questions creates memorable issues.

However, optional events provide valuable cultural insights. Families gain authentic understanding of daily school life through casual observation rather than polished admissions presentations.

## Managing Weekday Logistics

Working parents face genuine scheduling conflicts. The guide acknowledges that prime tour slots and interview times frequently occur during business hours, requiring workplace flexibility or personal time allocation. Many schools now offer evening and weekend alternatives specifically for employed families.

**Strategic approach:** Calendar blocking protects admissions time like a part-time job, and delegating tasks between spouses helps balance competing demands.

One school example: a highly competitive school opened tour registration at noon on a Wednesday, and all slots filled within 15 minutes — leaving working parents who couldn't respond immediately waitlisted for basic information sessions.

## Does Lower Event Attendance Penalize Applicants?

Head-Royce administrators: events are "for the family to make sure the school is a good fit for their child, not vice versa." The school does not penalize families for missing optional events.

One administrator noted that attending shows you're excited about the school and they're more likely to recognize your name when reviewing applications, but "not attending shouldn't strike against you."

## Financial Considerations

Beyond tuition ($45,000-55,000 annually), budget includes:
- Application fees: $100-200 per school
- Professional consultant services: $3,000-15,000
- Educational testing: $500-1,500
- Annual fundraising expectations: $1,000-10,000+

## Critical Success Strategies

1. **Apply to balanced school portfolios** including reach, likely, and safety options — applying to only ultra-competitive institutions increases rejection likelihood
2. **Start research 2-3 years early** to spread workload and build authentic relationships
3. **Focus on mission alignment** over perfecting applications
4. **Maintain authentic student voice** in essays; over-edited applications signal lack of fit
5. **Begin relationship building early** with current families and school communities

## For Working Parents Specifically

The guide emphasizes that "schools value diverse family structures and employment situations." Success doesn't require maximum event attendance but rather strategic participation focused on gathering essential information and building genuine connections.

Delegation, realistic expectations about emotional toll, and peer support from other families currently navigating admissions provide essential sustenance through the demanding process.

## Typical Admissions Timeline

October, November, December: tours, informational sessions, open houses
January: application deadlines
March: acceptance notices
August: school starts

Detailed timeline: August (initial info sessions/open houses), September-October (tours + begin applications), November (submit applications), December-January (family interviews).
