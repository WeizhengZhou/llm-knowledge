# Not Accepted at Private Schools: Next Steps and Reapplication — Berkeley Parents Network

Source: Synthesis from BPN search result, ruthkrishnan.com, privateschoolreview.com, and beyondthebrochurela.com

## Immediate Next Steps When Rejected

**Recommended approach when rejected from all applied private schools:**
1. Enroll your child in your local public kindergarten — you are guaranteed a spot in public school, but not in a particular school
2. Reapply to private elementary schools for the following year (1st grade entry, or kindergarten at schools that have a second TK/K round)

## Why Rejections Happen

Rejections are very common in the competitive Bay Area private elementary school environment. The admissions process is subjective — not random, but not a hard science. Rejection often reflects **enrollment management** rather than student inadequacy:
- Schools balance class composition by gender, ethnicity, preschool distribution, neighborhood
- Limited seats create mechanical rejection even for qualified applicants
- Some families are rejected simply because of timing or yield calculations

## Reapplication Strategy

Many families are admitted in the second year they apply (either for 1st grade or kindergarten):

- **Expand the number of schools**: apply to more schools next year to increase options
- **Ask your preschool director for feedback**: they may have spoken to admissions directors and gained information about your application
- **Reapply to same schools**: applying to the same schools for a second time signals strong interest and commitment
- **Consider different school types**: include progressive and traditional, religious and secular, SF and East Bay options
- **ISSFBA schools**: if you were rejected by all SF ISSFBA schools, consider South Bay or Peninsula options for a different competitive landscape

## Public School as a Real Option

- Federal funding addresses learning differences (504 plan, IEP)
- More ethnic and socioeconomic diversity on average
- Stronger schools: Palo Alto USD, Berkeley USD, Piedmont USD all offer excellent public K
- SFUSD lottery system creates uncertainty that drives private applications as a "hedge"

## Common Reasons for Rejection (from consultant sources)

Schools don't publicly disclose rejection reasons. Common contributing factors include:
- Poor fit with school's educational philosophy
- Class composition needs (gender balance, neighborhood distribution)
- Sibling/legacy spots consuming available seats
- Financial aid budget constraints (at need-aware schools)
- Application arrived after highly competitive candidates secured seats

## Emotional Recovery

Rejections are painful. Counselors recommend:
- Allow a grief period
- Avoid attributing rejection to child inadequacy
- Focus on public school as a genuine option, not a failure option
- Many children thrive in excellent public schools and later gain private admission for middle school
