# Developmental Milestones for 4-Year-Olds
Source: CDC "Learn the Signs. Act Early." — cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/4-years.html

## Social/Emotional
- "Pretends to be something else during play (teacher, superhero, dog)"
- "Asks to go play with children if none are around"
- "Comforts others who are hurt or sad"
- "Avoids danger, like not jumping from tall heights"
- "Likes to be a 'helper'"
- "Changes behavior based on where she is"

## Language/Communication
- "Says sentences with four or more words"
- "Says some words from a song, story, or nursery rhyme"
- "Talks about at least one thing that happened during her day"
- "Answers simple questions like 'What is a coat for?'"

## Cognitive
- "Names a few colors of items"
- "Tells what comes next in a well-known story"
- "Draws a person with three or more body parts"

## Gross Motor
- "Catches a large ball most of the time"

## Fine Motor
- "Serves herself food or pours water, with adult supervision"
- "Unbuttons some buttons"
- "Holds crayon or pencil between fingers and thumb (not a fist)"

## Context Note from CDC
Developmental milestones are things most children (75% or more) can do by a certain age. If your child is not meeting one or more milestones, has lost skills he or she once had, or you have other concerns, act early. These milestones represent typical development, not exceptional development — they are the floor, not the ceiling.
