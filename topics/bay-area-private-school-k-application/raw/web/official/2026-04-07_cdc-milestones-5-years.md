# Developmental Milestones for 5-Year-Olds
Source: CDC "Learn the Signs. Act Early." — cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/5-years.html

## Social/Emotional
- "Follows rules or takes turns when playing games with other children"
- "Sings, dances, or acts for you"
- "Does simple chores at home, like matching socks or clearing the table"

## Language/Communication
- Narrates stories with multiple connected events
- "Answers simple questions about a book or story after you read or tell it"
- Maintains extended back-and-forth dialogue exchanges
- "Uses or recognizes simple rhymes (bat-cat, ball-tall)"

## Cognitive (Learning, Thinking, Problem-Solving)
- "Counts to 10"
- "Names some numbers between 1 and 5 when you point to them"
- Demonstrates understanding of temporal vocabulary (yesterday, today, tomorrow)
- "Pays attention for 5 to 10 minutes during activities"
- Writes some letters contained in their name
- Identifies letter names when pointed out

## Movement/Physical Development
- "Buttons some buttons"
- "Hops on one foot"

## CDC Definition Note
The CDC defines milestones as skills "most children (75% or more) can do by a certain age." These materials supplement, but do not replace, formal developmental screening tools.
