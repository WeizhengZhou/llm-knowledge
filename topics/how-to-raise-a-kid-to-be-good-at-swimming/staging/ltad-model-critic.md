## Must-Fix Before wiki/ Graduation

1. **D3 = 0: No "Common Mistakes" section.** This concept article is cited by [[developmental-pathway]], [[preventing-burnout]], [[choosing-a-club-and-coach]], and [[early-specialization]] — it is a foundational decision-support article. Parents who read it will make specific LTAD-related errors that the article should name. Required additions:
   - "Treating LTAD stage ages as prescriptive deadlines: A child who is 9 but developmentally at the FUNdamentals stage does not need to be forced into 'Learn to Train' because they turned 8. Stage ages are approximate — biological maturity, not chronological age, should drive stage assignment."
   - "Evaluating a club's LTAD alignment only by what the coach says, not what the training schedule shows: A coach who claims to follow LTAD but runs 8-year-olds through 6-day-per-week practice schedules is not LTAD-aligned. Ask to see the actual training group structure for the youngest age groups, not just the coaching philosophy statement."
   - "Confusing 'Active for Life' (Stage 7) as failure: A child who swims recreationally into adulthood without reaching the competitive peak is a successful LTAD outcome. This is the most common result and should be celebrated, not treated as a development failure."

## Should-Fix

1. **The "AAP Hours Guideline" sub-section** appears in this article, in [[developmental-pathway]], and in [[preventing-burnout]] with nearly identical wording. This article is the concept article where the guideline belongs as context for LTAD volume principles. The guide articles should cite the guideline briefly and wikilink here or to [[preventing-burnout]] for the full explanation. Consider whether the sub-section in this article should be the canonical location, and whether the compiler should trim the identical text in the other two articles.

2. **"Stage 7 (Active for Life) is a success outcome" is stated in the Key Principles but not explained enough.** Parents who have invested years in their child's swimming will struggle to reframe recreational participation as success. Add one sentence: "In practice, the vast majority of competitive youth swimmers reach Stage 7 rather than Stage 6 — this is the expected statistical outcome, not an exception."
