# Critic Report — Swimming and Other Sports — 2026-04-09

## Overall Assessment

A useful concept article that covers complementary sports and cross-training well. The water polo section is a helpful extension for families already thinking about aquatic pathways. The "Conflict Areas" and "Scheduling Strategies" sections are genuinely decision-useful. The article is missing a "Common Mistakes" section, and the RO6 must-answer item on cross-training benefits between swimming and other activities is answered only in the abstract — parents need more specific guidance on which sports produce which benefits.

## Must-Fix Before wiki/ Graduation

1. **D3 = 0: No "Common Mistakes" section.** Parents make specific errors when navigating multi-sport participation alongside swimming. Required additions:
   - "Letting the swim coach make the call about other sports: A swim coach has an incentive for your child to prioritize swimming. Do not ask the swim coach whether your child should also play soccer — that decision belongs to the family and the pediatrician, informed by the LTAD model, not to the coach whose program benefits from full attendance."
   - "Scheduling both sports at peak intensity simultaneously: Signing up for competitive swimming and competitive soccer in the same season at full intensity is not multi-sport participation — it is double overtraining. Multi-sport participation works when one sport is primary and the other is at a reduced or recreational commitment level, or they are in different seasons."
   - "Assuming a reduced swim schedule during another sport's season will damage development: Most clubs accommodate reduced attendance during another sport's season for swimmers under age 12. A swimmer who misses 4-6 weeks of swimming for soccer season and then returns does not lose meaningful competitive ground at the developmental level."

## Should-Fix

1. **Complementary sports section is a list without decision guidance.** Parents reading that "gymnastics develops core strength, body awareness, and flexibility that directly transfer to swimming" need to know what to do with that — specifically, whether gymnastics is a better second sport than soccer for a child already showing swimming aptitude. Add one sentence per sport recommending when it is optimal as a second sport alongside swimming: e.g., "Gymnastics is the highest-transfer second sport for swimmers in the 5-10 age range, before the scheduling demands of both sports conflict. If your child is open to a second sport and is young, gymnastics is the first choice."

2. **The "Skill Transfer Principles" paragraph contains an unattributed claim:** "Research on elite athletes has found that the greater the number of activities experienced in developing years (ages 0-12), the less sport-specific practice was necessary to acquire expertise." This claim is presented as research consensus without a specific citation. The fact-checker flagged the 88% college athlete statistic; this claim should receive similar treatment — attribute it to a specific source or hedge it as "research suggests" with the appropriate fact-sheet permission language.

3. **Bay Area water polo program listing is thin.** The two Bay Area programs named (Lamorinda, West Valley) are listed with only name and a one-phrase description. For a reader who wants to explore water polo from swimming, this is useful — but it should note that both links need annual verification as club websites and program offerings change.

## Graduation Decision: NEEDS-REVISION

One must-fix: add "Common Mistakes" section. Return to wiki-compiler-agent.
