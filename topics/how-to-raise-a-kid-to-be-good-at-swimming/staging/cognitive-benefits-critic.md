## Must-Fix Before wiki/ Graduation

1. **D3 = 0: No "Common Mistakes" section.** This article is the ideal place to help parents avoid being misled by inflated claims. Required additions:
   - "Citing the '15% higher in math and science' statistic to justify swim school enrollment: This figure does not appear in the Griffith University study's own reporting. If a swim school uses this figure in their marketing materials, treat it as a sign that other claims may be similarly distorted."
   - "Treating the cognitive benefit research as proof that swimming is better than other sports for brain development: The 2024 RCT compared swimming to 'traditional exercise' — it did not compare swimming to soccer, gymnastics, or music lessons. Whether swimming is superior to other activities for cognitive development is not established by current research."
   - "Choosing to start swimming specifically for the cognitive benefits: The evidence is promising but early-stage (single RCT, small samples). Swimming is a worthwhile activity for many reasons — cognitive benefit is a bonus, not a sufficient standalone justification."

## Should-Fix

1. **The biological mechanisms section (BDNF, bilateral cross-patterning, aerobic capacity) is presented as three parallel mechanisms with similar epistemic weight.** However, the aerobic capacity mechanism is much better established than the bilateral cross-patterning / corpus callosum hypothesis, which is speculative and has limited empirical support for swimming specifically. Add a brief note distinguishing the mechanisms by strength of evidence: "Of these mechanisms, aerobic-capacity-driven cognitive benefits have the strongest empirical support. BDNF enhancement from exercise is well-documented. The bilateral cross-patterning / corpus callosum hypothesis is more speculative for swimming specifically."

2. **The parent involvement finding is the most actionable claim in the article** ("parent-accompanied swimming produced the fastest and largest IQ gains") but appears in the middle of a technical RCT description. Move this finding to the "What Parents Should Know" section with an explicit action frame: "For parents of preschool children, being in the water with your child during early lessons — not just watching from the deck — appears to amplify the cognitive benefits. If your child's swim school offers parent-and-child classes, choose them over drop-off format for children under 5."
