# Bay Area Swim Camps (2026)

## Overview

This article compares summer swim camp options in the Bay Area for the 2026 season. University-run camps (Cal, Stanford) serve ages 9-17 and require competitive experience. Club-run clinics (Alto, DACA) serve younger and developing swimmers. All pricing and dates below are for the 2026 season and will change.

## Cal (UC Berkeley) Summer Swim Camp

According to US Sports Camps, Cal Swim Camp serves ages 9-17 and requires competitive swim knowledge and experience with all four strokes; the camp does not teach beginners.

According to US Sports Camps, Cal (UC Berkeley) Summer Swim Camp 2026 sessions run June 1-3, June 8-10, June 15-17, June 22-24, and August 22-23. According to US Sports Camps, Cal Swim Camp 2026 costs $611 for 3-day sessions and $491 for the 2-day August session, with a team discount of $25 off per camper when 5+ register together.

According to US Sports Camps, Cal Swim Camp runs 9 AM-3 PM daily with a 10:1 camper-to-staff ratio at Legends Aquatics Center or Spieker Aquatic Complex at UC Berkeley.

## Stanford Summer Swim Camp

According to US Sports Camps, Stanford Swim Camp 2026 offers six sessions between June 8 and July 2, priced from $797 to $914 per session. According to US Sports Camps, Stanford Swim Camp serves ages 9-17, requires ability to complete a continuous 200-yard swim and experience with all four strokes, and is held at Avery Aquatics Center at Stanford University.

According to US Sports Camps, Stanford Swim Camp cancellation policy provides refunds if cancelled 14+ days before start (minus a $100 fee); no refunds are available within 14 days of the session start.

## Alto Swim Club Peak Performance Clinic

According to Alto Swim Club, the Peak Performance Clinic 2026 is for competitive swimmers ages 9-13, held at Gunn Pool in Palo Alto in a 4-day Monday-Thursday format (10 AM-1:30 PM) across five sessions from June 23 to July 24; pricing requires contacting the club directly.

The clinic covers all four strokes with a structured daily curriculum:
- Day 1: Freestyle and Push-Offs
- Day 2: Backstroke and Flip Turns
- Day 3: Breaststroke and Open Turns
- Day 4: Butterfly and Race Starts

Includes in-water instruction, classroom sessions, dryland workouts, and individual stroke video review.

Contact: [[alto-swim-club|Alto Swim Club]] at info@altoswimclub.com or 650-308-9110.

## DACA Summer Programs

Club sources indicate DACA offers accelerated learn-to-swim summer programs in one-week sessions from June through August, open to all ages and skill levels; current pricing requires checking daca.org directly.

DACA's summer programs are notable for serving all ages and skill levels, including beginners -- unlike the university camps which require competitive experience. See [[daca]] for more on the club.

## How to Choose

- **For competitive swimmers ages 9-17 wanting a university experience:** Cal or Stanford
- **For younger competitive swimmers (9-13) wanting technique-focused training:** Alto Peak Performance Clinic
- **For beginners or younger children needing all-level instruction:** DACA summer programs
- **Budget-conscious:** Cal ($491-$611) is significantly less expensive than Stanford ($797-$914)

## Common Mistakes

- **Booking a Stanford or Cal elite camp for a child who has not swum competitively:** Both university camps require competitive experience and all four strokes. According to US Sports Camps, Cal Swim Camp serves ages 9-17 and requires competitive swim knowledge and experience with all four strokes; the camp does not teach beginners. According to US Sports Camps, Stanford Swim Camp serves ages 9-17, requires ability to complete a continuous 200-yard swim and experience with all four strokes. A child who has only done lessons or summer league will not keep up and will have a demoralizing experience. Start with DACA's summer programs or a club-run beginner clinic instead.
- **Not checking age and skill prerequisites before registering:** Each camp has specific requirements. Registering a 7-year-old for a camp that serves ages 9-17, or a recreational swimmer for a camp requiring competitive experience, wastes money and risks discouraging the child.
- **Waiting until June to register for summer camps:** Bay Area university swim camps frequently fill months in advance. Cal and Stanford camps for popular sessions may sell out by March or April. Register as early as January for the best selection.
- **Assuming camp attendance substitutes for club team training:** A 3-day or week-long camp provides intensive technique exposure and a fun experience, but it does not replace the sustained development of a full club season. Camps are a supplement to year-round training, not a replacement.
