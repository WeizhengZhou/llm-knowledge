## Overview

According to club sources, PASA is located in Palo Alto and uses the Stanford Aquatic Center and other Peninsula facilities. PASA is described on the Alto Swim Club website as "one of California's premier age group clubs," historically strong at Far Westerns and Junior Nationals.

PASA is one of the oldest continuously operating USA Swimming clubs, founded in 1955 as the Palo Alto Swim Club (PASC). In 2003, it merged with area clubs to form Palo Alto Stanford Aquatics. The club serves approximately 1,000 competitive swimmers across six Bay Area sites (source: pasa-rinconada.org, April 2026). PASA has achieved a USA Swimming National Championship Team Title and has placed swimmers on Olympic Swimming Teams (source: pasa-rinconada.org).

## PASA and Alto Swim Club: What Happened in 2018

According to Alto Swim Club, the club was formed from the merger of Stanford Swimming and PASA Cardinal Swimming. This involved only the PASA Cardinal Site -- one of PASA's six locations. PASA as an organization continued operating at its remaining five sites without interruption. The Pacific Swimming Zone 1 North official directory lists both PASA and Alto Swim Club as separate registered clubs with distinct contacts and distinct USA Swimming abbreviations (source: pacswim.org, April 2026).

PASA and Alto are **not** the same organization. They are independently registered, independently coached, and compete under separate team codes (PASA vs. ALTO).

## PASA Sites

PASA operates across six locations in the Palo Alto area (source: pasa-rinconada.org, April 2026):

1. **Rinconada** (777 Embarcadero Road, Palo Alto) -- primary/anchor site
2. Alpine Hills
3. Foothills Tennis & Swim Club
4. Fremont Hills Country Club
5. Los Altos Golf & Country Club
6. A sixth site (identity unconfirmed in public sources)

Each site operates somewhat independently with its own coaching staff and group structure. The training group details below are specific to the Rinconada site and are illustrative of the PASA structure; other sites may differ.

## Who PASA Suits Best

PASA is well-suited for families on the Peninsula looking for a competitive club with a genuine developmental pathway from age 5 through high school. The Minnows group ($135/month, 2025-26) provides an accessible entry point for young swimmers who can already swim 25 yards unassisted -- this is a pre-competitive introduction, not a learn-to-swim program.

PASA's multi-site structure is a practical advantage: families can choose the location closest to home and potentially transfer between sites as needs change. The Rinconada monthly fees ($135--$310, 2025-26) are in a moderate range for Bay Area competitive clubs. See [[costs-and-commitment]] for broader cost context.

The Varsity group at the 13-14 level is notable -- it provides a less intensive continuation track for swimmers who want to stay competitive without the full Junior Group commitment. This is a good fit for multi-sport athletes or swimmers who prioritize swimming as one of several activities.

## Common Mistakes

- **Confusing PASA with [[alto-swim-club|Alto Swim Club]]:** These are separate organizations. Alto was formed in 2018 when one PASA site (the Cardinal/Stanford site) split off. PASA continued operating independently at five other locations. If you intend to join PASA, go to pasa-rinconada.org (or the relevant site); if you intend to join Alto, go to altoswimclub.com.
- **Assuming all PASA sites have the same groups and fees:** Each PASA site operates somewhat independently. The training group structure, coaching staff, and fees documented above are specific to the Rinconada site. Contact the individual site you plan to attend for its specific offerings.
- **Bringing a child who cannot yet swim 25 yards:** The youngest PASA Rinconada group (Minnows) requires a 25-yard unassisted swim. PASA is not a learn-to-swim program. If your child is not yet at this level, start with a swim school or rec program first (see [[getting-started]]).
- **Underestimating the commitment ramp:** PASA groups increase in commitment from 2 days/week (Minnows) to 9 sessions/week (Senior). Families should understand the multi-year trajectory before joining. See [[developmental-pathway]] for how training volume should progress by age.
