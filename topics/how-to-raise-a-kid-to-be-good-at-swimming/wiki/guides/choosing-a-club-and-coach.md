# Choosing a Club and Coach

## Overview

Selecting the right swim club and coach is one of the most consequential decisions a swim parent makes. This guide covers what credentials to look for, questions to ask, red flags to watch for, and a trial period strategy to test fit before committing.

## Coach Credentials to Verify

### USA Swimming Certification

According to USA Swimming, all coaches must complete the Quality Coaching Framework course, and membership will not be in good standing without this completion. According to USA Swimming, the coach certification program includes CPR certification, Online Safety Training, In-Water Safety Training, Welcome to USA Swimming course, and Quality Coaching Framework course, and is designed as a year-long self-paced commitment.

According to USA Swimming, new coaches must take the Core Certification; established coaches may choose either Core or one of the American Development Model (ADM) Certifications.

### Background Checks

According to USA Swimming, non-athlete coaches must pass a background check before coaching. Junior coaches ages 16-17 are exempt from background checks until they turn 18, at which point they have 30 days to complete one. According to USA Swimming, junior coaches (ages 16-17) cannot be designated as head coaches and must have another non-athlete coach present on deck at all times.

### ASCA Certification

According to ASCA, certification has five levels: Level 1 (Foundations), Level 2 (Stroke School), Level 3 (Physiology School), Level 4 (Leadership School), and Level 5 (Administration School). According to ASCA, Level 2 certification requires passing Level 1 and Level 2 courses and having coached for at least 6 months.

A coach with ASCA Level 3 or higher has demonstrated meaningful professional investment. ASCA certification is voluntary but widely respected in competitive swimming.

## Questions to Ask When Evaluating a Club

### About Coaching Philosophy

- What is your coaching philosophy?
- How do you individualize training for swimmers at different stages?
- What does a typical practice look like at each age group level?
- How do you handle conflicts between athlete and coach?

### About Training Structure

- What is the practice schedule for our age group (days/week, hours/session)?
- Does dryland (strength/conditioning) training happen, and how often?
- What is the training volume (yards per week) at different training group levels?

### About Track Record

- How long have you coached here?
- What are your ASCA certification levels?
- Are you a current USA Swimming member with background check completed?
- What championship meets have your athletes qualified for in the past 2 years?
- How recent are the fastest times on the team's all-time list?

### About Team Culture

- What is the team culture -- fun-oriented or highly competitive?
- How does the team handle poor performances by athletes?
- What is your approach when an athlete wants to quit or take a break?

### About Safety

- Is your club Safe Sport certified through USA Swimming?
- What is your policy on coach-athlete contact outside of team activities?
- Can I observe practice before my child tries out?

## Safe Sport Protections

According to USA Swimming, all adult USA Swimming members must complete USA Swimming-approved criminal background checks.

According to USA Swimming, pursuant to federal law, all adults authorized to interact with minor or amateur athletes who learn of facts giving reason to suspect child abuse must report to law enforcement and/or a state child protection agency AND the U.S. Center for SafeSport within 24 hours.

**Reporting contacts:** According to USA Swimming, SafeSport reporting contacts are: U.S. Center for SafeSport phone 833-5US-SAFE (833-587-7233) or online at safesport.org; USA Swimming reporting at usaswimming.org/safe-sport/report-a-concern; anonymous text 888-270-SWIM (888-270-7946).

## Green Flags (Good Coach Indicators)

- Explains the "why" behind training decisions to swimmers and parents
- Gives individualized feedback during practice (not one-size-fits-all)
- Uses positive reinforcement alongside constructive correction
- Maintains professional boundaries (no private one-on-one contact outside structured activities)
- Can articulate a long-term development philosophy beyond "going fast now"
- Tracks individual swimmer progress systematically
- Attends ASCA clinics and professional development

## Red Flags (Warning Signs)

- Dismisses safety concerns from parents
- Creates exclusive relationships with specific athletes
- Blames athletes publicly in front of the team for poor performance
- Discourages parents from watching practice
- Cannot explain training decisions when asked
- Uses punishment (removing water breaks, extra yardage as penalty) routinely
- Makes derogatory comments about swimmers' bodies
- Overemphasizes short-term times at the expense of technique and long-term development

## The Trial Period Strategy

1. **Observe a practice first** -- before your child tries out, watch how coaches interact with swimmers
2. **Do a 4-6 week trial** -- give the relationship enough time to develop
3. **Ask your child regularly** -- "Do you feel heard by your coach? Do they know your name?"
4. **Watch for behavioral changes** -- anxiety before practice, dreading going to the pool
5. **Assess fit at the end of the trial** -- does the training level, philosophy, and environment feel right?

## Common Mistakes

- **Choosing based on fastest swimmers only:** A club that produces fast 10-year-olds may be burning kids out. Look at senior (15-18) retention and development.
- **Ignoring commute:** A 45-minute drive each way for 4-5 practices per week is unsustainable. Proximity matters.
- **Not checking Safe Sport compliance:** Every USA Swimming club should have current Safe Sport certification. If they cannot confirm this, move on.
- **Skipping the trial:** Signing a full-year contract without a trial period locks you in to a potentially poor fit.
