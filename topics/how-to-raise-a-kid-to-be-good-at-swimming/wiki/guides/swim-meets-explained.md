# Swim Meets Explained

## Overview

For Bay Area families, competitive swimming takes place primarily within Pacific Swimming (PC), the local governing body under USA Swimming. This guide explains how meets work, the time standard hierarchy, and what to expect at your child's first competition.

## Pacific Swimming Overview

Pacific Swimming (PC) is one of 59 USA Swimming Local Swimming Committees (LSCs). According to Pacific Swimming, it is the third largest LSC in the country. According to Pacific Swimming, the LSC has 13,000+ registered athletes across 100+ member clubs.

According to Pacific Swimming, the LSC covers the San Francisco Bay Area, coastal Northern California from Monterey to the Oregon border, the Stockton-Modesto area, and the Reno-Carson City-Lake Tahoe area.

According to Pacific Swimming, the LSC has five geographical zones: Zone 1 North, Zone 1 South, Zone 2, Zone 3, and Senior Zone.

| Zone | Coverage |
|------|----------|
| Zone 1 North | According to Pacific Swimming, Zone 1 North covers San Mateo and north Santa Clara counties and has 22 member clubs |
| Zone 1 South | According to Pacific Swimming, Zone 1 South covers south Santa Clara, Santa Cruz, San Benito, and Monterey counties and has 23 member clubs |
| Zone 2 | According to Pacific Swimming, Zone 2 covers Alameda and Contra Costa counties plus the San Joaquin (Stockton/Modesto) area |
| Zone 3 | According to Pacific Swimming, Zone 3 covers San Francisco, Marin, Sonoma, Napa, Mendocino, Lake, Humboldt, Del Norte, and western Solano counties |

## Types of Meets

### Sanctioned Meets
All participants must be USA Swimming members. Times are automatically loaded into the USA Swimming SWIMS database. This is the most common meet type for competitive USA Swimming clubs.

### Approved Meets
Non-USA Swimming members can participate. Times earned by USA Swimming members still qualify for the SWIMS database. Accessible for summer league swimmers who are not year-round members.

### Observed Meets
For competitions under different governing structures (high school, NCAA, Masters). An observer must be a certified USA Swimming official.

## Course Formats

Short course yards (SCY) uses a 25-yard pool and is the primary format for most of the competitive year (fall through spring). According to Pacific Swimming, long course meters (LCM) uses a 50-meter pool, is the primary summer season format, and is the format for international competition and the Olympics.

## Time Standard Hierarchy

### Pacific Swimming Standards

Pacific Swimming time standards, from entry to elite, are: PC-B, PC-A, AGC (Age Group Championship), and Far Western (Q time).

| Standard | Meaning | What It Qualifies For |
|----------|---------|----------------------|
| PC-B | Entry-level developmental | Local developmental meets |
| PC-A | Upper developmental | More competitive local meets |
| AGC | Age Group Championship | Pacific Swimming Age Group Championships meets (March, July, December) |
| Far Western | Fastest age group standard | Far Western Championships (April, July) |

Pacific Swimming Age Group Championships (AGC) meets are held in March, July, and December. Far Western Championships are held in April (Spring) and July (Summer).

Pacific Swimming publishes specific numeric cut times as PDF documents available at pacswim.org/swim-meet-times/standards. These require a PDF reader to access.

### USA Swimming National Standards

SwimLikeAFish.org lists USA Swimming national time standards as: B (entry), BB (intermediate), A (solid), AA (strong), AAA (high-level), AAAA (elite age group).

### High School Swimming Note

According to Pacific Swimming, high school swimmers competing in CIF season must compete as 'unattached' in USA Swimming meets during that season.

## What to Expect at Your First Meet

### What to Bring

- Warm clothing and 3-4 dry towels per swimmer
- Nutritious snacks (meet vendors may lack healthy options)
- Entertainment (books, games) for between events
- Competition suit, team cap, team t-shirts, parka/sweatshirt, racing goggles
- Seating: blankets or folding chairs

### Arrival and Check-In

At most Pacific Swimming meets, deck seeding requires swimmers to check in with the clerk of the course 60 minutes before their event. After signing in, swimmers greet their coach to confirm arrival.

### During Competition

- Races are called "events," ordered and numbered; each event has multiple "heats"
- A Sharpie on the swimmer's arm with event, heat, and lane number is standard practice
- Swimmers listen for "FIRST CALL" for their event number
- Alto Swim Club's meet guide states that swimmers ages 11 and older handle race prep independently, while younger swimmers may have parental verification of heat/lane assignments. This is Alto's specific policy and may differ at other clubs.

### Meet Duration

Alto Swim Club's meet guide notes that dual meets between two teams typically last about 2 hours, while larger invitationals can run much longer (often all-day Saturday events). Early morning start times are very common.

### Parent Guidelines

Alto Swim Club states: 'The coach's tent and pool area are for swimmers only. Parents should only be on deck for timing or officiating.' After a race, swimmers speak with coaches first before approaching parents. Parents provide emotional support; coaches provide technical feedback.

## Common Mistakes

- **Over-coaching from the stands:** Leave technical feedback to the coach. Say "I love watching you swim" and give a hug.
- **Missing check-in deadlines:** If you miss the 60-minute check-in window, your child may be scratched from their events.
- **Not packing enough towels and food:** Meets can last all day. Pack as if you are camping.
- **Obsessing over times at the first meet:** The first meet is about the experience. Times will come.
