# USA Swimming Coach Certification Program

Source: usaswimming.org (official USA Swimming website)

## Core Requirements for All Coaches

All coaches must complete the **Quality Coaching Framework** course. Membership will not be in good standing without this completion.

## Certification Types

**New coaches** to USA Swimming must take the **Core Certification**. Established coaches may choose either Core or one of the **American Development Model (ADM) Certifications**.

## Mandatory Course Structure

The certification program is self-paced and designed as a year-long commitment:
- CPR certification
- Online Safety Training
- In-Water Safety Training
- Welcome to USA Swimming course
- Quality Coaching Framework course

## Background Check Requirements

Non-athlete coaches must pass a background check before coaching. Junior coaches (ages 16-17) are exempt until they turn 18, at which point they have 30 days to complete one.

## Junior Coach Restrictions

Junior coaches (16-17 years old) cannot be designated as head coaches and must have another non-athlete coach present on deck at all times.

## Continuing Education

Certifications do not expire, though the program plans to introduce continuing education components.

# ASCA Certification Levels

Source: American Swimming Coaches Association (ASCA) — swimmingcoach.org

## Overview

ASCA certification is the internationally recognized designation for swimming coaching excellence. Only ASCA members can be certified.

## The Five Required Levels

1. **Level 1:** Foundations of Coaching
2. **Level 2 (Stroke School):** The Construction and Correction of Swimming Strokes
3. **Level 3 (Physiology School):** The Planning and Execution of Training
4. **Level 4 (Leadership School):** Every Coach Must Be A Leader
5. **Level 5 (Administration School):** Running Your Program Effectively

## Level 2 Certification Requirements

Once ASCA members have passed Level 1 and Level 2 courses, and have coached for at least 6 months, they become ASCA Level 2 Certified.

## Professional Significance

By achieving ASCA certification, coaches demonstrate to peers, employers, athletes, and parents that they have gone above and beyond to become a certified professional.

## What These Credentials Mean for Parents

When evaluating a youth swim coach, parents should look for:
- USA Swimming member in good standing (required for sanctioned meets)
- Completed background check (required for all adult non-athlete members)
- Completed Safe Sport / Athlete Protection Training
- ASCA Level 1 or higher certification (indicates formal coaching education)
- CPR/First Aid certification
