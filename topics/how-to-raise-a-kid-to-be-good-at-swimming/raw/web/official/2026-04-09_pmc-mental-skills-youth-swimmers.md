# Mental Training with Youth Sport Teams: Developmental Considerations & Best Practice Recommendations

Source: PMC (National Center for Biotechnology Information) — peer-reviewed sports psychology literature

## Key Findings on Psychological Traits in Competitive Swimming

### Intrinsic Motivation and Self-Determination Theory

Research consistently finds that autonomous motivation is the most adaptive motivational pattern for youth swimmers. This is characterized by having intrinsic interests and personal values as primary reasons for action. Children's ability in swimming is positively associated with intrinsic motivation.

Supporting individuals' psychological needs — autonomy, competence, and relatedness — is fundamental to quality motivation. Children become intrinsically motivated when teachers/coaches employ an autonomy-oriented style that integrates all three basic psychological needs.

### Discipline and Commitment

Competitive swimming requires constant commitment and a high level of self-discipline. Research shows that continuers (swimmers who stay in the sport long-term) focus on enjoyment, social aspects, and non-performance-related reasons for swimming, which leads to smooth transitions from youth swimming into masters swimming.

### Parenting Influence on Motivation

Maternal directiveness is negatively associated with intrinsic motivation. Maternal performance goals showed a positive association with performance orientation but can undermine intrinsic enjoyment. Parents play a significant role in shaping children's motivation for and affinity with swimming.

### Psychological Skills Training (PST) for Youth Swimmers

Research by Jennifer Meggs and Mark A. Chen (2019) in Perceptual and Motor Skills assessed the competitive performance effects of PST:
- Self-talk and goal setting methods showed measurable effects on youth swimmer performance
- A 5-week intervention program produced significant results
- Participants demonstrated significant improvements in mental toughness measures, particularly in self-confidence and attention control
- Post-intervention, swimmers exhibited significant increase in perceptions of success, correlating with improved competitive swimming times

### Developmental Stages for Mental Skills

At mid-adolescence (ages 15-17), athletes are:
- More physically mature
- More capable of expressing feelings
- Able to think more abstractly
- More able to demonstrate greater mastery of their sport

### The MDPI Study on Motivational Level and Dropout Risk

A 2025 study in Sports (journal) on "The Motivational Level of Performance Swimmers and Its Impact on the Risk of Sports Dropout" found:
- Amotivation (lack of motivation) is the strongest predictor of dropout
- Intrinsic motivation (fun, mastery) predicts long-term participation
- Swimmers with high intrinsic motivation have lower dropout risk

## Key Psychological Traits That Help Kids Thrive in Competitive Swimming

Based on the composite research literature:

1. **Intrinsic motivation** — loving the sport for its own sake, not external rewards
2. **Comfort with repetition** — swimming requires the same drills thousands of times
3. **Discipline and work ethic** — showing up even when tired; persistence research shows this trait rarely develops if not established young
4. **Emotional resilience** — ability to handle disappointment, slow progress, early defeats
5. **Goal orientation** — mastery-focused goals (personal improvement) predict better long-term outcomes than performance-only goals (beating others)
6. **Coachability** — willingness to receive and act on correction
7. **Social belonging** — team connection and friendships within the sport sustain participation
8. **Autonomy** — swimmers who feel some control over their training show higher engagement
