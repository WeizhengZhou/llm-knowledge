## Section 1: Research Findings — Adolescent Swimmer Diets (PMC5035546)

### Study Design
- N = 191 US adolescents (91 swimmers, 100 non-athletes), ages 13–14
- Method: Food frequency questionnaires + calcium checklists
- Publication: International Journal of Sport Nutrition and Exercise Metabolism (2016)

### Key Nutrient Intake Levels Found

**Macronutrients (as % of total calories):**
| Nutrient | Swimmers Found | Recommended Range |
|----------|---------------|-------------------|
| Total fat | ~36% of calories | 25–35% |
| Saturated fat | ~12% of calories | <10% |
| Carbohydrate | ~52% of calories | 45–65% |
| Protein | 13–14% of calories | 10–30% |

**Micronutrients (daily averages):**
| Nutrient | Swimmers | Non-Athletes | RDA |
|----------|----------|--------------|-----|
| Calcium | 852–1197 mg | Similar | 1300 mg |
| Vitamin D | 4–7 μg | Similar | 10 μg EAR |
| Iron | 14–20 mg | Similar | 8–15 mg (age/sex) |
| Zinc | 9–13 mg | Similar | 9–11 mg |

### Key Deficiencies Found

- Only 10–30% of swimmers achieved adequate calcium intake
- Only 0–7% of swimmers achieved adequate vitamin D levels
- Inadequate daily servings of fruits, vegetables, grains, and dairy products in both groups

### Surprising Finding: Swimmers and Non-Athletes Had Similar Diets
"Swimmers and non-athletes had broadly similar diets." Female swimmers consumed more fruit and dairy servings than female non-athletes, but macronutrient patterns were essentially identical between groups — suggesting many youth swimmers are not eating to optimize athletic performance.

### Clinical Implications
"Dietary patterns may jeopardize optimal athletic performance and place them at risk for future chronic diseases, including osteoporosis." Note: Swimming is a non-weight-bearing sport, which does not stimulate bone density — making adequate calcium and vitamin D especially critical for young swimmers.

## Section 2: Evidence-Based Nutrition Recommendations for Youth Swimmers

### Macronutrient Targets (Practitioner Guidance)

**Carbohydrates — Primary Fuel Source:**
- Target: ~4.4 g/kg body weight per day (about 46% of total calories)
- Higher carbohydrate needs during heavy training weeks and meet weeks
- Best sources: whole grains, fruits, vegetables, rice, pasta, oats
- Function: Glycogen replenishment; primary energy substrate for swimming's anaerobic-aerobic demands

**Protein — Recovery and Growth:**
- Target: 1.2–2.1 g/kg body weight per day
- Pediatric recommendation (Nemours): 0.45–0.6 g/lb (= ~1.0–1.3 g/kg) for young athletes — higher than the adult recommendation for non-athletes
- Best sources: lean meat, fish, eggs, dairy, beans, nuts, Greek yogurt
- Timing: Consume within 30 minutes post-practice to maximize muscle repair

**Healthy Fats — Hormones and Cell Function:**
- Target: ~42% of total calories (some studies suggest slightly lower for youth)
- Sources: avocados, nuts, seeds, olive oil, fatty fish (omega-3)
- Function: supports hormone production, cell membrane integrity, anti-inflammatory recovery

### Micronutrients of Particular Concern for Swimmers

| Nutrient | Why Critical | Good Sources |
|----------|-------------|-------------|
| Calcium | Bone development; swimming non-weight-bearing | Dairy, fortified plant milk, broccoli, sardines |
| Vitamin D | Calcium absorption; immune function | Sunlight, fortified milk, fatty fish, eggs |
| Iron | Oxygen transport; endurance | Red meat, spinach, legumes, fortified cereals |

## Section 3: Meal Timing for Young Swimmers

### Before Practice / Competition

**3–4 hours before:** Full balanced meal — complex carbohydrates + lean protein (e.g., pasta with chicken, rice and fish, peanut butter on whole wheat toast)

**1–2 hours before:** Light snack — easily digestible carbohydrates (e.g., granola bar, banana, yogurt). Avoid high-fat, high-fiber foods that delay gastric emptying.

**30–60 minutes before (if needed):** Small, simple carbohydrate snack only (e.g., half a banana, crackers).

### During Long Meets

- Events extending >75 minutes: Simple carbohydrates may help maintain energy (sports drink, banana, crackers, gummy chews)
- Hydration: 8–16 oz water every 15 minutes during activity
- Pre-event: 8–16 oz water 2–4 hours before; 8 oz during warmup

### After Practice (Recovery Window)

- Critical window: within 30 minutes post-practice
- Best recovery snack: Low-fat chocolate milk (carbs + protein + calcium + potassium combination)
- Full recovery meal: within 2 hours of finishing practice
- Components: protein source + carbohydrate source + fluid replacement

## Section 4: Hydration Guidelines for Youth Swimmers

A common mistake: young swimmers often underestimate fluid needs because they are surrounded by water and don't perceive sweating.

**General daily hydration:**
- Rule of thumb: minimum half body weight in ounces per day (e.g., 80 lb child → 40 oz minimum)

**Exercise hydration protocol (Nemours Children's Health):**
| Timing | Amount |
|--------|--------|
| 2–4 hours before exercise | 8–16 oz water |
| During warm-up | 8 oz water |
| During exercise | 8–16 oz every 15 minutes |
| Post-exercise | Continue ½ cup (4 oz) every 15 minutes until urine is pale yellow |

**When sports drinks are appropriate:** Only recommended for exercise lasting >75 minutes or in extreme heat, due to added sugar content. Plain water is preferable for shorter practices.

## Section 5: Age-Specific Considerations

### Young Beginners (Ages 6–10)

- Calorie needs are generally met through normal healthy eating if portion sizes are adequate
- Focus on: calcium, vitamin D, and iron (common deficits in this age group)
- Avoid adult-targeted sports supplements entirely
- Meals should be child-friendly — nutrient-dense but not restrictive

### Adolescent Competitive Swimmers (Ages 11–17)

- Energy demands increase significantly with training volume (some programs 10–20+ hours/week)
- Relative Energy Deficiency in Sport (RED-S) is a real risk for female swimmers — often driven by inadvertent undereating during growth phases
- Female swimmers: monitor for low calcium intake carefully (bone density window)
- Growth spurts may temporarily affect performance and appetite — normalize this for young athletes

### Common Mistakes at Meets

- Eating too close to events (heavy meal within 2 hours → nausea, cramping)
- Skipping meals between events out of nerves
- Relying on vending machine snacks (chips, candy) with poor glycogen recovery value
- Not drinking water throughout the day before a meet

## Section 6: Foods to Emphasize and Limit

### Emphasize
- Whole grains (oatmeal, whole wheat, brown rice, quinoa)
- Lean proteins (chicken, turkey, fish, eggs, Greek yogurt, beans)
- Fruits and vegetables (broad variety; high in micronutrients and antioxidants)
- Dairy or fortified plant-based alternatives (calcium, vitamin D)
- Healthy fats (nuts, avocado, olive oil)

### Limit (not eliminate)
- High saturated fat foods (fast food, fried items) — slow digestion, interfere with recovery
- High-sugar drinks (soda, juice, sports drinks outside exercise)
- Processed snack foods with low nutritional density
