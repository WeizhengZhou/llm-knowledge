# Pacific Swimming LSC — FAQ and Meet Structure

Source: pacswim.org/resources/faq + pacswim.org/bay-area-swim-meets/types (official Pacific Swimming website)

---

## Organization Overview

Pacific Swimming (PC) is one of 59 USA Swimming Local Swimming Committees (LSCs). It is the third largest LSC in the country.

**Geographic coverage:**
- San Francisco Bay Area (core)
- Coastal Northern California from Monterey to Oregon border
- Stockton-Modesto area (Central Valley)
- Reno-Carson City-Lake Tahoe area (Nevada border)

**Size:** 13,000+ registered athletes across 100+ member clubs

**Zones:** Five geographical zones (Zone 1 North, Zone 1 South, Zone 2, Zone 3, Senior Zone)

---

## Types of Pacific Swimming Meets

### 1. Sanctioned Meets
- All participants must be USA Swimming members
- Operate under full USA Swimming technical and administrative rules
- All times automatically loaded into the USA Swimming SWIMS database
- **Most common meet type for competitive USA Swimming clubs**

### 2. Approved Meets
- Non-USA Swimming members can participate
- Follows USA Swimming technical rules
- Times earned by USA Swimming members qualify for SWIMS database
- Accessible for summer league swimmers who are not year-round USA Swimming members

### 3. Observed Meets
- For competitions under different governing structures (high school, NCAA, Masters)
- Observer must be certified USA Swimming official
- Member times can be recorded in SWIMS database
- Request must be made 10+ days prior to event

## Age Groups and Time Standards in Pacific Swimming

**Age groups:**
- 8 & Under
- 10 & Under
- 11-12
- 13-14
- 15-16
- 17-18

**Pacific Swimming Time Standards (fastest to slowest):**
1. **Far Western (Q time)** — fastest age group standard; qualifies for Far Western Championships
2. **Age Group Championships (AGC)** — qualifies for PC's March, July, December championship meets
3. **PC-A** — upper developmental standard
4. **PC-B** — entry-level developmental standard

**Senior standards (age-independent):**
- Senior Open → Senior 2 → Senior Circuit → Sectionals → Pro Swim Series → Junior Nationals → National Championships

## The Competitive Pathway for Bay Area Kids

**Typical progression:**

| Stage | Meet Type | Notes |
|-------|-----------|-------|
| 1 | Dual meets / Local invitationals | Open to all club members; no qualifying time needed |
| 2 | Approved/Sanctioned invitationals | Entry-level qualifying (PC-B) may apply |
| 3 | Age Group Championships (AGC) | Requires AGC qualifying time; held March, July, December |
| 4 | Far Western Championships | Fastest PC age group standard; held April and July |
| 5 | Zone Age Group Championships | Western Zone; 14-and-under focus |
| 6 | National-level meets | USA Swimming Futures, Junior Nationals, National Championships |

## Meet Formats

**Timed Finals:** Awards places based on heat times alone (simpler format, common for smaller meets and age groups)

**Trials & Finals:** Morning qualifying rounds determine afternoon finalists (more formal; used at championships)

**Deck-seeding:** Most PC meets require swimmers to check in with the clerk of the course 60 minutes before their event

---

## Short Course vs. Long Course

- **Short course yards (SCY):** 25-yard pool — used for most of the competitive year (fall through spring)
- **Long course meters (LCM):** 50-meter pool — used during summer season; primary format for international competition and Olympics

**Course conversion:** Pacific allows swimmers to maintain their qualifying class across courses without mandatory time conversion.

**Significance for youth:** Young swimmers first learn to compete in short course yards. Long course season (summer) is a separate skill development — 50-meter racing is considered harder and exposes stroke efficiency weaknesses.

---

## High School Swimming Note

High school swimmers competing in CIF (California Interscholastic Federation) season must compete as "unattached" in USA Swimming meets during that season — meaning their times cannot represent their club.
