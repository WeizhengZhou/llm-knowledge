# USA Swimming Age Groups and Competitive Levels Explained

Source: swimlikeafish.org

## Competition Pathway Overview

USA Swimming structures age group competition as a pyramid, progressing from introductory meets to elite-level championships. Competition is described as "like performing" — providing swimmers with pressurized environments to demonstrate their skills.

## Age Groups

USA Swimming organizes competition into these official age groups:
- 8 & Under
- 10 & Under
- 11-12
- 13-14
- 15-16
- 17-18

## Time Standards (Motivational Times)

USA Swimming uses time standards as benchmarks:
- **B**: Entry-level competitive standard
- **BB**: Intermediate standard
- **A**: Solid competitive level
- **AA**: Strong competitive level
- **AAA**: High-level competitive standard
- **AAAA**: Elite age group standard

These motivational times help age group swimmers track progress as they age up.

## Competitive Levels (Bottom to Top)

### 1. Non-Qualification Meets
Entry-level competitions open to any registered USA Swimming member. These typically occur locally and serve as first competition experiences for new swimmers.

### 2. Local Qualification Meets
Swimmers must achieve designated time standards to participate. These meets are organized by Local Swimming Committees (LSCs) — 59 committees nationwide managing competitions across geographical regions.

### 3. Age Group Championships
Held twice yearly (short course yards and long course meters seasons), typically around March and August. Structure varies by LSC; some states call these "State Championships." Features preliminaries and finals formats.

### 4. Age Group Zones
Four zones divide the country: Eastern, Central, Southern, and Western. Teams comprise up to 48 men and 48 women selected through varying processes (time cuts, power points, or combination methods). Most zone meets cap at 14 and under.

### 5. Senior/National Level
Beyond age group zones, elite swimmers progress to:
- Sectional Championships
- Junior Nationals (17-18 or selected 15-16 swimmers)
- National Championships / US Open

## College Recruitment Swimming Times

- Competitive D2 programs recruit based on Speedo Sectionals time standards
- No minimum time guarantees a scholarship
- D1 Power 5 programs typically recruit among the nation's top-ranked swimmers
- DI Mid-major and D3 programs are more accessible

## The Bay Area (Pacific Swimming LSC) Progression

Within Pacific Swimming (PC), age group swimmers progress:
1. Dual meets and local invitationals
2. PC Age Group Championships (AGC) meets (March, July, December)
3. Far Western Championships (April and July) — fastest PC age group standard
4. Zone Age Group Championships (Western Zone)
5. National meets (Futures, Junior Nationals, Nationals)

## Key Concept: Training Groups vs. Age Groups

Competitive clubs organize swimmers into training groups by ability level, not just age. A 10-year-old might train in the same group as a 13-year-old if their skill levels align. Club-specific group names vary (e.g., Alto Swim Club uses "Discovery," "Imagination," "Age Group Bronze/Silver/Gold" designations).

## Alto Swim Club Example (Bay Area)

Alto Swim Club's age group program structure:
- **Ages 8 & Under:** Discovery 1, Imagination 1, Age Group 1, Age Group Bronze (3-6 practices/week, 1-2 hours)
- **Ages 9-10:** Discovery 2, Imagination 2, Age Group 2, Age Group Silver
- **Ages 11-12:** Discovery 3, Imagination 3, Age Group 3, Age Group Gold (up to 6x/week, 2-2.5 hours — targets national AA, AAA, AAAA, Far Western qualifying times)
