# Determinants and Reasons for Dropout in Swimming - Systematic Review (PMC)

## Study Overview

The review analyzed 6 studies (1982-2008) involving 727 participants aged 5-30, predominantly from Canada (4 studies), USA (1), and Spain (1). Most participants were female (65.74%).

## Primary Dropout Reasons

The review identified controllable reasons as the main causes:

- **"had other things to do"** - most common across all age groups and genders
- Conflicts with trainers/coaches
- Failure in competence improvements
- Pressure from parents, peers, or coaches
- **"lack of enjoyment"** and boredom
- Interest in other activities
- Lack of swimming peers
- Limited one-on-one coaching

## Age-Related Differences

The 15-18 age group valued reasons like "no team work," parental/peer pressure, and insufficient challenge more than the 10-14 group. Overall dropout concerns peak at ages 13-18.

## Gender Patterns

Females more frequently cited "did not like the pressure" compared to males across studies.

## Critical Finding

Most dropout reasons were **controllable by athletes**, suggesting intervention potential through environmental modifications and motivation enhancement.

## Theoretical Framework

Only one longitudinal study used Self-Determination Theory, finding amotivation positively predicted dropout—indicating motivation as a key dropout predictor.

## Additional Findings from Search (Broader Literature)

From the Frontiers journal on burnout and dropout in youth sports:
- Psychological issues originating from athletes' personal lives and social relationships are the most influential factors
- No evidence indicated that early specialization was a direct predictor for burnout and dropout
- Sport specialization often requires increased training hours and may predispose young athletes to social isolation, poor academic performance, increased anxiety, greater stress, inadequate sleep, decreased family time, and burnout
- Resilience in sports may prevent burnout and the dropout rate
- Quality of the coaching climate is an important predictor of developmental benefits

## Prevention Strategies

- Address coaching quality (the primary controllable factor)
- Maintain athletes' autonomy and social connections
- Create a fun, learning-focused environment
- Youth sports programs should stimulate athletes to focus on learning new skills and developing abilities
- Allow participation at both competitive and recreational levels
