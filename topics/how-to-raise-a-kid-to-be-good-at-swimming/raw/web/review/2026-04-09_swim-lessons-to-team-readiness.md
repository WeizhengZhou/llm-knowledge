# Readiness Indicators: Moving from Swim Lessons to Swim Team

## Source: Synthesis from WeAquatics, Texas Swim Academy, Sunsational, and Menlo Swim articles

## Key Readiness Indicators

### Physical / Technical Skills

- Strong, sustained flutter kick on both back and stomach with intermittent paddling and breathing
- Can execute strong lengths of freestyle, backstroke, and breaststroke independently
- Basic butterfly understanding preferred
- Endurance: can swim from wall to wall (25 yards) without stopping
- 50 meters without difficulty before competitive readiness
- Body position, kick efficiency, and breathing coordination—not just completion of strokes

### Attention and Focus

- Team practices are 60 minutes vs. 30-minute LTS lessons; can the child sustain attention?
- Can follow multi-step directions from a coach (not just one-on-one)
- Can count their own laps and stay focused without constant supervision
- Experience following directions from authority figures (school, preschool experience helps)

### Emotional / Social Readiness

- Comfortable in the water without instructor in the pool
- Can handle coach feedback without emotional shutdown
- Willing to try new skills even when they might fail (e.g., flip turns, racing dives)
- Sets personal goals ("I want to learn butterfly," "I want to swim faster")
- Interested in racing and competition—not just swimming for fun
- Can function as a team member, not just individual

### Safety Habits

- Pool rules are automatic behaviors, not reminders
- Enters the water correctly every time
- Can swim responsibly with reduced supervision

### Motivation Signals

- Watches swimming videos voluntarily
- Asks about swim meets and times
- Expresses desire to join a team

## Timeline

- Some swimmers are ready after six months of intentional advanced swim lessons
- Others need a year or more
- Age 5-7 is typical for first team readiness (varies widely by child)
- Instructor assessment is more reliable than age alone

## Red Flag: Not Ready Signs

- Struggles to complete one length independently
- Cries or shuts down when corrected
- Needs constant one-on-one attention
- No interest in competition
- Can't maintain attention for 30+ minutes

## Recommended Steps

1. Ask the child's LTS instructor for a readiness assessment
2. Attend a team practice as an observer
3. Try a short trial period before full commitment
