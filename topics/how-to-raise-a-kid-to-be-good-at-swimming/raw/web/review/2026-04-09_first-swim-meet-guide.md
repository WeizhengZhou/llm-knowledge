# First Swim Meet Guide — Alto Swim Club

Source: altoswimclub.com/swim-meet-101 (official Bay Area club — L1 official)

## What to Bring

Essential items:
- Warm clothing and 3-4 dry towels per swimmer
- Nutritious snacks (meet vendors may lack healthy options)
- Entertainment (books, games) for between events
- Seating: blankets or folding chairs

Required team gear:
- Competition suit
- Team cap
- Team t-shirts
- Parka/sweatshirt
- Racing goggles

## Arrival and Check-In

- Swimmers must arrive by the announced check-in deadline
- Process: complete swimmer check-in by circling and initialing the heat sheet, then sign in for each event
- After signing in, swimmers greet their coach to confirm arrival and check-in status
- Team tents: "Our team will typically have 4+ tents at each meet and swimmers sit together under our team tents" while parents establish separate areas

## During Warm-Up

Team warm-ups typically begin shortly after check-in. Punctuality is mandatory. Warm-up at younger ages is "a very loose term, designed to develop good habits and get some butterflies out."

## During Competition

- Swimmers monitor the meet schedule and upcoming event numbers
- Swimmers ages 11 and older handle race prep independently; younger swimmers may have parental verification of heat/lane assignments only
- Swimmers check in with coaches before and after races for feedback and to report their assignments
- Swimmers check out before leaving each session

## Parent Guidelines (Critical)

The club emphasizes developing "independent athletes":
- **"The coach's tent and pool area are for swimmers only. Parents should only be on deck for timing or officiating."**
- Parents should remain "always supportive and positive"
- Coaches provide all swimming instruction — parents do not coach on the pool deck
- Post-race, swimmers speak with coaches first before approaching parents
- Parents establish separate areas from team tents

## Event Format

From general first-swim-meet guidance (Gold Medal Swim School, Pool School Swim):
- Races are called "events," ordered and numbered
- Each event has multiple "heats" (races) within it
- Heat sheets list events, heats, and lanes
- A **SHARPIE** on the swimmer's arm with their event, heat, and lane number is standard practice
- Swimmers must listen for "FIRST CALL" for their event number

## Meet Duration

- Dual meets between two teams typically last about 2 hours
- Larger invitationals can run much longer (often all-day Saturday events)
- Early morning start times are very common — pack the car the night before

## What to Expect Emotionally

- First meets are exciting but overwhelming for many kids
- Coaches manage pre-race anxiety — parents' role is to stay calm and positive
- When things go wrong (missed event, disqualification): parents defer to coach to handle
- After races: a hug and "I'm proud of you" is sufficient; avoid technical critique

## Team Environment

- Swimmers are expected to cheer for teammates
- Sitting under the team tent between events builds team community
- Meet culture teaches focus, routine, and preparation skills beyond the swim itself
